# Win Brazil through teachers, YouTubers, fabrics and carts

No quilt design product found does what your business model sets out to do, and adding YouTubers widens that lead rather than closing it. No rival pays teachers a share of their students' subscriptions. No rival pays creators a share of the subscriptions they bring. None shows a single Brazilian fabric brand, and none fills a shop's cart across several brands. The market leader, Electric Quilt, pays quilting YouTubers per sponsored video (two Chatterbox Quilts videos in late 2025) and hands out 25%-off partner codes. It does not share subscription income ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/chatterboxquilts), [Electric Quilt](https://electricquilt.com/landing-pages/annies519/)). The closest revenue-share precedents come from creative learning. Skillshare pays teachers **60% of referred members' net payments**, and Domestika pays affiliates **40% of the first purchase and 20% of later ones** ([Skillshare](https://help.skillshare.com/hc/articles/4415798485389), [Domestika](https://domestika.org/affiliates)). Brazil's biggest patchwork channels are Arte de Paninho (about 845K YouTube subscribers by her own bio), Ana Cosentino (320K) and Natália Engel (about 229K). Almost all of them already sell Hotmart courses and many run fabric shops. That makes them natural partners for a "course pack plus cart in your own shop" offer, more than for a bare affiliate link. The recommended deal follows Nuvemshop's Brazilian two-tier precedent: teachers get about 25% for as long as the student pays, and creators get about 20% for 12 months, plus a free Pro account and a viewer trial. A teacher's class link always wins a tie. Under CONAR's 2026 guide, every revenue-share post is advertising, so disclosure has to be written into the contract. Accounting sources say influencers cannot register as MEI. The app's other strengths remain: **300+ blocks**, block drawing with automatic cutting lists, three languages and the only 3D bed drape. The gaps that block the business are unchanged: no manufacturer fabrics yet, and no live login or payments. The economics set the roles. One dedicated video on a channel averaging about 29,000 views might bring 6 to 45 subscribers, so creators bring reach and teachers bring retention. All research came from search-result text. Audience counts are tracker estimates, and the tax and legal points need an accountant and a lawyer.

## How far to trust this: search snippets, tracker estimates, not opened pages

This report combines ten research notes. Five come from the first competitor study (desktop, web, mobile, Brazil, wider market). Three come from the atelier study (competitor fabrics and usability, fabric brands and carts, the teacher channel and pricing). Two are new, on the creator channel: a list of creators, and the partnership model. In every case the tool that opens web pages was blocked, so each finding is a line of text a search engine showed, with the address it came from. That has six consequences.

First, **prices, fees and ratings are snapshots**, and many come from third-party blogs rather than the provider's own price page. Asaas, Iugu and Kiwify fees, for example, come from comparison sites ([quasa.io](https://quasa.io/pt/media/asaas-ou-iugu-tarifa-publicada-vence-ate-o-volume-pedir-proposta), [Mobills](https://www.mobills.com.br/blog/ganhar-dinheiro/kiwify-afiliados/)). Second, **audience counts are estimates**. They come from trackers (vidIQ, NoxInfluencer, PLAYBOARD, SocialCounts, Feedspot, SponsorRadar) or from Instagram profile snapshots in the search index, which carry no date. Arte de Paninho's YouTube figure is what her own Instagram bio says, not a tracker count. Social Blade could not be opened. Third, **affiliate benchmarks mostly come from companies that sell affiliate software** (Rewardful, Refgrow, Track360, Cello), which have a reason to make programmes look good. Fourth, **CONAR's 2026 guide was read only through law-firm and trade-press summaries**, because conar.org.br could not be reached. Fifth, **"not found" means the snippets did not mention it**, not that it does not exist. Low-quality ranking pages (wifitalents, zipdo, gitnux) are used only where nothing better existed, and are labelled. Sixth, nothing was tried hands-on, so no claim about ease of use rests on testing.

The facts about your app come from your corrected brief. The app now has **300+ blocks** (goal 1,000+ at launch, then parity with the leaders). The **"Desenhar um bloco" dialog** has Cells and Grid-paper (Papel quadriculado) modes, grid sizes such as 4×4, seam-line, fill and erase tools, points every ¼ square, a fabric palette, symmetry, undo, saving to "My blocks", "use in quilt", a repeat preview, and an **automatic cutting list with seam allowance** (for example "8 × 4 in squares, cut once on the diagonal"). Rulers show inches ("pol") as well as metric, and the screens are in **Portuguese, Spanish and English**. These are still true from before: the 3D drape on a bed or table with cloth physics, symmetry, fussy cutting, a quilting-stitch preview, a cutting plan for 110/140/150 cm fabric widths, real-size A4 PDF templates, an app that works offline in the browser, a sync server that is built but not live, and no real login. The business model is a paid subscription with two partner channels. Patchwork ateliers get it free for teachers, and part of each student's payment goes to the teacher. YouTubers and other creators get part of each subscription they bring. The main objectives are showing all the main international and Brazilian fabric brands and filling shop carts automatically, with ease of use as the first focus.

## About 70 products, a dozen direct rivals, and none built for teachers, creators or Brazilian fabric

The field splits into six groups. Desktop is one serious product (EQ8) plus programs tied to machine brands, frozen or dead. On the web, a crowded set of mostly young freemium tools has appeared since 2025, and it now includes a retailer's designer with a cart (eQuilter) and a marketplace (Quiltster). Mobile is small and mostly on iPad. Utilities are calculators and stash apps. Longarm and embroidery software sits alongside a piecing designer rather than replacing it. Brazil has essentially nothing local. Products in **bold** appear in the feature matrices.

### Utilities, adjacent tools and the Brazilian scene

Quilters actually spend their app time in utilities. The Missouri Star app has about 34,000 iOS ratings at 4.9, far more than any design app ([appfollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/missouri-star-quilt-company/513781546?country=us)). Robert Kaufman's QuiltingCalc draws complaints that it lacks metric yardage ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quiltingcalc/id390366459)). There are low-waste cutting apps such as QuiltYield ([appshunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/quiltyield-fabric-calculator/id6790127815)), stash inventories such as Fabricora ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/fabricora/6753884696)), and colour matchers such as Quilty Solid, which matches photos to Art Gallery and Kona solids ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quilty-solid/id6444816769)). Longarm and stitch software costs US$400–1,000 for design programs and US$6,500–15,500 for robot systems ([Sewing Machines Plus](https://sewingmachinesplus.com/products/hq-20807-art-n-stitch-software), [Quilted Joy](https://quiltedjoy.com/blogs/blog/quilt-path-vs-intelliquilter-for-apqs-machines-complete-comparison-guide)). The only "fabric on a bed" visualisers are business tools such as Style3D, not tools for quilters ([Style3D](https://www.style3d.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/15927)).

In Portuguese and Spanish, the field is close to empty. No quilt design program made in Brazil or Latin America was found. ## What only you do, and where you have caught up

**The physics-based 3D drape is still the one feature nobody else has.** Searches found no quilt tool with cloth simulation. PreQuilt and Quiltler show flat layouts, and the nearest rival idea is Swatchit's six AI scenes, which include a bedroom ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/swatchit-quilt-color-design/id6760553382?uo=2)). A generated picture cannot show whether the quilt covers the mattress or how the border hangs. A Quiltingboard user asking for a "quilt visualizer" got only "use EQ" in reply ([Quiltingboard](https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/quilt-visualizer-t311061.html)). In this business model the drape has two more jobs. Once it shows *real* fabrics from a brand or shop, it becomes the best "see it before you buy" picture in the market, which is what shops and manufacturers want. It is also the most filmable thing in the app: a quilt dropping onto a bed is the kind of moment that makes a YouTube thumbnail or an Instagram reel, which matters for the creator channel.

**Drawing your own block with an automatic cutting list puts you level with EQ8, and makes it easier.** EQ8's EasyDraw needs every line connected to other lines and to the block outline. EQ's own advice is to spend "a lot of time experimenting" first, and its support pages explain why drawings fail ([EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/articles/understanding-easydraw-in-eq8)). A blogger says drawing your own EQ8 blocks "will take time and patience" ([UKQU](https://ukqu.co.uk/?p=302845)). EQ8's cutting chart also leaves out shapes it cannot rotary-cut, such as 30° and 90° kites, which must be printed as templates ([EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=3773)). Your Cells mode (colour squares and halves) and Grid-paper mode (draw on dots every ¼ square) copy the way Brazilian beginners are already told to plan, "on graph paper first, then work out the cuts" ([Decor Fácil](https://www.decorfacil.com/como-fazer-patchwork)). The honest pitch is "EQ8's drawing power without EQ8's rules", and it should be backed by a quick test with real students.

**Three languages are a clear lead.** Only QuiltFit, a small iPad yardage planner, has Portuguese and Spanish screens ([App Store PL](https://apps.apple.com/pl/app/id6776541705)). mySewnet translates web pages and part of one module ([mySewnet es-MX](https://mysewnet.com/es-mx/Quilting/Quilting%20Software)). No full block designer with drawing, cutting lists and templates was found in either language. Third-party reviews of PreQuilt in Portuguese, Spanish, Italian, Korean and Arabic show the demand is there, and that foreign sites already chase "programa para patchwork" searches ([Omid Saffari ES](https://omidsaffari.com/es/blog/programa-de-diseno-de-patchwork-prequilt)). Languages also decide which creators can promote the app at all: Brazilian, Spanish-language and English channels each need the app in their own language on screen.

**Metric plus inches fixes a real complaint.** Every rival found works in inches. NiftyFifty rounds to ⅛ inch, and a UK quilter reports that EQ8's metric mode just relabels a 9-inch block as "9 cm" ([UK Quilters United](https://ukqu.co.uk/?p=312819)). Brazil uses both systems: rulers are sold as 6 × 24 in and as 10 × 45 cm ([Ponto Frio](https://www.pontofrio.com.br/regua-patchwork-6x24-polegadas/p/1541011129), [Ponto Frio](https://www.pontofrio.com.br/regua-patchwork-10x45cm-cristal/p/1541010619)), and local tricoline is 1.40–1.50 m wide ([Magalu](https://m.magazineluiza.com.br/busca/tecido+tricoline+para+patchwork/)). Supporting both, with a 140 or 150 cm width, fits how Brazilians actually cut and buy.

**Symmetry, fussy cutting, the stitch preview and full offline use remain uncommon.** No web or mobile tool was found advertising symmetry editing, fussy cutting or a quilting-stitch preview. PreQuilt's mirror blocks are the nearest thing ([Stitched in Color](https://stitchedincolor.com/blog/2024/7/22/5-tips-to-get-started-with-prequilt)). Working offline is shared with QuiltFit and TideStitch, which lead with "no login" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/bt/app/id6776541705)), so offline is not unique. It is valuable in classrooms with weak wifi.

## Fabric library, login and content depth are the gaps that block the business

**The fabric library is both your main objective and your biggest gap.** EQ8 sets the bar: 6,000–6,200 labelled manufacturer fabrics, plus about 3,500 new printed fabrics a year in packs of 1,100–1,250, plus Moda's free monthly collection ([EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=2614), [EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=1403), [Moda](https://my.modafabrics.com/node/31813)). Only Quiltster also keeps up with new collections, through manufacturer files sent by SKU ([Quiltster](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=10650)). The newer apps avoid the licensing work by using the user's own photos or solid-colour names. Matching EQ8's count at launch is unrealistic. A realistic plan follows Quiltster's approach: curated current collections from partners, plus solid colour cards from several brands, plus Brazilian mills nobody else has. That is enough to be unique.

**Your own-fabric photos are still hidden.** Until a manufacturer library exists, photos are the only way to design with Brazilian fabric, and eight or more rivals treat them as basic ([QuiltWall](https://humannpc.pages.dev/quiltwall/)). The "+ Add fabric" tile already proposed in the project notes is the cheapest bridge to the fabric objective.

**No real login, payments or live sync means no subscription and no partner credit.** The model makes this the first priority, not one task among many. A teacher's share depends on knowing which student belongs to which teacher, and a creator's share depends on recording which code or link a new subscriber came through. Both need accounts. A subscription needs a payment provider tied to those accounts. The published claude.ai page cannot sync until the API runs on https on the internet. That matters doubly for creators: a viewer who clicks a link in a video description must land on a page that can sign her up and pay.

**Content depth still trails.** 300+ blocks beats PreQuilt (100+), QuiltWall (200+) and EQ Mini (300), but not NiftyFifty (1,200–1,400+, free) or EQ8 (6,700+). Your 1,000+ launch goal would put you in NiftyFifty's range. Appliqué and foundation paper piecing are still missing. Paper piecers design in Illustrator, Inkscape or Procreate today ([r/FPP](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/FPP/comments/1ibf4as/what_program_or_approach_do_you_use_to_create)), and NiftyFifty gives them 196 free patterns. For a paid app, a free rival with more blocks is a pricing problem, not just a features problem.

Fabric fits these systems because shops sell it in **whole units**: a half-yard, a quarter-yard, a 50 cm cut or a fat quarter. Shopify shops usually make the half-yard the unit, so quantity 3 means 1.5 yd ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-can-i-sell-fabric-in-fractional-quantities-like-1-2-or-1-4-yard/280316), [Little Fabric Shop](https://littlefabricshop.com/pages/cutting-info)). Your app must therefore know each shop's unit and round up. For example, 1.3 m of one fabric is 3 units at a 50 cm shop, and 2.84 → 3 units at a half-yard shop. The app should show the extra so nobody feels cheated. Variant IDs change, so they have to be refreshed. Many Shopify shops publish them at `/products.json`, but some block it ([Recharge](https://support.getrecharge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009746154)), and reading it without permission is weak ground for a paid app. Partner feeds are safer.

### Precedents and what affiliate income is worth

Quiltster and eQuilter prove that people will design with real fabrics and then buy. Quiltster lets the user plan a quilt and order the finished plan as a kit. Partners fulfil each order, listings show "Sold by", and one order can include several partners ([Quiltster](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=9996)). Its manufacturer submission rules (72 dpi JPG or PNG, one file per SKU, release dates) are a ready-made template for asking mills for files ([Quiltster](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=10650)). eQuilter works only in its own shop, on its own patterns. Nobody fills carts at *several* independent shops, and nobody covers Brazil.

### Licensing: solids are easy, prints need written permission

Fabric prints are copyrighted artwork, often licensed in from designers. Electric Quilt tells users to "contact the fabric manufacturer" about permission ([EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=1190)), and a blogger notes that manufacturer images in EQ8 are for personal use only ([Meadow Mist Designs](https://meadowmistdesigns.blogspot.com/2020/02/eq8-importing-fabric-series-post-2.html)). No public licence for software was found. How EQ and PreQuilt got their rights is not public either. What can be seen is an industry that hands out images because it wants sales: Moda's JPG zips, Robert Kaufman's mock-up programme, EQ8's SKU-labelled library and PreQuilt's partnership with Suzy Quilts ([Suzy Quilts](https://suzyquilts.com/best-way-to-pick-fabric-for-a-quilt/)). For a paid app, the safe routes are written agreements with each manufacturer or distributor, images supplied by a partner retailer under its own rights, and the user's own photos for personal designs. Creator-owned lines such as Alecrim Tecidos are the easiest printed fabrics to get: the person who owns the rights is also the partner who wants the sales. Solid-colour cards (colour values and names) carry much less risk and can cover many brands at once. Their counts vary between sources: Kona 243–366, Moda Bella 210–473, Art Gallery Pure Solids 135–203 ([Fat Quarter Shop](https://blog.fatquartershop.com/notion-of-month-color-cards/), [Little Fabric Shop](https://littlefabricshop.com/collections/fabric-color-cards.oembed)). The brand and colour names are still trademarks, so use them fairly. A lawyer should confirm the position under Brazil's copyright law (Lei 9.610/98) before any printed images go into a paid product.

**The recommended fabric plan has four layers, in this order.** First come the user's own photos, which need the "+ Add fabric" tile. Second come solid colour cards from Kona, Moda Bella, Art Gallery and Tilda. Third come Brazilian mills (Fernando Maluhy, Peripan, Karsten Ateliê, Fabricart and the digital-print shops) and creator-owned lines (Alecrim Tecidos, Pano Xadrez). Approach them directly and offer images in exchange for buyers sent to their stockists or their own shop. Mid-size firms without swatch programmes are more likely to say yes to a person than to a form. Fourth come international printed collections, either through a partner retailer or through manufacturers who already supply EQ. Missouri Star is the obvious US retailer target because it reaches Kaufman and most big brands. The cart should be built in three steps. Start with a **shopping list grouped by shop**, with each shop's unit applied and ordinary product links, which works with every store. Next add one-click cart links for partner shops on Shopify, VTEX and Nuvemshop. Last, add a Quiltster-style "send my kit request" to a partner shop that cuts and ships, which suits Brazil's 50 cm cut culture and avoids keeping variant IDs current. The 111-place list already holds **25 places with an online store**, the natural first cart partners. Their platforms have not been checked yet.

## The teacher channel is unclaimed, but the cut is a bonus rather than income

### Precedents and percentages

No quilt design tool found pays teachers a share of student subscriptions. Electric Quilt comes closest. It lists independent teachers who fill in a credentials form, gives them teacher-only resources and a 14-day trial for classrooms, and ran a seminar on teaching EQ8 ([EQ](https://classes.electricquilt.com/home/independent-online-eq-classes), [EQ](https://classes.electricquilt.com/class-catalog/how-to-teach-an-eq8-class/)). It also sells guilds a US$10 presentation video and keeps a guilds-and-groups page ([EQ](https://electricquilt.com/online-shop/eq-presentational-video-for-guilds/), [EQ guilds](https://electricquilt.com/home/guilds-groups-gatherings/)). But it pays teachers nothing, offers no certification, makes each student buy a US$239.95 licence ([EQ8 class](https://classes.electricquilt.com/class-catalog/eq8-beginner-class)), and sells its own video classes against them ([EQ At Home](https://classes.electricquilt.com/home/eq-at-home-faq)). Sewing-machine brands pay ambassadors in loaned machines and discounts, not cash ([WeAllSew](https://weallsew.com/?p=129546)). Craft video platforms share revenue on content. Creative Spark's standard is 50/50 ([C&T](https://ctpub.com/pages/become-an-instructor)). Craftsy switched instructors to pay by "minutes watched", and all twelve instructors interviewed reported a dramatic drop in income ([Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/tag/rebranding/)). Quilting teachers remember that story, so they will want written, stable terms. The strongest precedent is Skillshare: since April 2022 its teachers get **60% of the net membership payments** of the paying members they refer, paid monthly. That replaced a flat US$10 per free-trial sign-up ([Skillshare](https://help.skillshare.com/hc/articles/4415798485389), [Skillshare](https://help.skillshare.com/hc/articles/4440423784333)).

Brazilian teachers already understand the Hotmart pattern. Patchwork courses and sewing "clubs" sold as auto-renewing subscriptions are on Hotmart today ([Hotmart](https://hotmart.com/pt-br/marketplace/produtos/curso-basico-de-patchwork/W77194113F), [Inaá terms](https://comunidadeinaa.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Termos-e-Condicoes-11.04.2024.pdf), [Hotmart: Comunidade KDS](https://hotmart.com/pt-br/marketplace/produtos/comunidade-kds/P95945724A)). That cuts both ways. Teachers know what a commission is, but some will compare your share with the 40–70% of course affiliates. **A recurring 25% for as long as the student keeps paying** sits at the generous end of software norms, though below Skillshare's 60%. It is easier to defend if you explain it as "income that keeps coming for every student who stays" and pay it only on money actually received ([Univers Studio terms](https://univers.studio/pt/affiliates/terms/)).

It reached 9.5 million transactions in Q2 2026, and receivers grew from about 1,000 to 14,000 by August 2026 ([Finsiders](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/giro-noticias/pix-automatico-cresce-no-1o-semestre-de-2026-diz-bc/)). Hotmart reports a 32-point rise in retention for recurring Pix after adding it, a company figure with no baseline given ([EBANX](https://business.ebanx.com/en/press-room/press-releases/hotmart-sees-a-32-percentage-point-boost-in-retention-after-launching-recurring-payments-with-pix-via-ebanx)). ### Taxes and paying teachers (take this to an accountant)

A Brazilian company charging in reais avoids IOF for the customer. Buyers of foreign tools pay IOF at 3.38–3.5% (sources conflict, and a decree phases it to zero by 2028) ([Tecnoblog](https://tecnoblog.net/noticias/governo-cria-decreto-para-zerar-iof-do-cartao-ate-2028-veja-calendario/)). A Brazilian company is also the natural route for splits to Brazilian partners. Software as a service pays **ISS** at 2–5% depending on the city (São Paulo 2.9%) after the STF decision ([TaxUp](https://taxup.com.br/iss-software-saas-municipio-codigo-retencao/)). Every sale needs an NFS-e. In 2026, a test year of the tax reform, invoices must carry the new CBS (0.9%) and IBS (0.1%) fields ([Contábeis](https://www.contabeis.com.br/noticias/73889/reforma-tributaria-2026-veja-o-que-muda-na-fase-de-transicao/)).

For teachers there are two paths. A teacher with an MEI issues a nota fiscal for her share, within the R$81,000 a year MEI limit ([Contabilidade.com](https://contabilidade.com/blog/mei-em-2026-o-que-e-quem-pode-limite-impostos-e-quando-vale-a-pena)). Accounting blogs say some course teaching can fit an MEI instructor code, but there is no MEI code for marketing or influencer work ([Contabilidade Zen](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/mei-ou-cnpj-youtubers-creators-2026/), [Contabilizei](https://www.contabilizei.com.br/contabilizei-responde/marketing-pode-ser-mei/)). So whether a commission on app subscriptions fits a teacher's MEI is uncertain. A teacher without a CNPJ is paid by **RPA**, and the payer withholds INSS (11% cited), IRRF and possibly ISS ([Conta Azul](https://contaazul.com/blog/o-que-e-rpa-como-calcular-e-emitir/)). Sending the share through a provider's split does not by itself settle which path applies. On split platforms such as Kiwify, each party invoices its own share ([Contabilidade Zen](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/kiwify-eduzz-split-contabilidade-2026/)). Whether a commission counts as "intermediação de negócios", and which MEI occupation fits, were not answered by any source. That is a question for an accountant before launch, and the same question applies to creators.

### Unit economics, illustrated

The figures below are illustrations built from the fees above, not forecasts. Take a monthly plan at **R$24.90** paid by card through Asaas. The fee is about R$1.23, leaving R$23.67. A 25% teacher share of that net amount is about **R$5.92**. ISS at 2.9% of the price is about R$0.72. That leaves roughly **R$17** a month for you before income tax and running costs. A yearly plan at **R$179.90** has a fee of about R$5.87, a teacher share of about R$43.50, and ISS of about R$5.20, leaving about R$125 for the year (about R$10.40 a month). Yearly plans cut fixed fees and churn, but they lower monthly income. Ordinary Pix at a flat R$1.99 costs 8% of a R$24.90 charge, which is another reason to prefer Pix Automático or yearly plans.

The channel's shape comes from class data. A cooperative atelier runs weekly 2-hour classes by level, with 7–10 students per class, at R$150–165 a month ([Sicoob Cressem](https://www.sicoob.com.br/web/cressem/cursos-oficinas)). Private lessons average about R$40 an hour ([Superprof](https://www.superprof.com.br/aulas/patchwork/presidente-prudente/)). Senac's course runs 160 hours ([Senac SP](https://www.sp.senac.br/cursos-livres/curso-de-confeccionador-de-patchwork)). A teacher with three level-classes has 20–30 students. If 30–50% subscribe (an assumption to test), that is about 5–15 paying students. At R$5.92 each, 10 students give the teacher about **R$59 a month**, and 30 give about R$178, roughly one student's class fee. That is a nice bonus, not a reason to change habits. The pitch must lead with what saves the teacher work: free software, sending a block to the whole class, printed templates and cutting lists for handouts, and a class view of students' designs.

**The starting target list holds 111 places** (`research/ateliers/ateliers.xlsx`), drawn from search snippets. 80 are ateliers or schools, 53 also sell fabric, 25 have an online store and 11 are groups. 86 are in the South and Southeast, 41 of them in São Paulo. Only 43 are confirmed active, 3 may be closed, and 45 are low-confidence entries, so expect to clean it. The creator research adds a handful of Instagram ateliers with classes and shops that may not be on that list: Quilteria, Nosso Ateliê (Guarulhos), Ateliê Santina Reale (Cruzeiro, since 1993), Santa Costura, Rose Art's (São Caetano do Sul) and Valquiria Ferraz ([IG @quilteria](https://www.instagram.com/quilteria/), [IG @denossoatelie](https://www.instagram.com/denossoatelie/), [IG @ateliersantinareale](https://www.instagram.com/ateliersantinareale/), [IG @roseartsscs](https://www.instagram.com/roseartsscs/)). Suppose 80 teaching places average 10 paying students each. That is 800 subscribers, about R$19,900 a month gross at R$24.90, or roughly **R$13,600 a month after fees, the teacher's share and ISS**, before income tax and costs. That is enough to show the model works, not enough to live on. Growth has to come from Senac units, cooperatives, festivals (Floripa Quilt, Brazil Patchwork Show, Limeira, Curitiba's Quilt & Craft Show) and fabric shops ([NSC Total](https://www.nsctotal.com.br/?p=6305889), [Diário do Litoral](https://www.diariodolitoral.com.br/cotidiano/interior-de-sp-recebe-uma-das-maiores-feira-de-artesanato-textil-do/), [IG @quiltcraftshow](https://www.instagram.com/quiltcraftshow/)), and now from creators.

### Risks in the channel

**Churn after the course is the main risk.** Lifetime payout is roughly price × share ÷ monthly churn ([Refgrow](https://refgrow.com/blog/saas-affiliate-program-mistakes)). With 10% monthly churn, the average student stays ten months, so the teacher earns about R$59 and you about R$170 per student. Atelier classes are ongoing monthly memberships ("mensalidade"), which suits a subscription better than a one-off course. Your saved designs, "My blocks", the fabric library and the cart give students reasons to stay after class.

**Trust is the second risk.** After Craftsy, teachers will want a dashboard of their students and payments, and terms that cannot change overnight.

**Small payouts cause departures.** Neighbourhood gyms left Wellhub because their payment per visit felt small next to their own fees ([Correio 24 Horas](https://www.correio24horas.com.br/salvador/fim-do-gympass-academias-de-bairro-rompem-com-plataformas-e-tendencia-cresce-no-pais-0226)).

**Sign-up friction is real.** Opening a payment subaccount means identity checks, a hurdle for non-technical teachers. Paying monthly by Pix against an MEI nota or RPA is simpler at first.

**Channel conflict needs rules, and the creator channel makes it sharper.** Decide up front who gets credit when a student signs up alone, or through a YouTuber, and later joins a class. Hotmart's rules are a model: last click by default, a coupon beats a cookie, and "HotLeads" keeps the credit when no cookie is found ([Hotmart](https://help.hotmart.com/en/article/360017792651/how-do-cookies-work-at-hotmart-), [Hotmart](https://help.hotmart.com/pt-br/article/36806399753485/quais-sao-os-criterios-de-priorizacao-na-atribuicao-de-comissao-para-afiliados-na-hotmart-)). A **teacher coupon code** is the easiest thing to explain in class. The full teacher-versus-creator rule is in the creator section below.

**Free public classes compete for beginners.** Curitiba's Liceu is one example ([Prefeitura de Curitiba](https://www.curitiba.pr.gov.br/noticias/patchwork-croche-e-scrapbooking-estao-nas-oficinas-gratuitas-do-liceu-de-oficios-criativos/79722)).

## YouTubers bring reach, and no quilt app shares subscriptions with them yet

### Social media is now how quilters discover quilting

The case for a creator channel starts with how people find the hobby. The 2026 Craft Industry Alliance survey found that **social media is now the main way all generations discover quilting**, even though the typical quilter is 67 ([Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)). In Brazil that means YouTube, Instagram and Hotmart. A TikTok search for "patchwork Brasil" returned only tattoos and iron-on patches, not quilting creators ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/patchwork-tattoos-brasil)). YouTube viewing in Brazil is also local: **85% of the viewing of Brazilian channels comes from Brazil itself** ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/intl/pt-br/inside-youtube/campanha-yt-2025/)). Brazilian creators reach Brazilians, and Spanish- and English-speaking markets need their own creators. Teachers reach the 20–30 students in their classes. One mid-size YouTuber reaches more patchwork beginners in a week than the whole 111-place atelier list does in a term. That reach comes with a weaker bond, and the deal has to reflect it.

### Precedents: quilting pays per video, creative learning pays a share

Quilting and sewing companies mostly use two tools. One is **low-rate retail affiliate links** (6–10%, short cookies). The other is **non-cash ambassador programmes** (machine loans, discounts, visibility). Quilt software pays per video. The closest analogues to a share of subscriptions come from creative-learning platforms and from one Brazilian software company.

The pattern is clear. Physical-goods retailers pay 6–10% because their margins are thin. Digital subscriptions can afford 20–60% because each extra subscriber costs almost nothing to serve, and your app sits in that group. Quilt software has so far bought creators' time, not shared their results. **A revenue share would be a new kind of offer for Brazilian patchwork creators**, not a crowded one. No Brazilian sewing-machine or fabric brand was found with a documented creator programme, so most Brazilian deals today are probably informal (free product plus "#publi"). No source confirmed that.

## Give creators a shorter share than teachers, and let teachers win ties

### Why the two partners deserve different terms

The research supports treating creators and teachers differently, because they bring different customers. Customers who come through **rewarded referrals** from people they know churn about 18% less and are worth about 16% more over time. These are Wharton figures relayed by vendors ([Cello](https://cello.so/?p=17802)). One comparison puts paid-ad customers at about 8% monthly churn against 3% for organic search ([Hyros](https://hyros.com/updates/power-features/churn-attribution-ad-sources/)). Affiliates are paid at the moment of the sale and have little reason to care whether the customer stays ([Refgrow](https://refgrow.com/blog/complete-guide-referral-program-2026)). High-volume affiliates can bring lower-value accounts that churn and erode net revenue ([Cello](https://cello.so/blog/referral-programs-vs-affiliate-programs-b2b-saas/)). A teacher sees her students every week, uses the app in class and answers their questions. A YouTuber reaches thousands once, and many of them sign up on impulse. No source measured churn for craft-app sign-ups by channel, so you will need to track groups by channel yourself (teacher, creator code, organic). The direction is clear enough to set terms now.

### Deal terms side by side

| Term | Teacher (atelier) | Creator (YouTuber, Instagram) |
|---|---|---|
| Own account | Free for good | Free Pro account, plus a "Criadora parceira" badge on her profile and in the app |
| Share | About **25% of net money received** | About **20% of net money received**; optional tiers by volume, as SaaS programmes do (for example 15% for 1–10 subscribers, 20% for 11–25, 25% for 26+) ([Hamster Garage](https://www.hamstergarage.com/article/saas-affiliate-commission-rates-benchmarks-formulas)) |
| How long | **For as long as the student pays** (Skillshare, Nuvemshop "Especialistas") | **12 months per subscriber**, the most common cap ([Hamster Garage](https://www.hamstergarage.com/article/saas-affiliate-commission-rates-benchmarks-formulas)); yearly plan paid once on the first year |
| Fixed fee | None | Optional small fee for a dedicated tutorial video from a channel with steady views, for example R$300–1,000 (my estimate, scaled down from US data; no Brazilian price table was found) |
| Offer for the student or viewer | Class price, first free month with the teacher's code | Extended trial (Skillshare gives 30 days) or a first-month discount applied through the creator's link; avoid a large reusable public code |
| Tracking | Class link or class code, locked to the teacher | Link plus personal code, 30–60 day window between click and sign-up |
| Payment | Monthly, after a 30-day hold, only on money received | Same |
| Disclosure | Required when she posts the code online (CONAR treats any paid recommendation as advertising) | Required in every video, post and story (see the rules section below) |
| Exit | Notice period; earned shares keep being paid | Same, plus a morality and termination clause |

The 25%-versus-20% gap matters less than the time limit. A teacher's student who stays three years keeps paying her share, while a creator's share ends at month twelve. That matches what each one does for retention. The hybrid fee is optional because the research points both ways. Creators with real audiences usually ask for a base fee plus 10–15%, and they are advised to counter commission-only offers ([PostAffiliatePro](https://www.postaffiliatepro.com/blog/influencer-rates-flat-fee-vs-commission/), [CreatorsAgency](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/youtube-affiliate-vs-flat-rate-deals)). Typical US examples are US$1,500 plus 10%, or US$3,000 plus 10% for 90 days ([InfluencerFee](https://www.influencerfee.com/blog/youtube-influencer-affiliate-rates/)). But most Brazilian creators are small. In a Squid study, **78% had 50,000 followers or fewer, only 18% lived entirely from campaigns, and only 1.2% earned more than R$15,000 a month** ([ABC da Comunicação](https://www.abcdacomunicacao.com.br/qual-e-o-perfil-do-influenciador-brasileiro-estudo-inedito-da-squid-traca-o-raio-x-dos-criadores-de-conteudo/)). Few agencies sit between brands and small creators, so most small creators deal with brands directly ([Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/midia/mediacao-entre-agencias-e-microinfluenciadores-ainda-e-baixa)). For them, free product, status and a fair share are a real offer. Pay a fee only where the views justify it, and price by expected views, not followers ([Influency.me](https://influency.me/blog/cpm-de-influenciadores)).

### Creators who teach get a course pack and a cart in their own shop

Most of Brazil's big patchwork creators are also teachers with paid courses: Ana Cosentino, Tia Lili, Natália Engel, Márcia Satiko and Patricia Washhington. For them, a plain affiliate link is the weakest offer. The stronger offer is to **bundle the app into their own courses and shops**. Her course's blocks come pre-drawn in the app, as a "course pack" with cutting lists and A4 templates in her name. Her course students join through a class link, on teacher terms, for as long as they pay. Her own fabric line or shop becomes the cart the app fills. Ana Cosentino's Alecrim Tecidos and Tia Lili's Pano Xadrez are exactly that kind of shop. Natália Engel's twelve block-study modules and Tia Lili's R$597 "A Base de Tudo" course are natural first course packs.

The bundle serves three objectives at once. It brings in printed Brazilian fabrics with permission from the person who owns them. It fills a cart at a shop that wants the sale. It turns impulse viewers into course students, who behave like class students. PreQuilt's partnership with Suzy Quilts, which put her patterns inside the tool, is the nearest precedent ([Suzy Quilts](https://suzyquilts.com/?p=150778)). Under this design, one person can hold two codes: a **class link** for her course students on teacher terms, and a **creator code** for her public videos on creator terms.

### Attribution rules for teacher-versus-creator conflicts

Channel conflict means two sales routes competing for the same customer. The standard advice is to write the rules before the first dispute and aim for managed, not zero, conflict ([Reditus](https://getreditus.com/resources/glossary/channel-conflict)). One vendor's framework uses last click within each partner type and a tiebreaker that favours one partner class, with the voided credit logged ([Track360](https://track360.io/blog/hybrid-influencer-affiliate-programs-architecture-2026)). Hotmart shows why the rule must be right from the start: its "multi-click" split between first and last affiliate can only be switched on before a product has affiliates, and it cannot be undone ([Hotmart](https://help.hotmart.com/en/article/215828158/what-rules-to-attribute-commission-can-i-choose-for-my-product-)).

The proposed rule for your app has five parts, written into the partner terms before launch. First, a student who joins through a teacher's class link or class code belongs to that teacher for as long as she stays subscribed. That claim is locked. Second, a creator code counts only if the account has no teacher. If a teacher's student later types a creator's code, the teacher keeps the share and the creator gets nothing, or at most a small one-time bonus. Third, if a creator-referred subscriber later joins a teacher's class, the creator keeps her 12-month window and the share then moves to the teacher. This is simpler to explain than splitting each payment, and fair to both. Fourth, between two creators, a code typed at checkout beats an earlier link click, as on Hotmart. Fifth, every partner sees her attributed subscribers in a dashboard, anonymised, so she can trust the count. No documented teacher-versus-influencer dispute in craft or education software was found. This rule is a synthesis of the general sources plus the Hotmart and Nuvemshop precedents.

### Coupon codes leak within hours

Creator-exclusive codes can appear on Honey, RetailMeNot and similar sites within hours, and browser extensions offer them automatically at checkout ([Uniqodo](https://www.uniqodo.com/post/stop-paying-affiliate-commission-on-stolen-codes), [LoudCrowd](https://loudcrowd.com/blog/coupon-fraud/)). One developer found one influencer's code on 14 coupon sites ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/vhub_systems_ed5641f65d59/i-found-my-influencer-discount-code-on-14-coupon-sites-the-creators-performance-was-just-honey-1kn3)). When that happens, you pay a commission on customers the creator never sent. The known defences are partner-locked or single-use codes, rotating a code once it leaks, anti-coupon-site clauses, discounts applied through a link with no code to copy, and comparing each partner's redemptions with her audience and posting activity ([Track360](https://track360.io/blog/coupon-affiliate-sites-operator-management-guide-2026), [Saral](https://help.getsaral.com/en/articles/338-best-practices-when-influencer-codes-or-links-get-leaked), [Aspire](https://www.aspire.io/securecodes)). Self-referral and refund abuse are the other leaks. Retail programmes pay nothing on returned, cancelled, fraudulent or self-referral orders ([Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/89535)). Checks for both can be built on the billing data ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/mihirkanzariya/self-referral-and-coupon-leaks-the-affiliate-fraud-checks-worth-building-on-stripe-1376)). A vendor claims fraud costs "10–30% of affiliate spend", with no study behind it ([Refgrow](https://refgrow.com/blog/affiliate-fraud-detection-guide)).

For your app, the practical design is to make the creator's viewer offer an **extended trial or a first-month discount applied through her link**, not a large public code. The code exists mainly so the viewer can type it when the link is lost. Pay commission only after a 30-day hold, so refunds and chargebacks reverse first, and never on a partner's own account. Brazilian coupon sites (Cuponomia, Méliuz, Pelando) were not researched, so check them by hand once codes are live. The teacher's class code leaks less, because it is shared in a room and tied to a class list, which is one more reason to let it win ties.

## Link plus code, Asaas split and a small partner table do the tracking

**Use both a referral link and a personal coupon code.** Codes work across devices and when cookies are refused. Links give the cleanest attribution. Hotmart is the well-documented Brazilian reference. It uses last click by default, the seller chooses the cookie length (60 days by default, or 90, 180 or "forever"), a coupon overrides the cookie, and "HotLeads" keeps the commission when no cookie is found, for example in private tabs or on another device ([Hotmart](https://help.hotmart.com/en/article/360017792651/how-do-cookies-work-at-hotmart-), [Hotmart](https://help.hotmart.com/en/article/360016601731/how-are-affiliate-commissions-determined-at-hotmart-)). Cookie lengths elsewhere are Domestika 30 days, Fat Quarter Shop 7, Cricut about 5, and typical SaaS 60–90; lifetime cookies are rare ([QualityUnit](https://cdn.qualityunit.com/faq/recommended-cookie-lifetime-affiliate-programs)). Your server already has users and accounts, so the simplest start is first-party. Give each partner a short link (for example `/?p=code`) and a matching code. Save the code on the account at sign-up, on your own server. Keep a 30–60 day window between click and sign-up. Add a small partner table with code, kind (teacher or creator), share percentage, months cap and start date, and produce monthly statements from it. That may be simpler than a third-party tool at first, and it avoids long-lived third-party cookies.

**For payouts in Brazil, Asaas or Pagar.me split payments are the realistic route.** Asaas divides each payment among several Asaas accounts ("wallets") and names affiliates as an intended use. On a subscription, the split works as a **template for future charges**: changing it does not change charges already created, and sending an empty split removes it. Split works only through the API, not from the plain digital account ([Asaas](https://docs.asaas.com/docs/payment-split-overview), [Asaas blog](https://blog.asaas.com/glossario/split-de-pagamento/)). One limit matters directly for creator discounts. **If the split adds up to more than the net amount of a charge, Asaas blocks the subscription** (webhook `SUBSCRIPTION_SPLIT_DIVERGENCE_BLOCK`) until it is fixed within two business days ([Asaas](https://docs.asaas.com/docs/fluxo-de-bloqueio-de-assinatura-por-diverg%C3%AAncia-de-split)). A fixed-reais split on a discounted first month could trip this, so set partner splits as a percentage of the net amount, and test discounts and refunds before launch. Each partner needs her own wallet, which means identity checks. Pagar.me supports subscriptions split to several recipients with one or more rules, and platforms already use it for producer, co-producer and affiliate splits ([Pagar.me](https://docs.pagar.me/reference/split-1), [OnProfit](https://help.onprofit.com.br/en-us/article/how-to-activate-payment-split-on-pagarme-1k5tx5t/)). Split fees, and whether a partner without a CNPJ can receive a split, were not found for either provider.

**Brazil's infoproduct platforms show what creators already know, but they suit courses, not your app's billing.** Kiwify sells subscriptions by card, Pix and boleto. It supports open or approved affiliation and "co-production" splits among two or more users. Each party invoices its own share, and individuals (CPF) reportedly face a R$2,200 limit on referral-commission withdrawals where companies do not ([Kiwify](https://help.kiwify.com/en/article/how-to-set-up-co-production-6xhfc6/), [Contabilidade Zen](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/kiwify-eduzz-split-contabilidade-2026/)). Whether Hotmart, Kiwify and Eduzz pay affiliates on every subscription renewal was not confirmed from official pages.

**For creators outside Brazil, the conventional set-up is Stripe plus an affiliate tool.** Rewardful does not pay commissions itself. You pay by PayPal or Wise mass payout, which needs verified business accounts ([Rewardful](https://help.rewardful.com/en/articles/2773351-how-do-i-pay-commissions), [Rewardful](https://www.rewardful.com/paypal-and-wise-mass-payouts)). FirstPromoter starts at US$49 a month for up to 1,000 affiliates, with link and coupon tracking, several codes per affiliate, recurring commissions, refunds and plan changes tracked through Stripe or Paddle, and payouts from the Stripe balance ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/206380/First-Promoter/), [FirstPromoter](https://changelog.firstpromoter.com/stripe-payouts-pay-affiliates-directly-from-your-stripe-balance-1SFpUk)). Impact, used by Cricut, Canva and Domestika, is aimed at larger programmes. Stripe in Brazil has the Pix limits noted in the payments table, and whether Rewardful or FirstPromoter payouts reach Brazilian recipients well was not found. The sensible order is Asaas for Brazilian teachers and creators first, then Stripe plus FirstPromoter when Spanish-language creators join.

## Under CONAR 2026 every revenue-share video is an advertisement

**CONAR's new Influencer Advertising Guide makes performance pay advertising.** CONAR published a new edition in May 2026, in force from 1 June 2026, replacing the March 2021 guide ([Propmark](https://propmark.com.br/digital/conar-atualiza-guia-de-publicidade-com-influenciadores-e-inclui-regras-sobre-ia/), [Contábeis](https://www.contabeis.com.br/artigos/77061/conar-novas-regras-para-influenciadores-e-ia-a-partir-de-1o-de-junho/)). It explicitly covers **affiliates, meaning anyone paid on clicks, sales or other results**. Commission links, coupons and affiliate programmes count as advertising, exactly like a contracted campaign ([UAI](https://www.uai.com.br/networking-e-negocios/2026/09/23/ganha-comissao-com-link-de-afiliado-para-o-conar-isso-ja-e-publicidade/), [Cescon Barrieu](https://cesconbarrieu.com.br/novas-diretrizes-do-conar-para-marketing-de-influencia-o-que-muda/)). Any give-and-take between brand and creator is enough to make content advertising, even if the brand has no editorial control ([TozziniFreire](https://www.tozzinifreire.com.br/en/boletins/novo-guia-do-conar-o-que-muda-para-campanhas-com-influenciadores-digitais)). So **every creator on your revenue share is an advertiser, even with no fee**, and so is a teacher who posts her class code on Instagram.

Disclosure must be clear, prominent, immediate and part of the content itself. It must be visible on first view, not hidden behind "more", in the bio or after scrolling. The platforms' own paid-content tools are to be used first. Sources differ on whether those tools replace "#publi"/"#publicidade" or go alongside them ([Propmark](https://propmark.com.br/artigos/o-novo-guia-do-conar-para-influenciadores-e-os-riscos-da-falta-de-transparencia/), [KLA Law](https://www.klalaw.com.br/conar-atualiza-guia-de-publicidade-com-influenciadores/), [Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/456124/guia-conar-2026-o-que-todo-influenciador-precisa-saber-agora)), so ask for both. Compliance is **not only the creator's job**. Brands should map their affiliate and ambassador formats and record the relationship in a contract ([Cescon Barrieu](https://cesconbarrieu.com.br/marketing-de-influencia-conar/)). AI-made content and virtual influencers follow the same rules, with extra care for children under the ECA Digital law (Law 15.211/2025) ([Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/brazil/social-media/1796358/conar-updates-guidelines-on-advertising-with-influencers)). The official CONAR PDF could not be opened, so the exact wording, including whether long videos need repeated disclosure, is unconfirmed.

**YouTube's own rule is the paid-promotion box.** Videos with paid product placements, endorsements or sponsorships must tick the paid promotion box in the video details. That covers videos, descriptions, comments, live streams and Shorts, and viewers then see an "includes paid promotion" notice ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235?hl=en)). YouTube reportedly renamed the setting "Branded Content" and may apply the label itself when a creator fails to; the date was not confirmed ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-renames-paid-product-placement-to-branded-content-adds-disclosure-controls-while-testing-local-affiliate-retailers/)). US guidance says the platform label alone may not be enough and the disclosure belongs in the video itself ([Creator Essentials](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/sponsored-content/)). Whether YouTube requires the box for pure affiliate links with no fee was not confirmed, but CONAR settles it for Brazil. The partner agreement should require three things: the box ticked, a spoken or on-screen "publicidade" mention at the start of the segment, and the link and code in the first lines of the description. Give partners a ready disclosure line in Portuguese, Spanish and English, and keep proof (the video link and a screenshot) in the partner record.

**Under the consumer code (CDC), your company and the creator can be jointly liable.** Article 36 requires advertising to be easy to identify, and article 37 forbids misleading advertising, including by leaving things out ([Conjur](https://www.conjur.com.br/2023-abr-05/responsabilidade-influencers-indicam-produtos-otica-cdc//?print=1)). Because the CDC defines "supplier" broadly (art. 3), the influencer can be part of the supply chain, and joint liability (arts. 7 sole paragraph and 25 §1) can apply. Many scholars argue for strict liability, while a minority disagrees ([Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/423676/parceria-influenciador-e-empresa-responsabilidade-de-ambas-as-partes)). One source says São Paulo's court distinguishes a passing mention from structured advertising, and that a **discount code plus affiliate link weighs toward liability** ([Lex Legal](https://lexlegal.com.br/?p=29901)). The ruling itself was not seen. In practice, review creators' scripts for claims you cannot prove (for example "prints exact templates for every block" or "used by X quilters"), and keep the 3D drape and fabric claims to what the app really does.

**LGPD: your own referral code is easier than third-party tracking.** The data protection authority's guide on cookies (October 2022) is guidance, not a binding rule. It says consent is the fitting legal basis for non-essential cookies such as marketing ones, that refusing must be as visible as accepting, and that withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it ([Lefosse](https://lefosse.com/noticias/anpd-publica-guia-orientativo-sobre-o-uso-de-cookies), [Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/direito-digital/anpd-divulga-novo-guia-com-orientacoes-sobre-cookies)). A referral code saved on the account at sign-up and used only to pay the partner is arguably part of running the service (contract or legitimate interest) rather than marketing tracking. Third-party ad or affiliate pixels would need a consent banner. That reading is an inference for a lawyer to confirm.

**Paying creators: no MEI, so a company invoice or an RPA.** Accounting firms agree there is **no MEI activity code for influencers or content creators**, and marketing is not on the MEI list. Creators usually need an ME under Simples Nacional. These are accounting blogs, not the official Receita list ([Contabilidade.com](https://contabilidade.com/blog/influencer-pode-ser-mei-descubra-como-abrir-empresa/), [Contabilidade Zen](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/mei-ou-cnpj-youtubers-creators-2026/), [Actana](https://actana.com.br/blog/afiliado-shopee-pode-ser-mei-simples-nacional)). Paying a creator who has no CNPJ means your company issues an **RPA** and withholds INSS (11%) and progressive IRRF, plus ISS in some cases. Shopee's affiliate programme withholds exactly that, and without an invoice the withholding can reach 27.5% IRRF plus INSS ([Contabilidade Zen](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/rpa-recibo-pagamento-autonomo-modelo-2026/), [Shopee](https://shopee.com.br/blog/afiliado-shopee-comissao-pagamentos/), [Meu Contador Online](https://www.meucontadoronline.com.br/blog/influenciador-nota-fiscal-2026/)). Paying many individual affiliates by RPA each month raises eSocial reporting questions (S-1200 or S-2500) that a forum thread left unanswered ([Contábeis forum](https://www.contabeis.com.br/forum/Tributos%20Federais/415123/rpa-em-lote-para-afiliado-pf-comissao-extra-shopee-periodicidade-certa-no-esocial-s-1200-s-2500/)). The simplest rule is to pay creators only through their own company (ME/Simples) against a nota fiscal, or through a split where each party invoices its own share. Big creators such as Ana Cosentino and Tia Lili already run shops, so they almost certainly have a company. Small ones may not. The effect of the tax reform (CBS/IBS) on commission payments was not researched, and the disclosure rules in Mexico, Argentina and Spain were not covered. All of this needs an accountant and a lawyer before the first creator contract.

### A contract checklist for creators

The recommended clauses come from the sources above. The contract should cover deliverables, the share and the base it is calculated on (net money received), the hold period and refund rule, usage rights for the video, exclusivity if any, approval of claims, the disclosure duties, and how either side ends the deal ([Matt McWilliams](https://www.mattmcwilliams.com/hybrid-affiliate-influencer-deals/)). It should also include the retail programmes' standard exclusions: no commission on self-referrals or fraud, no bidding on your brand name in search ads, and no posting on coupon sites ([Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/89535)). Add a morality clause, a right to change terms with notice, as Canva and Skillshare both did, and a promise to keep paying what was already earned ([Canva help](https://www.canva.com/pt_pt/help/canva-affiliate-marketing-program/)). BERNINA's fixed 18-month ambassador term shows that a fixed term is a normal way to manage the relationship ([WeAllSew](https://weallsew.com/?p=117250)).

## One video pays a creator about as much as a small sponsorship

### A creator's numbers, illustrated

The figures below are illustrations, not forecasts, using the same R$24.90 monthly plan and Asaas card fee as the teacher example. Net of the fee, each payment is about R$23.67. A **20% creator share is about R$4.73 a month per subscriber**. After ISS (about R$0.72), **you keep about R$18.21 a month during her 12-month window and about R$22.94 after it**, compared with about R$17.03 for a teacher-referred student. Over a full 12 months, one creator-referred subscriber earns the creator at most about R$56.80.

How many subscribers does a video bring? Ana Cosentino's channel averages about **28,700 lifetime views per video** (28.7M views over about 1,000 videos), Mininos de Sánchez about 65,400 and Angela Walters about 36,500 (calculated from the tracker figures above). Recent videos may get fewer views than the lifetime average. Take a dedicated tutorial with 30,000 views. If 2–5% of viewers click the link (an assumption; no source measured this) and **1–3% of clicks become paying subscribers**, a typical rate for paid subscriptions ([Hamster Garage](https://www.hamstergarage.com/article/saas-affiliate-program-benchmarks-kpi-guide)), the video brings **between about 6 and 45 subscribers, roughly 20 in the middle**. Twenty subscribers pay the creator about R$95 a month. If they stay six months on average (also an assumption), that is about **R$570 from one video**, and at most about R$1,140 if all twenty stay the full year.

That is the same order of money as the alternatives. YouTube's own ads pay Brazilian creators about R$1–20 per 1,000 monetised views, depending on niche ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/pt/quanto-youtube-paga-por-video/)), so the same 30,000 views earn about R$30–600 from AdSense. A comparable US channel (A Quilting Life, about 130K subscribers) is estimated at US$129–259 per sponsored video ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/aquiltinglife)). US sponsorship prices run about US$10–50 per 1,000 expected views, falling as channels grow ([1of10](https://www.1of10.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/), [Adopter Media](https://adopter.media/?p=1151)), and the median YouTube asking price fell from US$675 to US$311 between Collabstr's 2025 and 2026 reports ([Collabstr](https://collabstr.com/blog/influencer-costs-2025)). So a revenue share from one good video is competitive with a small sponsorship, but it is paid slowly and nobody knows the amount in advance. That uncertainty is why creators ask for a base fee. The way to win them without one is to make the share visible: a live dashboard, and payment every month.

The course pack changes the scale. Ana Cosentino claims more than 10,000 students. If only 1% of them took the app through her class link on teacher terms, that is 100 subscribers paying her about **R$590 a month for as long as they stay**, more than a single video would bring in a year. This is the strongest reason to pitch teacher-creators on bundling rather than on videos.

A viewer discount costs you more than it costs the creator. On a yearly plan at R$179.90 with 15% off for viewers (R$152.92), the card fee is about R$5.06, the creator's 20% about R$29.57 and ISS about R$4.43, leaving you about R$113.85 for the year. That compares with about R$125 for a full-price yearly student through a teacher. A free first month costs you only server time, and the creator earns nothing on it, which makes an extended trial the cheapest viewer offer.

### How the two channels fit together

Put the teacher and creator illustrations side by side. The teacher channel at 80 ateliers × 10 students gives about 800 subscribers and roughly **R$13,600 a month** to you. Now suppose ten Brazilian creators each publish four app videos or integrations a year, at about 20 subscribers each. That is about 800 new subscribers a year. If they stay six months on average, about 400 are active at any time, giving you roughly **R$7,300 a month** and the creators about R$1,900 a month between them (about R$190 each). Together that is about **R$20,900 a month** before income tax and costs. If you also paid a R$300–1,000 fee for each of those forty videos, at an average of R$650 the fees would cost about R$2,170 a month, close to a third of the creator channel's income. Fees should go only to channels with proven views.

The numbers show why the two channels need each other. **Creators are the top of the funnel**: they put the app in front of tens of thousands of beginners in Portuguese, which no atelier can. **Teachers are the bottom of the funnel**: they turn sign-ups into students who stay, which no video can. The attribution rule above lets a viewer start with a creator and settle with a teacher without anyone feeling cheated. Creators who also teach sit in both places at once, which makes them the first people to approach. Creator videos also solve a problem the teacher channel cannot. Viewers in towns with no atelier on the 111-place list can still find the app, and the Spanish-language channels give a way into Latin America before any teacher network exists there.

Keep teachers free for good, since their free account is the whole channel.

## Ease of use is an open position, especially for quilters over 60

The core quilter is a retired woman averaging **67 years old** who prefers her local shop ([Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)). The research found **no quilt design software marketed with large text, a simple mode or features for older users** ([Quilting Daily](https://www.quiltingdaily.com/workshop-wednesday-easy-quilt-design-software-mccalls-quilting-blog)). EQ's only product made for "beginning computer users", the Quilt Design Wizard, is no longer sold ([EQ shop](https://electricquilt.com/online-shop/quilt-design-wizard)). EQ8 users describe a "huge learning curve", although others say it "isn't brain surgery" ([Quiltingboard](https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/eq8-not-t306667-2.html)). EQ8's learning help is mostly outside the program and paid: the *EQ8 Lessons for Beginners* book, EQ classes and AQS workshops ([Searchpress](https://searchpress.com/book/9781893824928/eq8-lessons-for-beginners), [American Quilter](https://preview.americanquilter.com/design-your-first-quilt-in-eq8)). PreQuilt is "easy to use once you get the hang of it", and its makers wrote guides and a YouTube course for the learning curve ([sewingmachinefun](https://www.sewingmachinefun.com/free-quilt-design-software/)). Quilt Wizard's interface is "not remotely intuitive" ([AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/quilt-wizard/475622761?country=us)), and Quiltler 2 drew "confusing" complaints. The newer apps (Swatchit, QuiltFit) have too few ratings to judge, and no 2025–2026 usability comparison exists.

Your app has the ingredients to claim this ground: a guided tour, a Cells drawing mode that needs no geometry, an undo timeline, screens in the student's own language, and cutting lists in the units her rulers use. Both partner channels are themselves ease-of-use features. In class, a teacher shows the student how, as EQ's teacher network does for EQ8 but in English only. On YouTube, a creator's tutorial in Portuguese is the help video EQ8 users buy as books and classes. Electric Quilt's sponsored "Designing Scrap Quilts in EQ8" video is exactly that kind of tutorial ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/chatterboxquilts)), so the creator channel can double as the app's video manual if the contract allows you to link to the videos from inside the app. The claim of ease still needs evidence. Watch five or ten students over 60 in a pilot atelier try three tasks: colour a quilt, draw a simple block, print the cutting list. Fix what trips them up. Consider a larger text setting and bigger tap targets for tablets. Until then, say "made to be easy" rather than "easiest", and keep creators to the same wording.

## Conclusion

Adding creators changes what kind of company this is. The atelier study showed that the value lies in relationships, with teachers, mills and shops. The creator study shows that in Brazilian patchwork those relationships are often held by the same people. The women with the biggest channels also run the courses, own the fabric lines and ship the kits. A creator programme designed only as an affiliate link would treat them as billboards and pay them like billboards. Designed as a course pack with a cart in their own shop, it makes each of them teacher, fabric partner and cart partner at once. That is a bundle Electric Quilt's pay-per-video model cannot offer, and no rival offers it in Portuguese.

The research also shows that the hard parts of the creator channel are rules, not reach. Brazil has many small creators who deal directly with brands, and patchwork has no crowded sponsor market to compete in. The risks are in the plumbing: who gets credit when a viewer later joins a class, codes leaking to coupon sites, a split that blocks a discounted subscription, a creator who cannot issue an MEI invoice, a video without "publicidade" in the first seconds. Each is cheap to solve before launch and expensive after it, as Hotmart's irreversible attribution switch shows. The right order is the teacher pilot first, because it tests price and retention. Payments and written partner rules come second. A handful of teacher-creators come third, because their videos only pay off once a viewer can sign up, pay and be counted correctly.
