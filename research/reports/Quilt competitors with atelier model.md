# Win Brazil through teachers, real fabrics and carts

No quilt design product found does what your new business model sets out to do. None pays teachers a share of their students' subscriptions. None shows a single Brazilian fabric brand. None fills a shop's cart for the user across several brands. The two nearest precedents are each limited. Quiltster sells partner-shop kits in its own closed marketplace and keeps a 10% fee ([Quiltster Seller Agreement](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=10470)). eQuilter's designer adds its own 20,000+ fabrics to its own cart, but only on its own patterns ([eQuilter help](https://www.equilter.com/help/free_pattern_designer)). With **300+ blocks**, a working block drawing tool that writes its own cutting list, and screens in Portuguese, Spanish and English, your app now matches EQ8 and NiftyFifty on drawing, beats every rival on languages, and still owns the 3D bed drape. Four gaps decide whether the business works. First, the fabric library is your main objective, but today it holds zero manufacturer fabrics, and the printed ones need written permission. Second, login and payments are not live, and without them there is no subscription and no way to credit teachers. Third, block count still trails EQ8's 6,700+ ([EQ](https://electricquilt.com/landing-pages/eq8)) and NiftyFifty's 1,200–1,400+ ([NiftyFifty](https://niftyfiftyquilting.com/quilt-designer)). Fourth, there is no appliqué or foundation paper piecing. Two hard facts shape the plan. Almost every big brand (Moda, Art Gallery, Riley Blake, Tilda) refuses to sell to consumers ([Moda](https://my.modafabrics.com/node/21961)), so "fill the brand's cart" really means "fill a retailer's cart". And at about R$24.90 a month with a 25% share, a teacher with ten paying students earns about R$62 a month, so the free software and the classroom features have to win teachers over, with the commission as a bonus. Your biggest competitor for a *paid* app in Brazil is not EQ8. It is free tools and graph paper. All the research came from search-result text, because web pages could not be opened. Treat every price, fee and percentage as "check before you rely on it", and take the tax and legal points to an accountant and a lawyer.

## How far to trust this: search snippets, not opened pages, and your own brief for the app

This report combines eight research notes: five from the first competitor study (desktop, web, mobile, Brazil, wider market) and three new ones (competitor fabrics and usability, fabric brands and carts, the teacher channel and pricing). In every case the tool that opens web pages was blocked, so each finding is a line of text a search engine showed, with the address it came from. That has four consequences. First, **prices, fees and ratings are snapshots**, and many come from third-party blogs rather than the provider's own price page. Asaas, Iugu and Kiwify fees, for example, come from comparison sites ([quasa.io](https://quasa.io/pt/media/asaas-ou-iugu-tarifa-publicada-vence-ate-o-volume-pedir-proposta), [Mobills](https://www.mobills.com.br/blog/ganhar-dinheiro/kiwify-afiliados/)). Second, **"not found" means the snippets did not mention it**, not that it does not exist. Third, low-quality "best quilt software" ranking pages (wifitalents, zipdo, gitnux) are used only where nothing better existed, and are labelled. Fourth, nothing was tried hands-on, so no claim about ease of use rests on testing.

The facts about your app come from your corrected brief, and they replace the earlier report wherever the two disagree. The app now has **300+ blocks** (goal 1,000+ at launch, then parity with the leaders). The **"Desenhar um bloco" dialog** has Cells and Grid-paper (Papel quadriculado) modes, grid sizes such as 4×4, seam-line, fill and erase tools, points every ¼ square, a fabric palette, symmetry, undo, saving to "My blocks", "use in quilt", a repeat preview, and an **automatic cutting list with seam allowance** (for example "8 × 4 in squares, cut once on the diagonal"). Rulers show inches ("pol") as well as metric, and the screens are in **Portuguese, Spanish and English**. These are still true from before: the 3D drape on a bed or table with cloth physics, symmetry, fussy cutting, a quilting-stitch preview, a cutting plan for 110/140/150 cm fabric widths, real-size A4 PDF templates, an app that works offline in the browser, a sync server that is built but not live, and no real login. The business model is new: a paid subscription sold through patchwork ateliers, free for teachers, with part of each student's payment going to the teacher. Its main objectives are showing all the main international and Brazilian fabric brands and filling shop carts automatically, with ease of use as the first focus.

## About 70 products, a dozen direct rivals, and none built for teachers or Brazilian fabric

The field splits into six groups. Desktop is one serious product (EQ8) plus programs tied to machine brands, frozen or dead. On the web, a crowded set of mostly young freemium tools has appeared since 2025, and it now includes a retailer's designer with a cart (eQuilter) and a marketplace (Quiltster). Mobile is small and mostly on iPad. Utilities are calculators and stash apps. Longarm and embroidery software sits alongside a piecing designer rather than replacing it. Brazil has essentially nothing local. Products in **bold** appear in the feature matrices.

### Utilities, adjacent tools and the Brazilian scene

The utility layer is where quilters actually spend app time. The Missouri Star app has about 34,000 iOS ratings at 4.9, far more than any design app ([appfollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/missouri-star-quilt-company/513781546?country=us)). Robert Kaufman's QuiltingCalc draws complaints that it lacks metric yardage ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quiltingcalc/id390366459)). There are low-waste cutting apps such as QuiltYield ([appshunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/quiltyield-fabric-calculator/id6790127815)), stash inventories such as Fabricora ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/fabricora/6753884696)), and colour matchers such as Quilty Solid, which matches photos to Art Gallery and Kona solids ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quilty-solid/id6444816769)). Longarm and stitch software costs US$400–1,000 for design programs and US$6,500–15,500 for robot systems ([Sewing Machines Plus](https://sewingmachinesplus.com/products/hq-20807-art-n-stitch-software), [Quilted Joy](https://quiltedjoy.com/blogs/blog/quilt-path-vs-intelliquilter-for-apqs-machines-complete-comparison-guide)). The only "fabric on a bed" visualisers are business tools such as Style3D, not tools for quilters ([Style3D](https://www.style3d.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/15927)).

In Portuguese and Spanish, the field is close to empty. No quilt design program made in Brazil or Latin America was found. Spanish searches found only courses and printed patterns ([Tilda ES](https://www.tildasworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Dear-Santa-Quilt-plaid-stone-Es.pdf)), and Portuguese searches found only PreQuilt reviews and WaveSpeed's AI page ([Omid Saffari pt-BR](https://omidsaffari.com/pt-br/blog/programa-para-patchwork-prequilt)). No Brazilian fabric maker was found with a simulator of its own ([Magalu](https://m.magazineluiza.com.br/busca/fernando+maluhy/)).

## What only you do, and where you have caught up

**The physics-based 3D drape is still the one feature nobody else has.** Searches found no quilt tool with cloth simulation. PreQuilt and Quiltler show flat layouts, and the nearest rival idea is Swatchit's six AI scenes, which include a bedroom ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/swatchit-quilt-color-design/id6760553382?uo=2)). A generated picture cannot show whether the quilt covers the mattress or how the border hangs. A Quiltingboard user asking for a "quilt visualizer" got only "use EQ" in reply ([Quiltingboard](https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/quilt-visualizer-t311061.html)). For the new model, the drape gains a second job. Once it shows *real* fabrics from a brand or shop, it becomes the best "see it before you buy" picture in the market, which is what shops and manufacturers want.

**Drawing your own block with an automatic cutting list puts you level with EQ8, and makes it easier.** EQ8's EasyDraw needs every line connected to other lines and to the block outline. EQ's own advice is to spend "a lot of time experimenting" first, and its support pages explain why drawings fail ([EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/articles/understanding-easydraw-in-eq8)). A blogger says drawing your own EQ8 blocks "will take time and patience" ([UKQU](https://ukqu.co.uk/?p=302845)). EQ8's cutting chart also leaves out shapes it cannot rotary-cut, such as 30° and 90° kites, which must be printed as templates ([EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=3773)). Your Cells mode (colour squares and halves) and Grid-paper mode (draw on dots every ¼ square) copy the way Brazilian beginners are already told to plan, "on graph paper first, then work out the cuts" ([Decor Fácil](https://www.decorfacil.com/como-fazer-patchwork)). The honest pitch is "EQ8's drawing power without EQ8's rules", and it should be backed by a quick test with real students.

**Three languages are a clear lead.** Only QuiltFit, a small iPad yardage planner, has Portuguese and Spanish screens ([App Store PL](https://apps.apple.com/pl/app/id6776541705)). mySewnet translates web pages and part of one module ([mySewnet es-MX](https://mysewnet.com/es-mx/Quilting/Quilting%20Software)). No full block designer with drawing, cutting lists and templates was found in either language. Third-party reviews of PreQuilt in Portuguese, Spanish, Italian, Korean and Arabic show the demand is there, and that foreign sites already chase "programa para patchwork" searches ([Omid Saffari ES](https://omidsaffari.com/es/blog/programa-de-diseno-de-patchwork-prequilt)).

**Metric plus inches fixes a real complaint.** Every rival found works in inches. NiftyFifty rounds to ⅛ inch, and a UK quilter reports that EQ8's metric mode just relabels a 9-inch block as "9 cm" ([UK Quilters United](https://ukqu.co.uk/?p=312819)). Brazil uses both systems: rulers are sold as 6 × 24 in and as 10 × 45 cm ([Ponto Frio](https://www.pontofrio.com.br/regua-patchwork-6x24-polegadas/p/1541011129), [Ponto Frio](https://www.pontofrio.com.br/regua-patchwork-10x45cm-cristal/p/1541010619)), and local tricoline is 1.40–1.50 m wide ([Magalu](https://m.magazineluiza.com.br/busca/tecido+tricoline+para+patchwork/)). Supporting both, with a 140 or 150 cm width, fits how Brazilians actually cut and buy.

**Symmetry, fussy cutting, the stitch preview and full offline use remain uncommon.** No web or mobile tool was found advertising symmetry editing, fussy cutting or a quilting-stitch preview. PreQuilt's mirror blocks are the nearest thing ([Stitched in Color](https://stitchedincolor.com/blog/2024/7/22/5-tips-to-get-started-with-prequilt)). Working offline is shared with QuiltFit and TideStitch, which lead with "no login" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/bt/app/id6776541705)), so offline is not unique. It is valuable in classrooms with weak wifi.

## Fabric library, login and content depth are the gaps that block the business

**The fabric library is both your main objective and your biggest gap.** EQ8 sets the bar: 6,000–6,200 labelled manufacturer fabrics, plus about 3,500 new printed fabrics a year in packs of 1,100–1,250, plus Moda's free monthly collection ([EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=2614), [EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=1403), [Moda](https://my.modafabrics.com/node/31813)). Only Quiltster also keeps up with new collections, through manufacturer files sent by SKU ([Quiltster](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=10650)). The newer apps avoid the licensing work by using the user's own photos or solid-colour names. Matching EQ8's count at launch is unrealistic. Matching Quiltster's approach (curated current collections from partners), plus solid colour cards from several brands, plus Brazilian mills nobody else has, is realistic, and it is enough to be unique.

**Your own-fabric photos are still hidden.** Until a manufacturer library exists, photos are the only way to design with Brazilian fabric, and eight or more rivals treat them as basic ([QuiltWall](https://humannpc.pages.dev/quiltwall/)). The "+ Add fabric" tile already proposed in the project notes is the cheapest bridge to the fabric objective.

**No real login, payments or live sync means no subscription and no teacher credit.** The new model makes this the first priority, not one task among many. A teacher's share depends on knowing which student belongs to which teacher, and that needs accounts. A subscription needs a payment provider tied to those accounts. The published claude.ai page cannot sync until the API runs on https on the internet.

**Content depth still trails.** 300+ blocks beats PreQuilt (100+), QuiltWall (200+) and EQ Mini (300), but not NiftyFifty (1,200–1,400+, free) or EQ8 (6,700+). Your 1,000+ launch goal would put you in NiftyFifty's range. Appliqué and foundation paper piecing are still missing. Paper piecers design in Illustrator, Inkscape or Procreate today ([r/FPP](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/FPP/comments/1ibf4as/what_program_or_approach_do_you_use_to_create)), and NiftyFifty gives them 196 free patterns. For a paid app, a free rival with more blocks is a pricing problem, not just a features problem.

Fabric fits these systems because shops sell it in **whole units**: a half-yard, a quarter-yard, a 50 cm cut or a fat quarter. Shopify shops usually make the half-yard the unit, so quantity 3 means 1.5 yd ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-can-i-sell-fabric-in-fractional-quantities-like-1-2-or-1-4-yard/280316), [Little Fabric Shop](https://littlefabricshop.com/pages/cutting-info)). Your app must therefore know each shop's unit and round up. For example, 1.3 m of one fabric is 3 units at a 50 cm shop, and 2.84 → 3 units at a half-yard shop. The app should show the extra so nobody feels cheated. Variant IDs change, so they have to be refreshed. Many Shopify shops publish them at `/products.json`, but some block it ([Recharge](https://support.getrecharge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009746154)), and reading it without permission is weak ground for a paid app. Partner feeds are safer.

### Precedents and what affiliate income is worth

Quiltster and eQuilter prove that people will design with real fabrics and then buy. Quiltster lets the user plan a quilt and order the finished plan as a kit. Partners fulfil each order, listings show "Sold by", and one order can include several partners ([Quiltster](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=9996)). Its manufacturer submission rules (72 dpi JPG or PNG, one file per SKU, release dates) are a ready-made template for asking mills for files ([Quiltster](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=10650)). eQuilter works only in its own shop, on its own patterns. Nobody fills carts at *several* independent shops, and nobody covers Brazil.

### Licensing: solids are easy, prints need written permission

Fabric prints are copyrighted artwork, often licensed in from designers. Electric Quilt tells users to "contact the fabric manufacturer" about permission ([EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=1190)), and a blogger notes that manufacturer images in EQ8 are for personal use only ([Meadow Mist Designs](https://meadowmistdesigns.blogspot.com/2020/02/eq8-importing-fabric-series-post-2.html)). No public licence for software was found. How EQ and PreQuilt got their rights is not public either. What can be seen is an industry that hands out images because it wants sales: Moda's JPG zips, Robert Kaufman's mock-up programme, EQ8's SKU-labelled library and PreQuilt's partnership with Suzy Quilts ([Suzy Quilts](https://suzyquilts.com/best-way-to-pick-fabric-for-a-quilt/)). For a paid app, the safe routes are written agreements with each manufacturer or distributor, images supplied by a partner retailer under its own rights, and the user's own photos for personal designs. Solid-colour cards (colour values and names) carry much less risk and can cover many brands at once. Their counts vary between sources: Kona 243–366, Moda Bella 210–473, Art Gallery Pure Solids 135–203 ([Fat Quarter Shop](https://blog.fatquartershop.com/notion-of-month-color-cards/), [Little Fabric Shop](https://littlefabricshop.com/collections/fabric-color-cards.oembed)). The brand and colour names are still trademarks, so use them fairly. A lawyer should confirm the position under Brazil's copyright law (Lei 9.610/98) before any printed images go into a paid product.

**The recommended fabric plan has four layers, in this order.** First come the user's own photos, which need the "+ Add fabric" tile. Second come solid colour cards from Kona, Moda Bella, Art Gallery and Tilda. Third come Brazilian mills (Fernando Maluhy, Peripan, Karsten Ateliê, Fabricart and the digital-print shops), approached directly with an offer of images in exchange for buyers sent to their stockists. Mid-size firms without swatch programmes are more likely to say yes to a person than to a form. Fourth come international printed collections through a partner retailer, with Missouri Star the obvious US target because it reaches Kaufman and most big brands, or through manufacturers who already supply EQ. For the cart, start with a **shopping list grouped by shop**, with each shop's unit applied and ordinary product links, which works with every store. Next add one-click cart links for partner shops on Shopify, VTEX and Nuvemshop. Last, add a Quiltster-style "send my kit request" to a partner shop that cuts and ships, which suits Brazil's 50 cm cut culture and avoids keeping variant IDs current. The 111-place list already holds **25 places with an online store**, the natural first cart partners. Their platforms have not been checked yet.

## The teacher channel is unclaimed, but the cut is a bonus rather than income

### Precedents and percentages

No quilt design tool found pays teachers a share of student subscriptions. Electric Quilt comes closest. It lists independent teachers who fill in a credentials form, gives them teacher-only resources and a 14-day trial for classrooms, and ran a seminar on teaching EQ8 ([EQ](https://classes.electricquilt.com/home/independent-online-eq-classes), [EQ](https://classes.electricquilt.com/class-catalog/how-to-teach-an-eq8-class/)). But it pays them nothing, offers no certification, makes each student buy a US$239.95 licence ([EQ8 class](https://classes.electricquilt.com/class-catalog/eq8-beginner-class)), and sells its own video classes against them ([EQ At Home](https://classes.electricquilt.com/home/eq-at-home-faq)). Sewing-machine brands pay ambassadors in loaned machines and discounts, not cash ([WeAllSew](https://weallsew.com/?p=129546)). Craft video platforms share revenue on content. Creative Spark's standard is 50/50 ([C&T](https://ctpub.com/pages/become-an-instructor)). Craftsy switched instructors to pay by "minutes watched", and all twelve instructors interviewed reported a dramatic drop in income ([Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/tag/rebranding/)). Quilting teachers remember that story, so they will want written, stable terms.

Brazilian teachers already understand the Hotmart pattern. Patchwork courses and a sewing "club" sold as an auto-renewing yearly subscription are on Hotmart today ([Hotmart](https://hotmart.com/pt-br/marketplace/produtos/curso-basico-de-patchwork/W77194113F), [Inaá terms](https://comunidadeinaa.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Termos-e-Condicoes-11.04.2024.pdf)). That cuts both ways. Teachers know what a commission is, but some will compare your share with the 40–70% of course affiliates. **A recurring 25% for as long as the student keeps paying** sits at the generous end of software norms. It is easier to defend if you explain it as "income that keeps coming for every student who stays" and pay it only on money actually received ([Univers Studio terms](https://univers.studio/pt/affiliates/terms/)).

### Taxes and paying teachers (take this to an accountant)

A Brazilian company charging in reais avoids IOF for the customer, which buyers of foreign tools pay at 3.38–3.5% (sources conflict, and a decree phases it to zero by 2028) ([Tecnoblog](https://tecnoblog.net/noticias/governo-cria-decreto-para-zerar-iof-do-cartao-ate-2028-veja-calendario/)). A Brazilian company is also the natural route for splits to Brazilian teachers. Software as a service pays **ISS** at 2–5% depending on the city (São Paulo 2.9%) after the STF decision ([TaxUp](https://taxup.com.br/iss-software-saas-municipio-codigo-retencao/)). Every sale needs an NFS-e, and in 2026, a test year of the tax reform, the invoices must carry the new CBS (0.9%) and IBS (0.1%) fields ([Contábeis](https://www.contabeis.com.br/noticias/73889/reforma-tributaria-2026-veja-o-que-muda-na-fase-de-transicao/)). For teachers there are two paths. A teacher with an MEI issues a nota fiscal for her share, within the R$81,000 a year MEI limit ([Contabilidade.com](https://contabilidade.com/blog/mei-em-2026-o-que-e-quem-pode-limite-impostos-e-quando-vale-a-pena)). A teacher without a CNPJ is paid by **RPA**, and the payer withholds INSS (11% cited), IRRF and possibly ISS ([Conta Azul](https://contaazul.com/blog/o-que-e-rpa-como-calcular-e-emitir/)). Sending the share through a provider's split does not by itself settle which path applies. Whether a commission counts as "intermediação de negócios", and which MEI occupation fits, were not answered by any source. That is a question for an accountant before launch.

### Unit economics, illustrated

The figures below are illustrations built from the fees above, not forecasts. Take a monthly plan at **R$24.90** paid by card through Asaas. The fee is about R$1.23, leaving R$23.67. A 25% teacher share of that net amount is about **R$5.92**. ISS at 2.9% of the price is about R$0.72. That leaves roughly **R$17** a month for you before income tax and running costs. A yearly plan at **R$179.90** has a fee of about R$5.87, a teacher share of about R$43.50, and ISS of about R$5.20, leaving about R$125 for the year (about R$10.40 a month). Yearly plans cut fixed fees and churn, but they lower monthly income. Ordinary Pix at a flat R$1.99 costs 8% of a R$24.90 charge, which is another reason to prefer Pix Automático or yearly plans.

The channel's shape comes from class data. A cooperative atelier runs weekly 2-hour classes by level, with 7–10 students per class, at R$150–165 a month ([Sicoob Cressem](https://www.sicoob.com.br/web/cressem/cursos-oficinas)). Private lessons average about R$40 an hour ([Superprof](https://www.superprof.com.br/aulas/patchwork/presidente-prudente/)). Senac's course runs 160 hours ([Senac SP](https://www.sp.senac.br/cursos-livres/curso-de-confeccionador-de-patchwork)). A teacher with three level-classes has 20–30 students. If 30–50% subscribe (an assumption to test), that is about 5–15 paying students. At R$5.92 each, 10 students give the teacher about **R$59 a month**, and 30 give about R$178, roughly one student's class fee. That is a nice bonus, not a reason to change habits. The pitch must lead with what saves the teacher work: free software, sending a block to the whole class, printed templates and cutting lists for handouts, and a class view of students' designs.

**The starting target list holds 111 places** (`research/ateliers/ateliers.xlsx`). The data comes from search snippets: 80 are ateliers or schools, 53 also sell fabric, 25 have an online store and 11 are groups. 86 are in the South and Southeast (41 in São Paulo alone). Only 43 are confirmed active, 3 may be closed, and 45 are low-confidence entries, so expect to clean it. Suppose 80 teaching places average 10 paying students each. That is 800 subscribers, about R$19,900 a month gross at R$24.90, or roughly **R$13,600 a month after fees, the teacher's share and ISS**, before income tax and costs. That is enough to show the model works, not enough to live on. Growth would have to come from Senac units, cooperatives, festivals (Floripa Quilt, Brazil Patchwork Show, Limeira) and fabric shops ([NSC Total](https://www.nsctotal.com.br/?p=6305889), [Diário do Litoral](https://www.diariodolitoral.com.br/cotidiano/interior-de-sp-recebe-uma-das-maiores-feira-de-artesanato-textil-do/)). Students who come in through shops and cart links count too.

### Risks in the channel

**Churn after the course is the main risk.** Lifetime payout is roughly price × share ÷ monthly churn ([Refgrow](https://refgrow.com/blog/saas-affiliate-program-mistakes)). With 10% monthly churn, the average student stays ten months, so the teacher earns about R$59 and you about R$170 per student. Atelier classes are ongoing monthly memberships ("mensalidade"), which suits a subscription better than a one-off course. Your saved designs, "My blocks", the fabric library and the cart give students reasons to stay after class. **Trust is the second risk.** After Craftsy, teachers will want a dashboard of their students and payments, and terms that cannot change overnight. **Small payouts cause departures.** Neighbourhood gyms left Wellhub because their payment per visit felt small next to their own fees ([Correio 24 Horas](https://www.correio24horas.com.br/salvador/fim-do-gympass-academias-de-bairro-rompem-com-plataformas-e-tendencia-cresce-no-pais-0226)). **Sign-up friction is real.** Opening a payment subaccount means identity checks, a hurdle for non-technical teachers. Paying monthly by Pix against an MEI nota or RPA is simpler at first. **Channel conflict needs rules.** Decide up front who gets credit when a student signs up alone and later joins a class. Hotmart's order (link, then cookie, then coupon, then history) is a model, and a **teacher coupon code** is the easiest thing to explain in class ([Hotmart](https://help.hotmart.com/pt-br/article/36806399753485/quais-sao-os-criterios-de-priorizacao-na-atribuicao-de-comissao-para-afiliados-na-hotmart-)). Free public classes, such as Curitiba's Liceu, also compete for beginners ([Prefeitura de Curitiba](https://www.curitiba.pr.gov.br/noticias/patchwork-croche-e-scrapbooking-estao-nas-oficinas-gratuitas-do-liceu-de-oficios-criativos/79722)).

Canva Pro is the closest comparison: a creative tool people already know and pay for. A niche tool for hobby quilters, many of them older and careful with money, should sit a little below it. A **class price ("preço de turma")**, like Spotify's University plan at about half price, would make the teacher's recommendation feel like a perk. Pricing guides suggest Brazil prices 20–56% below US list ([Fungies](https://fungies.io/purchasing-power-parity-saas-pricing-2026)), which supports pricing well under the US$50–100 a year that foreign tools charge. No Brazilian sewing or quilt app with a subscription priced in reais was found, so there is no local anchor, and the price must be tested with pilot ateliers. Offer card, Pix Automático and boleto. Card instalments matter for yearly plans, since instalment card use is still the most-used method in one survey ([E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/pix-ou-cartao-dados-mostram-como-o-brasileiro-paga-no-comercio-eletronico)). Keep teachers free for good, since their free account is the whole channel.

## Ease of use is an open position, especially for quilters over 60

The core quilter is a retired woman averaging **67 years old** who prefers her local shop ([Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)). The research found **no quilt design software marketed with large text, a simple mode or features for older users** ([Quilting Daily](https://www.quiltingdaily.com/workshop-wednesday-easy-quilt-design-software-mccalls-quilting-blog)). EQ's only product made for "beginning computer users", the Quilt Design Wizard, is no longer sold ([EQ shop](https://electricquilt.com/online-shop/quilt-design-wizard)). EQ8 users describe a "huge learning curve", although others say it "isn't brain surgery" ([Quiltingboard](https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/eq8-not-t306667-2.html)). EQ8's learning help is mostly outside the program and paid: the *EQ8 Lessons for Beginners* book, EQ classes and AQS workshops ([Searchpress](https://searchpress.com/book/9781893824928/eq8-lessons-for-beginners), [American Quilter](https://preview.americanquilter.com/design-your-first-quilt-in-eq8)). PreQuilt is "easy to use once you get the hang of it", and its makers wrote guides and a YouTube course for the learning curve ([sewingmachinefun](https://www.sewingmachinefun.com/free-quilt-design-software/)). Quilt Wizard's interface is "not remotely intuitive" ([AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/quilt-wizard/475622761?country=us)), and Quiltler 2 drew "confusing" complaints. The newer apps (Swatchit, QuiltFit) have too few ratings to judge, and no 2025–2026 usability comparison exists.

Your app has the ingredients to claim this ground: a guided tour, a Cells drawing mode that needs no geometry, an undo timeline, screens in the student's own language, and cutting lists in the units her rulers use. The teacher channel itself is an ease-of-use feature, because a person shows the student how, in class, as EQ's teacher network does for EQ8 but in English only. The claim still needs evidence. Watch five or ten students over 60 in a pilot atelier try three tasks: colour a quilt, draw a simple block, print the cutting list. Fix what trips them up. Consider a larger text setting and bigger tap targets for tablets. Until then, say "made to be easy" rather than "easiest".

## Conclusion

The business model turns the app's main weakness into its strategy. The earlier question was whether a small app could out-feature EQ8. The new question is whether it can own the path from "my teacher showed me this block" to "the fabric is in my cart". On that path nobody is in the way in Brazil. No rival pays teachers, shows Brazilian fabric or fills a local shop's cart, and the one Portuguese-language design app is a small iPad planner. Brazil's own commerce makes the path short: Pix Automático lets an older student pay without a card, shops sell in 50 cm units that convert neatly, and VTEX and Nuvemshop shops take one link per cart. The value moves away from the software alone and towards relationships: with teachers who bring students, with mills who allow their prints, and with shops who fill the order. Those relationships are slow to build and hard to copy.

The numbers also show what the model cannot do on its own. A teacher's share of a few reais per student will not move anyone, affiliate income per quilt is small, and 80 ateliers alone add up to a modest business. The model works if the app saves teachers real work, keeps students after class, and spreads beyond the first list through shops and festivals. That makes the cheapest next move a pilot, not more code. A handful of ateliers, a coupon code and a price in reais will show, within weeks, whether students pay and teachers recommend. That evidence should come before building payments, splits and cart adapters.
