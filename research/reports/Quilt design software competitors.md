# Draping, metric and Portuguese set your app apart

Of the roughly 70 quilt design products, add-ons and quilting apps found, none does all of what your app does: a 3D view that drapes the quilt on a bed or table with cloth physics, a cutting guide and real-size A4 templates in millimetres, symmetry editing, fussy cutting and a quilting-stitch preview, free, offline, from one HTML file. The 3D drape is the one feature no competitor was found to have at all. The closest thing is Swatchit's AI "room scene" pictures, which look nice but are not true to size or to how fabric hangs ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/swatchit-quilt-color-design/id6760553382?uo=2)). Your app is behind on content and reach. It has 27 blocks against EQ8's 6,700+ ([EQ](https://electricquilt.com/landing-pages/eq8)) and NiftyFifty's free 1,400+ ([NiftyFifty](https://niftyfiftyquilting.com/quilt-designer)). It has no drawing of your own blocks, no appliqué, no foundation paper piecing, no manufacturer fabrics, no login or sync live yet, and screens in English only. Its own-fabric photo feature is built, but people can't reach it. The market leader, EQ8, costs about US$240 and is a 2017 desktop program that people describe as hard to learn ([Quiltingboard](https://www.quiltingboard.com/showthread.php?p=8296666)). Since 2025 a wave of cheap web and iPad tools has arrived, mostly charging about US$50 a year, and they are fighting for Google rankings. The biggest open space is Brazil. This research found no quilt designer made in Brazil and no browser designer with Portuguese screens. The only Portuguese-language design app found is a brand-new iPad subscription app (QuiltFit). Brazil has a busy circuit of patchwork shows, local mills sell fabric by the metre at 1.40 m wide, and beginners are still told to plan on graph paper. **The recommended position is "see your quilt on the bed before you cut, with cutting plans in centimetres, in Portuguese, free".** The three things to do first are to make the "Add fabric" button reachable, translate the screens into Portuguese and go live with a real login. One warning applies to everything below. All findings come from search-result text, because the research tools could not open web pages. Every price, rating and feature claim should be checked on the vendor's own page before you repeat it publicly.

## How far to trust this research: search snippets, not opened pages

Five research notes fed this report: desktop software, web tools, mobile apps, the Brazilian market and the wider market. In every case the tool for opening web pages was blocked, so each finding is a line of text that a search engine showed, along with the address it came from. That has three consequences. First, **prices and ratings are snapshots from retailers, app trackers and third-party reviews**, and several conflict. Quiltler's yearly price appears as both US$49.99 and US$29.99 ([Quiltler](https://quiltler.com/quilt-design-software-free)), and PreQuilt's prices come from one blog review ([Omid Saffari](https://omidsaffari.com/ko/blog/prequilt-review-for-fabric-color-planning-ko)). Second, **"not found" means the snippets didn't mention it, not that it doesn't exist**. For example, EQ8 almost certainly does sashing, but no snippet said so. Third, many "best quilt software 2026" pages (Gitnux, WifiTalents, Worldmetrics and others) look automatically generated or paid for by affiliates. They are not used as evidence here. NiftyFifty and Quiltler publish comparison pages about their own competitors, so treat their claims as marketing. No competitor was tried hands-on, so nothing here measures how easy a product is to use or how good its PDFs are.

The facts about your own app come from the brief you supplied, which was checked against the code. One point was checked in the code for this report: the cutting guide gives fabric to buy in metres, rounded up to 5 cm, for a fabric width of 110, 140 or 150 cm (150 cm is the default). It also has a seam allowance setting (7.5 mm default) and works out backing widths and binding strips (`app/src/cutting/plan.js`, `app/quilt.html`).

## About 70 products found, but only a dozen compete with you directly

The field splits into six groups. Desktop is one serious product (EQ8) plus brand-tied, frozen or dead programs. On the web, a crowded set of mostly young freemium tools has appeared since 2025. Mobile is small, iPad-heavy and has no clear leader. Utilities are calculators and stash apps, and that is where quilters actually spend their app time. Longarm and embroidery software is expensive, tied to machines, and complements a piecing designer rather than replacing it. Brazil has essentially nothing local. ## Five things no competitor was found to do

**The physics-based 3D drape is the headline.** Searches for "3D quilt preview", "quilt visualization on bed" and "virtual quilt mockup" found no quilt tool with cloth simulation. PreQuilt and Quiltler show flat layouts ([Quiltler](https://quiltler.com/best-quilt-design-apps)). The nearest alternatives are aimed at businesses (Style3D's bed-set simulation for textile brands ([Style3D](https://www.style3d.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/15927)), Skopos's furniture-fabric tool ([Skopos](https://www.skoposfabrics.com/news/new-3d-tool-for-easy-visualisation))) or are static Photoshop bedding mockups sold to pattern sellers ([Gumroad](https://fieldoffice.gumroad.com/l/quiltblanketmockup)). Quilters do ask for this: a Quiltingboard thread titled "Quilt visualizer?" got only "use EQ" in reply ([Quiltingboard](https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/quilt-visualizer-t311061.html)). An old iPad review complained that a small on-screen quilt is hard to judge against the real thing ([Melanie Testa](https://melanietesta.com/2015/01/07/designing-we-will-go/)). That is exactly what a drape on a full-size bed answers. The strongest rival idea is Swatchit's six AI scenes, which include a bedroom ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/swatchit-quilt-color-design/id6760553382?uo=2)). They are generated pictures, so the size of the quilt on the bed and the way it falls over the edge are not real. Your drape shows whether a quilt actually covers the mattress and how the border hangs, which a picture cannot.

**Real-size templates and cutting in millimetres are the second.** Every competitor found works in inches: NiftyFifty rounds to ⅛ inch ([NiftyFifty](https://niftyfiftyquilting.com/pattern-designer)) and Swatchit gives "quilter-friendly fractions" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6760553382)). A UK quilter reports that EQ8's metric mode just relabels a 9-inch block as "9 cm" instead of converting it ([UK Quilters United](https://ukqu.co.uk/?p=312819)). Only QuiltPaper (graph paper, not a block designer) handles cm and mm ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1139385294)). Your cutting guide buys fabric in metres for 110, 140 or 150 cm widths, and that 140 cm option matches the 1.40 m tricoline sold in Brazilian shops ([Magazine Luiza](https://www.magazineluiza.com.br/busca/tecido+patchwork+poa+cinza/)). Printing on A4 rather than US Letter matters for every country outside North America.

**Symmetry editing, fussy cutting and the quilting-stitch preview together are the third.** No web or mobile tool was found advertising any of the three. The only stitch preview found elsewhere is the Quilting Mill's tool, which lays a longarm pattern over an uploaded photo ([Quilting Mill](https://thequiltingmill.com/pages/preview-tool)). Separate stitch-design programs cost US$400–1,000 ([Sewing Machines Plus](https://sewingmachinesplus.com/products/hq-20807-art-n-stitch-software)). EQ8 has motif tools, which reviewers call clumsy ([Quilting Wemple](https://quiltingwemple.com/design-a-quilt-eq8/)). EQ8 may also do fussy cutting, since one forum user said a rival lacked it "compared with EQ8" ([Linda's](https://lindas.com/blogs/product-guides/quilting-design-software-eq8-floriani-guide)), but no source confirmed it.

**The whole app working offline, free, from a single file is the fourth.** No browser competitor was found that works fully offline. EQ Mini and mySewnet need internet to activate or run ([EQ shop](https://electricquilt.com/online-shop/eq-mini), [mySewnet](https://mysewnet.com/es-es/Quilting/Quilting-Software)). Your app runs on Chromebooks, Linux, Windows, Mac and tablets without installing anything. That matters because "no iPad, phone or Chromebook version" ranks among the top three complaints about EQ8 ([NiftyFifty](https://niftyfiftyquilting.com/quilt-design-software/eq8-alternatives), a competitor's page). Be careful with the "privacy, no account" pitch, though. QuiltFit and TideStitch already lead with "no login, on device" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/bt/app/id6776541705), [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/app/apple/6785949038/)), so it isn't unique, only uncommon among browser tools.

**Free webcam capture of fabric is the fifth, but only once people can reach it.** Taking fabric photos is now standard: Quiltography, Quilt Wizard, Quiltler, Quilt Duck, TideStitch, Quilter's App, Quilt Cat and QuiltWall all do it. Free tiers usually ration it: Quiltler allows 10 photos ([Quiltler](https://quiltler.com/)), Quilt Duck 4 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6755566364)) and Quilt Cat 5 ([Patchwork Posse](https://www.patchworkposse.com/sewing-quilting-apps/)). Unlimited free photos from a file or the computer's camera would be a real advantage. Today it doesn't count, because the buttons sit in hidden sections.

## Library size, hidden fabric photos and no login leave you behind

**Content depth is the widest gap.** 27 blocks compare with 100+ in old iPad apps ([Quilt Wizard](https://apps.apple.com/app/id475622761)), 200+ in QuiltWall and Quilt Pattern Creator ([QuiltWall](https://humannpc.pages.dev/quiltwall/)), 1,400+ free at NiftyFifty and 6,700+ in EQ8. Editable parts soften this, because one block can make many variations, but people browsing a block list judge by the count. EQ8's moat is 30 years of depth: drawing modes, appliqué, foundation-piecing printouts and about 6,200 manufacturer fabrics ([Lady Sew and Sew](https://ladysewandsew.co.uk/?p=724029)). Your app has none of the last four. Foundation paper piecing is a popular niche with its own community (r/FPP), whose members currently design in Illustrator, Inkscape or Procreate ([r/FPP mirror](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/FPP/comments/1ibf4as/what_program_or_approach_do_you_use_to_create)). NiftyFifty serves them for free.

**Your fabric photos are hidden, and that hurts more than it looks.** Using your own fabric is a baseline feature in at least eight competitors and a long-standing pain in EQ8: EQ sells a separate "Deep Dive: Fabrics, Panels and Photos" book because scanning, scaling and cropping are fiddly ([EQ](https://electricquilt.com/online-shop/eq8-deep-dive-fabrics-panels-and-photos)). For Brazilian quilters it matters doubly. Local fabrics from Fernando Maluhy, Karsten or Peripan are not in any US fabric library ([Magazine Luiza](https://m.magazineluiza.com.br/busca/fernando+maluhy/)), so their own photos are the only way to design with the fabric they will actually buy. A reviewer also points out that photo colour and lighting mislead people ([appfollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/quilt-wizard/475622761?country=us)). Some simple help with brightness, or a "photograph on a white sheet in daylight" hint, would answer a known complaint.

**No accounts, sharing or sync are live, and that blocks the rest.** Sync is now normal: Quiltler syncs iPhone and iPad, Swatchit syncs phone and web, QuiltWall uses Google sign-in ([Swatchit](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6760553382), [QuiltWall](https://humannpc.pages.dev/quiltwall/)). Swatchit adds share links so a guild can comment. Your sync server is built but not deployed and has only a development login. Until it is live on https, the published claude.ai page cannot sync at all. No source showed quilters explicitly asking for sync or collaboration, so this gap is inferred from what competitors offer, not from complaints.

**Reach is the final gap: English screens, millimetres only, no app store listing.** Brazil uses both systems. Rulers are sold in inches (6 × 24 in) and in centimetres (10 × 45 cm) ([Ponto Frio](https://www.pontofrio.com.br/regua-patchwork-6x24-polegadas/p/1541011129), [Ponto Frio](https://www.pontofrio.com.br/regua-patchwork-10x45cm-cristal/p/1541010619)), and a buying guide warns about rounding errors when you mix them ([Busca Melhores](https://buscamelhores.com.br/qual-a-melhor-regua-para-patchwork)). Even the Portuguese PreQuilt review tests a "20 polegadas" quilt ([Omid Saffari](https://omidsaffari.com/pt-br/blog/programa-para-patchwork-prequilt)). Millimetres only is a strength for local patterns but shuts out people following US patterns. In the US, the biggest market, it is a clear weakness. Without a store listing, discovery depends on search and word of mouth. That is where Quiltler and NiftyFifty already compete hard, and the 2026 industry survey says social media is now the main way all generations discover quilting ([Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)).

## NiftyFifty, Quiltler and QuiltFit are the threats to watch

**NiftyFifty is the most direct threat.** It is free and runs in the browser. It already offers the cutting-chart-plus-true-size-template combination you thought was rare, along with 1,400+ blocks, appliqué, 196 foundation-piecing patterns and block tutorials with cutting charts for any size ([NiftyFifty](https://niftyfiftyquilting.com/pattern-designer), [FPP](https://niftyfiftyquilting.com/fpp-patterns)). It publishes "honest comparison" and "EQ8 alternatives" pages for 2026, so it is investing in search ranking ([NiftyFifty](https://niftyfiftyquilting.com/quilt-design-software)). Its weaknesses as found: inch-only, English-only, a flat preview, and no known way it makes money. If NiftyFifty added metric and Portuguese, your app's practical advantages would shrink to the 3D drape, symmetry, fussy cutting and stitch preview.

**Quiltler is the best-run commercial rival.** It has a browser designer that needs no account to start, iOS apps, phone fabric photos placed at real scale, separate planners for borders and layer cakes, a paid tier for designers and an embeddable designer that it sells to fabric companies ([Quiltler](https://quiltler.com/fabric-companies)). It writes comparison pages against PreQuilt and "8 free tools" to capture searches ([Quiltler](https://quiltler.com/quiltler-vs-prequilt)). Its Portuguese-language visibility already exists indirectly: a Portuguese review calls it "the strongest free option for saving" ([Omid Saffari](https://omidsaffari.com/pt-br/blog/programa-para-patchwork-prequilt)). Its business-to-business embed shows one way to make money that suits you as well: fabric shops putting a designer on their own websites.

**QuiltFit is small but aimed at your exact pitch.** It is offline, needs no login, has Portuguese and Spanish screens, a cut list, sewing steps and a shopping list, on iPad ([App Store PL](https://apps.apple.com/pl/app/id6776541705)). With about five ratings it has no traction yet ([appshunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/quiltfit-quilt-design-planner/id6776541705/reviews)). It is the only competitor found that a Brazilian could use today in Portuguese. It is iPad-only and its useful parts need a €9.99 monthly subscription, which is expensive in Brazil.

**Swatchit and AI images threaten the 3D advantage from below.** If quilters decide an AI bedroom picture is "good enough", the cloth physics becomes a technical feat few people value. The answer is to show what AI can't: the quilt at true size on a real mattress size, the drop over the edges, and how the border falls. A side-by-side "will it fit my bed?" demo makes that point better than the word "physics".

**Weaker threats.** PreQuilt has the brand and the manufacturer swatches, but it charges US$50 a year for saving and yardage ([Omid Saffari](https://omidsaffari.com/ko/blog/prequilt-review-for-fabric-color-planning-ko)). EQ could launch a cloud or tablet version. No sign of an EQ9 or a browser edition turned up, and the company's 2026 news was a starter box and an add-on ([Keepsake Quilting](https://www.keepsakequilting.com/products/eq-for-you-starter-box)). A third-party site says EQ8 relies on Apple's Rosetta translation layer on newer Macs, which Apple is phasing out. If true, EQ8 users on Mac may need an alternative ([RosettaCheck](https://rosettacheck.com/apps/nwfxqxma), unverified). The last threat is history itself. Threadbias, PatternJam, MyWebQuilter, Quilt-Pro, Quiltography and Quilt Wizard all stalled or died. Small quilt tools struggle to last, so keeping running costs low (an app that works offline, with modest sync hosting) is a defence in its own right.

## Brazil has shows, mills and teachers, but no Portuguese designer

**The space is empty.** Portuguese and Spanish searches found no quilt design program made in Brazil, Portugal or Latin America. The Portuguese-language results were mySewnet's product page (a sewing-machine brand's desktop software; whether the screens are translated is unconfirmed), WaveSpeed's square-grid photo generator ("borders are not included; plan them separately"), and foreign review sites ([mySewnet pt-BR](https://mysewnet.com/pt-br/Quilting/Quilting-Software), [WaveSpeed pt](https://wavespeed.ai/pt/free-tools/quilt-pattern-generator)). No Portuguese EQ8 course, Brazilian EQ8 teacher or Mercado Livre listing for EQ8 was found. EQ training exists only in English, for example a US$49.99 CTPub course ([CTPub](https://creativespark.ctpub.com/courses/eq8-software-basics)). A Portuguese beginner's guide tells people to "draw the project on graph paper first, then work out the cuts" ([Decor Fácil](https://www.decorfacil.com/como-fazer-patchwork)). That is precisely the job your cutting guide and A4 templates automate.

**The community is real and gathers in person.** Brazil Patchwork Show in São Paulo has run since about 2007 and was expected again in April 2026 ([Neventum](https://neventum.com/tradeshows/brazil-patchwork-show)). Floripa Quilt held its 13th edition on 22–25 April 2026 with free entry, a beginners' quilt contest and workshops ([NSC Total](https://www.nsctotal.com.br/?p=6305889)). Limeira Patchwork's 14th edition in Americana/SP had **74 exhibitors from 35 cities and 80 coach groups** and calls itself the country's largest textile craft fair ([Diário do Litoral](https://www.diariodolitoral.com.br/cotidiano/interior-de-sp-recebe-uma-das-maiores-feira-de-artesanato-textil-do/)). QuiltBrasil in Gramado reached 28 editions, but sources conflict on whether it paused in 2025 ([Carolina Oneto](https://carolinaoneto.com/list-of-quilt-shows-in-2025-in-europe-australia-asia-and-africa-south-america/)). There are national mills (Fernando Maluhy, founded 1966; Karsten Ateliê) ([ConnectAmericas](https://connectamericas.com/pt/company/fernando-maluhy-cia-ltda)), several patchwork magazines ([PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/pt-br/magazines/m/patchwork/issues)), and well-known teachers such as BERNINA ambassador Estela Mota ([BVV Brno](https://www.bvv.cz/en/bpm/news/gallerist-estela-mota-from-brazil-to-the-brno-patchwork-meeting)) and Janome maker Carolina Oneto, who blogged about designing quilts with software ([carolinaoneto.com](https://carolinaoneto.com/2024/05)). Teaching is about sewing technique: Senac's 160-hour course, Hotmart, eduK ([Senac SP](https://www.sp.senac.br/cursos-livres/curso-de-confeccionador-de-patchwork)). No digital design course was found, and that is an opening for teachers to adopt a free tool for their handouts.

**Paying for foreign tools is awkward.** Competitors price only in US dollars. Brazilians pay the dollar price at the day's rate plus **3.5% IOF**, a tax on international card purchases that has been at that single rate since July 2025 ([InvestNews](https://investnews.com.br/economia/fechou-o-cerco-iof-vai-a-35-para-cambio-com-cartoes-de-credito-debito-e-pre-pagos-internacionais/amp/)), plus the card's margin. For scale, half a metre of local patchwork cotton costs about R$30–39 ([Magazine Luiza](https://m.magazineluiza.com.br/busca/fernando+maluhy/)), so EQ8's US$240 equals dozens of fabric cuts. No competitor mentioned Pix, boleto or payment in instalments, which are the normal ways Brazilians pay.

**What the evidence doesn't tell you.** No numbers turned up on how many Brazilians quilt, how many visitors the shows attract, how big teachers' audiences are, or whether Brazilian quilters design on phones or computers. That last question matters for a 3D browser app. Brazilian patchwork also leans towards bags, cushions and rag dolls made to sell ("artesanato lucrativo") ([Hotmart](https://hotmart.com/pt-br/marketplace/produtos/curso-basico-de-patchwork/W77194113F), [Bem Paraná](https://www.bemparana.com.br/noticia/oficina-gratuita-ensinara-curitibanos-a-fazer-bonecas-de-pano)). Your bed-quilt focus fits the quilt-show crowd better than the accessory crowd. Every conclusion about Brazilian demand rests on the absence of competitors, not on Brazilian quilters saying they want this. A handful of conversations at a show or in a teacher's class would test it cheaply. Spanish (Latin American) screens would add Argentina, Mexico and Colombia, where only mySewnet has Spanish pages ([mySewnet es-mx](https://mysewnet.com/es-mx/Quilting/Quilting%20Software)).

The pattern is clear. **The going rate for a hobby web designer is about US$50 a year**, and the usual paywalls are saving, fabric photos and cutting instructions, which are exactly the features your app gives away. Quilters also pay for learning: EQ sells lesson books, deep-dive books and classes at shows ([EQ classes](https://classes.electricquilt.com/home/eq-classes-at-quilt-shows)). Two more money-makers sit alongside the subscriptions. Tiers for pattern designers (PreQuilt Market) and embeds for fabric companies (Quiltler) charge businesses rather than hobbyists. And pattern sellers earn real money: one survey of 106 quilt-pattern sellers averaged US$15,229 over 12 months ([Payhip](https://payhip.com/sell-patterns-online)), and they buy Photoshop bed mockups for their listings. No quilting-specific evidence of subscription fatigue was found, and nobody has published conversion rates.

For you, this suggests keeping the core free, since it is already your biggest pull against PreQuilt and Quiltler. If you ever charge, charge for what costs you money to run: sync and photo storage. Price it in reais, payable by Pix, card in instalments or Mercado Pago, at well under the US$50 benchmark. A second option is a teacher or designer tier: bulk handouts, a shop's own fabric list, or high-resolution "on the bed" images from the 3D view for pattern listings. Nothing here proves Brazilians would pay. Free with a voluntary "support the project" option is a defensible choice for a project with low running costs.

## Position it as "see it on your bed before you cut", in Portuguese

**Recommended positioning.** In Brazil, aim to be the free quilt designer that speaks Portuguese, measures in centimetres, prints templates on A4, and shows the quilt on your bed before you cut a single piece. Elsewhere, present it as the easy, free alternative for people who find EQ8 too expensive or too slow to learn and who want to see the finished quilt for real. Do not pitch it as an EQ8 replacement for professional pattern designers, and do not compete on number of blocks. The 2026 survey describes the core buyer as a retired woman averaging 67 who prefers her local shop ([Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)). 61% prefer local shops ([Craft Industry Alliance 2024](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2024/)), and 18% of 2025 respondents were new quilters ([Craft Industry Alliance 2025](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/)). Both groups point the same way: a simple tool that teachers and shops recommend, with a guided tour rather than a manual.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "can a 27-block app compete with EQ8?" to "who owns the moment just before cutting?". On that ground, no competitor was found to match your combination of a true-size 3D drape, metric cutting plans and real-size A4 templates, free and offline. The contest is no longer about who has the most blocks. NiftyFifty gives 1,400 away and AI tools generate pictures on demand. It is about trust that the quilt will look and fit as planned, and that the fabric bought will be enough. Brazil turns that advantage into a market rather than just a feature. Nobody serves it in Portuguese and in centimetres with free cutting plans, and its quilters still plan on graph paper.

The risks are just as specific. The advantage is easy to lose to a well-funded rival adding metric and Portuguese, to "good enough" AI room pictures, or to running out of steam, as most small quilt tools before you did. That argues for moving soon on the cheap, visible steps (the fabric button, Portuguese, a share link and a demo video), keeping running costs low, and testing the Brazilian demand with real quilters before deciding whether the app should ever charge.
