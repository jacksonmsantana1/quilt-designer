# Creator (YouTuber) revenue-share partnerships for a paid quilt design app, with a Brazil focus

Research notes, 2026-11-02. Method note: WebFetch was blocked by the network policy (www.conar.org.br failed to resolve, so no other sites were fetched). Every finding below comes from search-result text and the URLs the search tool showed. Many benchmark figures come from vendor blogs (affiliate-software companies) and are flagged as such. About 48 searches were used.

App context assumed: browser quilt designer in PT/ES/EN, about R$19.90–24.90/month or R$149–199/year. Teachers/ateliers get it free and receive part of their students' subscriptions. The owner now wants YouTubers on a similar revenue share.

---

## 1. Precedents in quilting, sewing, crafts and creative SaaS

### Takeaway
Quilting and sewing companies mostly use **low-rate retail affiliate links** (6–10%, short cookies) and **non-cash ambassador programs** (machine loans, discounts, visibility). Quilt software (Electric Quilt) does **paid sponsorships of individual quilting YouTubers** plus discount-code landing pages. The closest analogues to "teacher gets a share of student subscriptions" come from creative-learning platforms: **Skillshare pays teachers 60% of referred members' net payments**, and **Domestika pays affiliates 40% of the first purchase and 20% of recurring purchases**. I found no documented creator programs from sewing-machine or fabric brands in Brazil.

Minimum withdrawal R$100. — [Nuvemshop commission rules](https://atendimento.nuvemshop.com.br/regras-gerais-de-comissionamento-dos-parceiros); [Nuvemshop associates FAQ](https://atendimento.nuvemshop.com.br/pt_BR/programadeassociados/perguntas-frequentes-sobre-o-programa-de-parceiros-associados); [Nuvemshop partners blog](https://www.nuvemshop.com.br/blog/parceiros-nuvemshop/)

### Inferences
- The niche splits in two. Physical-goods quilting retailers pay small percentages (6–10%) because margins are thin. Digital and subscription products (Skillshare, Domestika, SaaS) can afford 20–60% because each extra subscriber costs almost nothing to serve. A quilt app sits in the second group.
- Nuvemshop's two tiers (open affiliates paid on early payments only; vetted partners on lifetime recurring) maps directly onto "YouTubers versus teachers". It is a Brazilian precedent for giving the committed partner (teacher) a better, longer share than the broadcast partner (YouTuber).
- Electric Quilt, the market leader, uses both paid sponsored videos with quilting YouTubers and discount-code landing pages. That points to a hybrid (small flat fee plus code) rather than pure revenue share.
- BERNINA's program shows that in sewing, **status, early access and free product** motivate creators strongly, beyond cash. A free "Pro" account and a "Criadora parceira" badge cost the app little.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Electric Quilt's official affiliate rate, its sponsorship fees, or whether it runs a formal creator program.
- No Brazilian sewing-machine brand (Janome, Singer, Brother, Elgin) or patchwork fabric brand showed a documented creator or affiliate program. Brazilian deals are probably informal (free product plus #publi), but I found no source.
- I could not find a ranked list of Brazilian patchwork/quilting YouTube channels with subscriber counts. Searches only returned general Brazilian YouTube rankings. The owner will need to build a prospect list by hand (YouTube search, Social Blade, vidIQ).
- Missouri Star's actual affiliate rate was not found.

## 2. Typical deal terms and economics (percent of subscription, caps, flat fees, CPM, BRL pricing, coupons, hybrids)

### Takeaway
SaaS affiliate programs cluster around **20–30% recurring** (Rewardful average about 24%; one dataset's median is 20%). Recurring commissions are often **capped at 12 months** (sometimes 12–24). Creators with real audiences usually expect a **hybrid**: a base fee plus about 10–15% commission, or a reduced flat fee plus revenue share. In Brazil, most creators are small (78% have 50k followers or fewer), few live from campaigns, and published BRL price ranges are too broad to be useful (R$500–50,000 per post). Pricing is increasingly done by views (CPM), not followers.

### Cited Findings
**Percentages and duration (vendor benchmarks)**
- Average SaaS affiliate commission is about 24.16%, from anonymised Rewardful program data. A dataset of 121 SaaS campaigns (LinkJolt) gives a median of 20% and a mean of 22.9%. — [Rewardful: SaaS affiliate program benchmarks](https://www.rewardful.com/articles/saas-affiliate-program-benchmarks); [Track360 2026 benchmark](https://www.track360.io/blog/saas-affiliate-commission-rates-benchmark-2026)
- A typical range is 20–30% recurring. A QualityUnit 2025 guide says 15–30%. — [QualityUnit: SaaS commission rates](https://cdn.qualityunit.com/blog/saas-affiliate-commission-rates); [Refgrow 2026 benchmarks](https://refgrow.com/blog/affiliate-program-benchmarks-2026)
- 71% of SaaS affiliate programs pay recurring commissions. Recurring commissions are "typically capped at 12 months"; another benchmark says 12–24 months. Performance tiers are common, for example 15% recurring for 1–10 customers, 20% for 11–25, and 25% for 26+. — [Hamster Garage: SaaS commission benchmarks](https://www.hamstergarage.com/article/saas-affiliate-commission-rates-benchmarks-formulas); [Refgrow: choosing a rate](https://refgrow.com/blog/how-to-choose-affiliate-commission-rate)
- Click-to-paid conversion for paid subscriptions is about 1–3%. — [Hamster Garage KPI guide](https://www.hamstergarage.com/article/saas-affiliate-program-benchmarks-kpi-guide)
- Do not confuse two Rewardful figures: the "15–25% MRR contribution" is the share of revenue that affiliates bring, not a commission rate. — [Rewardful benchmarks](https://www.rewardful.com/articles/saas-affiliate-program-benchmarks)
- Lifetime cookies are rare and mostly used for subscriptions. Typical SaaS cookies are 60–90 days. — [QualityUnit: cookie lifetime](https://cdn.qualityunit.com/faq/recommended-cookie-lifetime-affiliate-programs); [Refgrow](https://refgrow.com/blog/how-to-choose-affiliate-commission-rate)

**Hybrid and flat-fee deals**
- A common hybrid is a guaranteed base fee plus a percentage of referral revenue; a typical recommendation is base fee plus 10–15%. SaaS creators may earn 15–30% on referred subscriptions. — [PostAffiliatePro: flat fee vs commission](https://www.postaffiliatepro.com/blog/influencer-rates-flat-fee-vs-commission/); [Matt McWilliams: hybrid deals](https://www.mattmcwilliams.com/hybrid-affiliate-influencer-deals/)
- YouTube examples: a $1,500 flat fee (60–70% of the creator's usual rate) plus $25 per sale or 10% revenue share; or $3,000 flat plus 10% on conversions for 90 days with no cap. Creators are advised to counter affiliate-only offers with a base fee. — [InfluencerFee: YouTube affiliate rates](https://www.influencerfee.com/blog/youtube-influencer-affiliate-rates/); [CreatorsAgency: affiliate vs flat rate](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/youtube-affiliate-vs-flat-rate-deals)
- Impact.com pushes "hybrid payments" as the route to better-paid sponsorships for creators. — [Impact: hybrid payments](https://impact.com/influencer/higher-paying-brand-sponsorships-for-creators/)

**YouTube sponsorship CPM (mostly US data)**
- Sponsored integrations usually cost brands about $10–50 per 1,000 expected views (other sources say $15–40 or $15–80). Sponsored Shorts cost about $5–15. — [1of10: sponsorship rates](https://www.1of10.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/); [elev8or: YouTube CPM](https://www.elev8or.io/cpm/youtube); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)
- CPM falls as channels grow: about $50–100 for 5,000–15,000 views per video, down to $10–15 at 250k–750k views. — [Adopter Media](https://adopter.media/?p=1151)
- Micro YouTubers ask about $200–1,000 per video. Integrations cost much less than dedicated videos, and price should follow views, not subscribers. — [Modash: YouTube pricing (pt)](https://www.modash.io/pt/blog/precos-influenciadores-youtube); [Modash](https://www.modash.io/pt/blog/youtube-influencer-pricing)
- Collabstr's reports show median YouTube asking prices falling from $675 to $311 between its 2025 and 2026 editions. — [Collabstr: influencer costs](https://collabstr.com/blog/influencer-costs-2025)

**Brazil-specific**
- Brazil has about 500,000 Instagram creators with more than 10k followers (Nielsen, 2022), the most in the world, and about 10.5 million counting accounts with 1k–10k. — [Acontecendo Aqui](https://acontecendoaqui.com.br/marketing/brasil-e-o-maior-mercado-do-mundo-em-marketing-de-influencia/); [O Povo](https://www.opovo.com.br/noticias/brasil/2022/05/29/brasil-tem-500-mil-influenciadores-digitais-empatando-com-numero-de-medicos.html)

- Mediation between agencies and micro-influencers in Brazil is still low, so most small creators deal directly with brands. — [Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/midia/mediacao-entre-agencias-e-microinfluenciadores-ainda-e-baixa)
- A BRL range given by a hiring marketplace: R$500–50,000 per publication ("indicative values"). — [Cronoshare](https://www.cronoshare.com.br/quanto-custa/contratar-influencer)
- MField data (Forbes Brasil, 2023): the average paid per action to Brazilian influencers reaches R$34,000, all platforms and sizes mixed. This is not representative of micro creators. — [Forbes Brasil](https://forbes.com.br/forbes-tech/2023/02/quanto-ganham-os-influenciadores-brasileiros/)
- Influency.me recommends pricing by CPM (cost per thousand views) rather than followers, and says about 54% of influencers don't know the metric. It has a calculator using average CPM by niche, but the figures were not visible. — [Influency.me: influencer CPM](https://influency.me/blog/cpm-de-influenciadores); [Mobile Time](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/18/12/2023/influency-me-lanca-ferramenta-que-calcula-investimento-em-campanha-com-influenciadores-digitais/)
- YouTube AdSense in Brazil pays about R$1–20 per 1,000 monetised views, depending on niche. This is the creator's ad income, useful as a floor when comparing offers. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/pt/quanto-youtube-paga-por-video/)

**Brazilian infoproduct norms (what Brazilian creators are used to)**
- On Hotmart, producers set affiliate commissions; reported values run from about 10% up to 70% of the product price, and "above 40%" is seen as attractive. — [Hostinger: Hotmart affiliate guide](https://www.hostinger.com/br/tutoriais/como-vender-na-hotmart-como-afiliado); [Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/internet/como-ser-um-afiliado-na-hotmart/)

**Viewer discounts**
- In quilting, partner codes usually give viewers a discount, for example Missouri Star's 20% off over $50 and EQ's 25% off. — [Connie Kresin](https://conniekresin.com/missouri-star-quilt-co-partner-for-july/); [EQ landing page](https://electricquilt.com/landing-pages/annies519/)

### Inferences
- Worked example (my own arithmetic, before payment fees and taxes):
  - Monthly plan: R$24.90 × 30% = R$7.47 per month per subscriber. A 12-month cap gives at most about R$89.64 per subscriber.
  - Annual plan: R$179 × 30% = R$53.70 per subscriber.
  - A small quilting YouTuber whose video gets 10,000 views at a 1–3% click-to-paid rate, on the views that click (perhaps 2–5% of viewers), would bring only a few to a few dozen subscribers. Revenue share alone pays them little at first. This is why creators ask for a base fee, and why a small flat fee or free product, plus revenue share, is the realistic offer.
- A sensible creator offer:
  - 20–30% of each payment for the first 12 months (or annual plan equivalent);
  - a viewer code giving 10–20% off the first period, or an extended trial;
  - a free Pro account for the creator;
  - optionally a small fixed fee (for example R$300–1,000) for a dedicated tutorial video from channels with steady views.

  The BRL fee is my estimate scaled down from US CPM data; I found no reliable Brazilian CPM table.
- Teachers can reasonably get a higher and/or longer share (for example a share for as long as the student is subscribed). Precedents are Skillshare (60% to teachers) and the Nuvemshop "Especialista" tier. Teachers retain students and provide ongoing support; YouTubers mostly bring one-time traffic.

### Gaps
- No reliable published CPM or price table for small/medium Brazilian YouTubers in BRL, or for the craft niche specifically. Squid, Influency.me and YouPix numbers by platform were not accessible through search. The YouPix 2025 creator survey was not found.
- No data on Brazilian sewing/craft creator rates.
- No official Rewardful page with the percentage of programs using 12-month versus lifetime commissions.

## 3. How creator deals differ from teacher deals

### Takeaway
Customers who come through **referrals** (people with a real relationship to the referrer) churn less and are worth more over time. That favours teachers. Affiliates and broadcasters get paid at the moment of the sale and have little reason to care about retention, and high-volume affiliates can bring lower-value accounts that churn. So creator deals should be shorter (capped), performance-based, and protected against code leakage. Teacher deals can be longer and tied to retention.

### Cited Findings
- Referred customers (rewarded referrals) show about 18% lower churn and about 16% higher lifetime value. These are Wharton figures as relayed by vendors. Another aggregation says churn is 20% lower than for paid-ad customers. — [Cello](https://cello.so/?p=17802); [Talkable](https://get.talkable.com/blog/3-reasons-why-referral-programs-acquire-new-customers)
- One reported comparison: paid-ad customers churn about 8% a month versus 3% for organic search. — [Hyros](https://hyros.com/updates/power-features/churn-attribution-ad-sources/)
- Recurring affiliate commissions (often 15–25% of subscription revenue) can erode net revenue retention when high-volume affiliates bring lower-value accounts that churn. — [Cello: referral vs affiliate programs](https://cello.so/blog/referral-programs-vs-affiliate-programs-b2b-saas/)
- Affiliates have little ongoing reason to care about retention once paid. — [Refgrow referral guide](https://refgrow.com/blog/complete-guide-referral-program-2026)
- In Brazil, YouTube content is mostly local: 85% of viewing of local channels comes from Brazil itself. Brazilian YouTubers reach Brazilians, but Spanish- and English-speaking markets need separate creators. — [YouTube Blog: O Brasil está aqui](https://blog.youtube/intl/pt-br/inside-youtube/campanha-yt-2025/)
- Most creators expect authentic integration rather than pure affiliate promotion. — [PostAffiliatePro](https://www.postaffiliatepro.com/blog/influencer-rates-flat-fee-vs-commission/)

### Inferences
- **Teachers:** small audience, strong relationship, students use the app in class, low churn, ongoing support. They justify a lifetime share for as long as the student stays subscribed, with a higher percentage, and free access.
- **YouTubers:** large audience, weak relationship, impulse sign-ups, more churn after the trial. They justify a time-limited share (12 months), a lower percentage or tiers, a viewer discount for urgency, and possibly a fee for production work.
- A YouTuber can also be a teacher (many Brazilian patchwork YouTubers sell Hotmart courses). The app needs rules for someone who is both, for example a "teacher" class link for their course students and a "creator" code for public videos.

### Gaps
- No data isolates churn of affiliate/influencer-referred customers from other referral customers, and nothing is specific to craft apps. The owner will need to track cohorts by channel (teacher, creator code, organic).

## 4. Mechanics: tracking, attribution, platforms and payouts usable in Brazil

### Takeaway
Use **both a referral link and a personal coupon code**. Codes work across devices and when cookies are refused; links give the cleanest attribution. Hotmart is a well-documented Brazilian reference for attribution: last click by default, cookie length chosen by the seller (60 days by default, up to "forever"), and coupons override the cookie. For payouts, the realistic Brazilian options are (a) running billing on **Asaas or Pagar.me with split payments**, which pays partners automatically from each recurring charge, or (b) a SaaS affiliate tool (Rewardful, FirstPromoter) on Stripe. Stripe Brazil has limits: Pix one-time only and restrictions on Connect, and Rewardful and FirstPromoter do not pay partners themselves.

### Cited Findings
**Attribution models (Hotmart as the Brazilian reference)**
- Hotmart offers three rules:
  - **Last click** (default; Hotmart calls it the most consistent);
  - **First click**, where the first affiliate to bring the buyer gets the commission;
  - **Multi-click**, where the commission is split between the first and last affiliate. It can only be turned on before the product has affiliates or coupons, and it cannot be undone.

  — [Hotmart: commission rules](https://help.hotmart.com/en/article/215828158/what-rules-to-attribute-commission-can-i-choose-for-my-product-); [Hotmart: how commissions are determined](https://help.hotmart.com/en/article/360016601731/how-are-affiliate-commissions-determined-at-hotmart-)
- Hotmart's cookie length is set by the producer: 60, 90 or 180 days, or forever, with 60 days the default. A **coupon overrides cookie attribution**. "HotLeads" keeps the commission when no cookie is found (private tabs, other browsers or devices). — [Hotmart: how cookies work](https://help.hotmart.com/en/article/360017792651/how-do-cookies-work-at-hotmart-)
- Hotmart also publishes "prioritization criteria for assigning affiliate commissions"; the details were not visible in search. — [Hotmart support](https://suportehotmart.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/36806399753485-What-are-the-prioritization-criteria-for-assigning-affiliate-commissions-at-Hotmart)
- Other cookie benchmarks: Domestika 30 days, Fat Quarter Shop 7 days, Cricut about 5 days. Typical SaaS is 60–90 days. — [Domestika](https://domestika.org/affiliates); [Awin FQS](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/89535); [Lasso Cricut](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/cricut/)

**Brazilian infoproduct platforms (built-in affiliate systems and split)**
- Kiwify: subscriptions by card, Pix and boleto. Affiliation can be open or require approval. "Co-production" splits commissions among 2 or more users. Individuals (CPF) reportedly face a R$2,200 limit on referral-commission withdrawals; companies (CNPJ) do not. — [Kiwify help: co-production](https://help.kiwify.com/en/article/how-to-set-up-co-production-6xhfc6/); [Contabilidade Zen: Kiwify/Eduzz split](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/kiwify-eduzz-split-contabilidade-2026/); [Mobills: Kiwify affiliates](https://www.mobills.com.br/blog/ganhar-dinheiro/kiwify-afiliados/)

**Payment-provider split (Brazil)**

- The YouTube Shopping affiliate program opened in Brazil in late 2025. It initially invited creators with 5,000+ subscribers. ### Inferences
- YouTube Shopping in Brazil covers products from partner retailers, so a quilt app subscription cannot be tagged there. The relevance is indirect: Brazilian craft YouTubers now earn from tagged fabrics and rulers on Shopee and Mercado Livre, which raises their expectations of performance pay. The app's own link plus code is still needed.
- Recommended tracking for this app:
  - Each partner gets a short link (`/?p=code`) and a matching coupon code, stored on the account at sign-up (first-party, server-side). That avoids long-lived third-party cookies.
  - Attribution goes to the first partner code tied to the account at sign-up. A code typed at checkout wins over a link click, as on Hotmart.
  - Keep a 30–60 day window between click and sign-up.
- Payout approach for Brazil:
  - Billing on **Asaas** (Pix Automático plus card plus split) is the simplest way to pay Brazilian partners automatically from each recurring charge.
  - It needs a partner sub-account (wallet) per teacher or creator, and attention to the split-divergence block when discounts or refunds change the net amount.
  - For PT/ES/EN partners outside Brazil, a Stripe plus FirstPromoter/Rewardful setup paying via PayPal/Wise is the conventional alternative.
- The owner's server already has users and accounts. A small partner table (code, kind = teacher/creator, share %, months cap, start date) plus monthly statements may be simpler than a third-party tool at first.

### Gaps
- Whether Hotmart, Kiwify and Eduzz pay affiliates on every renewal of a subscription was not confirmed from official pages.
- Asaas and Pagar.me fees for split, and whether a partner without a CNPJ can receive split payments, were not found.
- Whether Rewardful or FirstPromoter payouts reach Brazilian recipients well (Pix, BRL) was not found.

### Cited Findings

## 6. Rules in Brazil: CONAR, consumer code (CDC), LGPD, tax and contracts

### Takeaway
- **CONAR (2026 edition, in force since 1 June 2026):** affiliate links, coupons and pay-per-sale explicitly count as advertising. Disclosure must be clear, prominent and immediate (visible without tapping "more"), preferably using the platform's paid-content tool. Brands share responsibility, so the app must require and check disclosure.
- **CDC:** the creator and the brand can be jointly liable for misleading claims.
- **LGPD:** non-essential marketing cookies need consent.
- **Tax:** influencers generally cannot register as MEI under an influencer activity code. A company paying an individual without a CNPJ normally issues an RPA and withholds INSS (11%) and IRRF (progressive). Paying through a creator's company (ME/Simples Nacional) with an invoice is simpler.

### Cited Findings
**CONAR Influencer Advertising Guide (May 2026 edition)**
- CONAR published a new edition of the guide in May 2026, in force from 1 June 2026. The original guide dates from March 2021. — [Propmark](https://propmark.com.br/digital/conar-atualiza-guia-de-publicidade-com-influenciadores-e-inclui-regras-sobre-ia/); [Contábeis](https://www.contabeis.com.br/artigos/77061/conar-novas-regras-para-influenciadores-e-ia-a-partir-de-1o-de-junho/); [Original 2021 guide PDF (Poder360 mirror)](https://static.poder360.com.br/2020/12/Guia-influenciadores.pdf)
- The guide explicitly covers **affiliates**, meaning people paid on clicks, sales or other performance metrics. "Pay for performance makes it advertising"; posts with commission links follow the same rules as a contracted campaign. Commission links, coupons and affiliate programs count as advertising. — [UAI, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.uai.com.br/networking-e-negocios/2026/09/23/ganha-comissao-com-link-de-afiliado-para-o-conar-isso-ja-e-publicidade/); [Mundo do Marketing](https://mundodomarketing.com.br/marketing-de-influencia-entra-na-era-do-compliance-apos-novo-guia-do-conar); [Cescon Barrieu](https://cesconbarrieu.com.br/novas-diretrizes-do-conar-para-marketing-de-influencia-o-que-muda/)
- A reciprocal arrangement between brand and influencer is enough to make content advertising; editorial control by the brand is no longer required. — [TozziniFreire](https://www.tozzinifreire.com.br/en/boletins/novo-guia-do-conar-o-que-muda-para-campanhas-com-influenciadores-digitais)
- Disclosure must be clear, prominent, immediate and part of the content itself, visible on first view without scrolling, the bio or "more". Platform paid-content tools ("paid partnership", "contains paid promotion") are to be prioritised.
  - Sources differ on whether the tools **replace** #publi/#publicidade or **are combined** with them.

  — [Propmark: new guide and transparency](https://propmark.com.br/artigos/o-novo-guia-do-conar-para-influenciadores-e-os-riscos-da-falta-de-transparencia/); [KLA Law](https://www.klalaw.com.br/conar-atualiza-guia-de-publicidade-com-influenciadores/); [Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/456124/guia-conar-2026-o-que-todo-influenciador-precisa-saber-agora)
- Compliance is **not only the influencer's responsibility**. Brands and agencies should map formats such as affiliate and ambassador programs, and lawyers recommend recording the affiliate relationship in a contract. — [Cescon Barrieu](https://cesconbarrieu.com.br/marketing-de-influencia-conar/); [UAI](https://www.uai.com.br/?p=177249)
- AI-made content, avatars and virtual influencers follow the same rules. There is extra care for children and teenagers in light of the ECA Digital (Law 15.211/2025). — [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/brazil/social-media/1796358/conar-updates-guidelines-on-advertising-with-influencers); [Cescon Barrieu](https://cesconbarrieu.com.br/novas-diretrizes-do-conar-para-marketing-de-influencia-o-que-muda/)
- Note: the official CONAR PDF could not be fetched (conar.org.br blocked here). The points above come from law-firm and trade-press summaries.

**Consumer Defence Code (CDC)**
- Art. 36: advertising must be easy to identify as such; hidden advertising breaks this. Art. 37 forbids misleading advertising, including by omission. — [Conjur](https://www.conjur.com.br/2023-abr-05/responsabilidade-influencers-indicam-produtos-otica-cdc//?print=1); [Jus.com.br](https://jus.com.br/artigos/86640/os-influenciadores-digitais-nas-relacoes-de-consumo-uma-analise-acerca-da-aplicabilidade-do-codigo-de-defesa-do-consumidor/2)
- Under the broad definition of supplier (art. 3), the influencer can be part of the supply chain, and joint liability (arts. 7 sole paragraph and 25 §1) can apply. Many scholars argue for strict liability; a minority view disagrees. — [Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/423676/parceria-influenciador-e-empresa-responsabilidade-de-ambas-as-partes); [Lex Legal](https://lexlegal.com.br/?p=29901)
- One source says São Paulo's court (TJSP) distinguishes a passing product mention from structured advertising with a **discount code and affiliate link**; the code and link weigh toward liability. The ruling itself was not seen. — [Lex Legal](https://lexlegal.com.br/?p=29901)

**LGPD and tracking**
- ANPD's guide "Cookies e Proteção de Dados Pessoais" (October 2022) interprets the law; it is not a binding regulation. For non-essential cookies such as marketing, consent is the fitting legal basis. Refusing must be as visible as accepting, and withdrawing consent as easy as giving it. — [Lefosse](https://lefosse.com/noticias/anpd-publica-guia-orientativo-sobre-o-uso-de-cookies); [Felsberg](https://www.felsberg.com.br/cookies-e-protecao-de-dados-pessoais-orientacoes-da-anpd/); [Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/direito-digital/anpd-divulga-novo-guia-com-orientacoes-sobre-cookies); [Confidata](https://confidata.com.br/blog/cookies-rastreamento-consentimento-anpd)

**Tax on paying creators**
- Paying an individual (no CNPJ): the company (tomador) issues an **RPA** and withholds **INSS** and **IRRF**, and ISS in some cases. Shopee's affiliate program, for example, withholds 11% INSS plus progressive IRRF. Without an invoice, withholding can reach 27.5% IRRF plus INSS. — [Contabilidade Zen: RPA 2026](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/rpa-recibo-pagamento-autonomo-modelo-2026/); [Shopee blog](https://shopee.com.br/blog/afiliado-shopee-comissao-pagamentos/); [Meu Contador Online](https://www.meucontadoronline.com.br/blog/influenciador-nota-fiscal-2026/)
- Paying affiliate individuals in batches by RPA raises eSocial reporting questions (S-1200/S-2500); there is no settled answer in the forum thread. — [Contábeis forum](https://www.contabeis.com.br/forum/Tributos%20Federais/415123/rpa-em-lote-para-afiliado-pf-comissao-extra-shopee-periodicidade-certa-no-esocial-s-1200-s-2500/)
- MEI: accounting firms agree there is **no MEI activity code for influencers or content creators**, and marketing is not on the MEI list. Creators usually need an ME under Simples Nacional. Some say course sales can fit an instructor code. MEI revenue limit: R$81,000/year. These are accounting blogs, not the official Receita list. — [Contabilidade.com](https://contabilidade.com/blog/influencer-pode-ser-mei-descubra-como-abrir-empresa/); [Neon](https://neon.com.br/aprenda/mei/cnae-marketing-digital/); [Contabilizei](https://www.contabilizei.com.br/contabilizei-responde/marketing-pode-ser-mei/); [Contabilidade Zen: MEI or CNPJ for YouTubers](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/mei-ou-cnpj-youtubers-creators-2026/); [Actana: Shopee affiliate can't be MEI](https://actana.com.br/blog/afiliado-shopee-pode-ser-mei-simples-nacional)
- On split platforms (Kiwify), each party invoices its own share with its own CNPJ. — [Contabilidade Zen](https://www.contabilidadezen.com.br/blog/kiwify-eduzz-split-contabilidade-2026/)

**Contracts (what to include)**
- Recommended clauses: deliverables, fee or commission, the base the commission is calculated on, hold period, refund rule, usage rights, exclusivity, approval steps, disclosure requirements and how either side ends the deal. — [Matt McWilliams](https://www.mattmcwilliams.com/hybrid-affiliate-influencer-deals/)
- Retail programs add: no commission on returned, cancelled, fraudulent or self-referral orders, and no bidding on the brand name in search ads. — [Awin FQS terms](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/89535)

### Inferences
- Under CONAR 2026, **every** revenue-share creator is an advertiser, even with no fixed fee. The app should give partners a ready disclosure line in PT/ES/EN, require the YouTube paid-promotion box, and keep proof (link to video, screenshot) in the partner record.
- The app should avoid unprovable claims in creator scripts (for example "prints exact templates", "used by X quilters"), since the CDC can make the app and the creator jointly liable.
- Tax admin is easiest if the app pays only creators with a CNPJ (ME/Simples) who send an invoice, or pays through a split platform where each party invoices its own share. Paying individuals by RPA means monthly withholding and eSocial work, which needs an accountant. This applies to teachers too.
- LGPD: a first-party referral code saved at sign-up, used only to pay the partner, is arguably part of running the service (contract or legitimate interest) rather than marketing tracking. Third-party ad or affiliate pixels would need consent. This is an inference; confirm with a lawyer.

### Gaps
- Exact wording of the CONAR 2026 guide (hashtag versus platform tool; whether long YouTube videos need repeated disclosure). Primary PDF not accessible.
- Effect of Brazil's tax reform (CBS/IBS from 2026–27) on paying commissions was not researched.
- Whether Spanish-speaking markets (Mexico, Argentina, Spain) need separate disclosure rules was not covered.

## 7. Risks: channel conflict, coupon leaks and fraud, dependence on a few creators, reputation

### Takeaway
The main risks:
1. **Teacher versus YouTuber conflict** over the same student. Fix it with clear written attribution rules set before the first dispute, for example teacher class links always win, or a split.
2. **Coupon codes leaking** to Honey/Cuponomia-type sites within hours. Fix it with partner-locked or single-use codes, link-applied discounts, code rotation and contract clauses.
3. **Self-referral and refund abuse.** Fix it with a hold period and clawbacks.
4. **Dependence on, or reputation damage from, a few creators.** Fix it with many small creators, morality and termination clauses, and caps.

### Cited Findings
**Channel conflict and attribution**
- Channel conflict means sales routes competing for the same customer. Typical forms: coupon interception, brand bidding, and last-click disputes. Advice: write the rules before the first dispute, aim for managed rather than zero conflict, and fix it with rules, attribution and compensation design. — [Reditus glossary: channel conflict](https://getreditus.com/resources/glossary/channel-conflict)
- A hybrid design: last-click within each partner type, tiered attribution windows, and a tiebreaker that favours one partner class, with the voided credit logged for audit. Flat influencer fees are treated as content payments, not commissions. This is one vendor's framework. — [Track360: hybrid influencer-affiliate architecture 2026](https://track360.io/blog/hybrid-influencer-affiliate-programs-architecture-2026)
- Some platforms pay everyone in the purchase path. Hotmart's multi-click splits between first and last affiliate. — [Hotmart rules](https://help.hotmart.com/en/article/215828158/what-rules-to-attribute-commission-can-i-choose-for-my-product-); [Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/Affiliate-Marketing-Time-to-Adopt-Attribution-Rules)
- Conflict occurs when participants compete for the same customer under rules that fail to protect their distinct roles. — [SalesTechStar: Channel Conflict 2.0](https://salestechstar.com/staff-writers/channel-conflict-2-0-managing-the-influencer-ecosystem/)

**Coupon leakage and fraud**
- Influencer-exclusive codes can appear on Honey, RetailMeNot and similar sites within hours; browser extensions surface them automatically. Leaks are deliberate (affiliate posts the code) or accidental (scraped). — [Uniqodo](https://www.uniqodo.com/post/stop-paying-affiliate-commission-on-stolen-codes); [LoudCrowd](https://loudcrowd.com/blog/coupon-fraud/)
- Defences:
  - partner-locked or single-use codes;
  - rotating leaked codes;
  - anti-coupon-site clauses;
  - "codeless" discounts applied through a link;
  - comparing redemptions with the partner's audience and posting activity;
  - checking whether the buyer was already at checkout before entering the code.

  — [Track360: coupon affiliate sites guide](https://track360.io/blog/coupon-affiliate-sites-operator-management-guide-2026); [Saral: leaked codes](https://help.getsaral.com/en/articles/338-best-practices-when-influencer-codes-or-links-get-leaked); [Social Snowball](https://socialsnowball.io/post/how-to-stop-leaked-discount-codes-and-promo-codes); [Aspire SecureCodes](https://www.aspire.io/securecodes)
- One developer found an influencer's code on 14 coupon sites. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/vhub_systems_ed5641f65d59/i-found-my-influencer-discount-code-on-14-coupon-sites-the-creators-performance-was-just-honey-1kn3)
- Self-referral and coupon-leak checks can be built on Stripe data. — [DEV Community: self-referral and coupon leaks](https://dev.to/mihirkanzariya/self-referral-and-coupon-leaks-the-affiliate-fraud-checks-worth-building-on-stripe-1376)
- Claimed fraud cost: "10–30% of affiliate spend". Vendor figure with no primary study. — [Refgrow fraud guide](https://refgrow.com/blog/affiliate-fraud-detection-guide)
- Retail programs withhold commission on returned, cancelled, fraudulent and self-referral orders. — [Awin FQS](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/89535)

**Dependence, reputation and program changes**
- Canva closed its open affiliate program and moved affiliates into an invite-style Canvassador program. Existing affiliates had to reapply, and applications are currently closed. A brand can and does change terms, so terms should state that the brand may change them, with notice. — [Canva help](https://www.canva.com/pt_pt/help/canva-affiliate-marketing-program/)
- Skillshare changed teacher referral pay from a flat $10 to 60% of net payments in 2022, which shows terms evolve as a program matures. — [Skillshare help](https://help.skillshare.com/hc/articles/4440423784333)
- BERNINA ambassador terms (18 months, exclusivity for artisans) show fixed terms and exclusivity as tools for managing relationships. — [WeAllSew 2024](https://weallsew.com/?p=117250)
- Under CONAR 2026 and the CDC, brands share responsibility for creators' undisclosed or misleading content. — [Cescon Barrieu](https://cesconbarrieu.com.br/marketing-de-influencia-conar/); [Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/423676/parceria-influenciador-e-empresa-responsabilidade-de-ambas-as-partes)

### Inferences
- **A teacher-versus-YouTuber rule for this app** (proposal):
  1. A student who joins through a teacher's class link or class code is "owned" by that teacher for as long as they stay subscribed. This is first touch, and it is locked.
  2. A creator code counts only if the account has no teacher. If a teacher's student later uses a creator's code, the teacher keeps the share and the creator gets nothing, or a small one-time bonus.
  3. If a creator-referred subscriber later joins a teacher's class, either keep the creator for their 12-month window and then move the share to the teacher, or split. Publish the rule.
  4. Show partners their attributed users (anonymised) so they trust the system.
- Write the rules into partner terms before launch, as the channel-conflict sources advise. Hotmart's switch-once multi-click option shows that changing attribution later is painful.
- **Coupon design:**
  - Make the creator's viewer discount an extended trial, or a first-month discount applied through the creator's link, rather than a big reusable public code.
  - Watch for codes on Brazilian coupon sites (Cuponomia, Méliuz, Pelando are the common ones; not researched here).
  - Pay commission only after a 30-day hold, so refunds and chargebacks reverse first.
  - Never pay on a partner's own account (self-referral).
- **Concentration:** cap any single creator's share of new sign-ups in the dashboards, and favour many small Brazilian patchwork creators (most Brazilian creators are under 50k followers). Small creators also fit a quilting niche better.
- **Reputation:** include a morality and termination clause, review scripts or claims, require disclosure, and have the right to end the partnership with notice while still paying commissions already earned.

### Gaps
- No quilting- or Brazil-specific data on coupon-site leakage rates or affiliate fraud.
- No documented teacher-versus-influencer conflict case in a craft or education SaaS. The proposed rule is my synthesis of the general sources plus the Hotmart and Nuvemshop precedents.
- Brazilian coupon sites (Cuponomia, Méliuz, Pelando) and their behaviour with SaaS codes were not researched.
