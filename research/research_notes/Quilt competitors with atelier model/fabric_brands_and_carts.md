# Quilting fabric brands, their stores, and pre-filling shopping carts from a quilt design app

Research notes, 2 November 2026. Method: 53 WebSearch calls. WebFetch was blocked by the session's network policy (shopify.dev did not resolve), so every finding below comes from search-result text and the URLs it showed. Search snippets can be out of date or out of context. Items marked "unverified" or "background knowledge" were not confirmed by a source in this session.

## 1. Main quilting fabric manufacturers (international and Brazilian): country, direct sales or wholesale only, store platform, swatch images for software

### Takeaway
Nearly all the big international quilting-cotton makers (Moda, Art Gallery, Riley Blake, Tilda, Robert Kaufman, FreeSpirit/Jaftex brands, Windham, Northcott and others) sell **wholesale only**. A cart can only be filled at a **retailer**, not at the brand. Clear exceptions are Liberty (sells by the metre on libertylondon.com) and print-on-demand Spoonflower. The industry is consolidating: Missouri Star is linked to Robert Kaufman and LoveCrafts/WEBS, and Jaftex owns FreeSpirit, Henry Glass, Blank and Studio E. In Brazil, quilting fabric is mostly "tricoline" (100% cotton). Brazilian makers (Fernando Maluhy, Peripan, Karsten Ateliê, Fabricart, Caldeira and others) mostly sell through multi-brand shops and the Magalu and Mercado Livre marketplaces. None of them was found offering a digital swatch library.

## 2. Technical ways to pre-fill a cart, and whether they work for fabric sold by the half-metre, yard or fat quarter

### Takeaway
All the main platforms have a documented or common way to build a cart from a link. The best are Shopify (`/cart/<variant>:<qty>,<variant>:<qty>`), VTEX (`/checkout/cart/add?sku=&qty=&seller=&sc=`, many items allowed), Nuvemshop/Tiendanube (`/cart-link/<variant>-<qty>`) and Amazon's Add-to-Cart form (`ASIN.n`/`Quantity.n` + `AssociateTag`). Plain WooCommerce takes one product per link unless the shop has a plugin. Tray's simple link only works for products without variants. Fabric works because shops sell in **whole units** (a half-yard, quarter-yard, 50 cm cut, or fat quarter as its own variant). The app must therefore know each shop's unit and convert the design's metres into whole units.

### Cited findings
- **Shopify cart permalinks:** the format is `/cart/variant_id:quantity`, comma-separated for several variants, for example `https://my-shop-name.myshopify.com/cart/36485954240671:1`. Query parameters can add a discount code or attribute the order to a sales channel — [Shopify Help: Cart permalinks](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/cart-permalink). Community posts add more. `?discount=CODE` applies a discount. `checkout[email]=` and a note parameter can be added. Customers cannot edit quantities when they follow a permalink. `/cart/add?id[]=…` and `/cart/update?updates[variant]=qty` add to an existing cart. Some users report errors on some stores — [Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/cart-use-permalinks-to-pre-load-the-cart/7494/1).
- **Shopify variant IDs are public:** many stores expose `/products.json`, which lists products with nested variants and IDs. It returns up to 250 per page (default 50) and is paged with `since_id`. Some stores block it (403/404) — [Recharge](https://support.getrecharge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009746154); [Apify Shopify scraper](https://apify.com/micro-tool-factory/shopify-products).
- **Shopify and fractional yardage:** Shopify quantities are whole numbers. Fabric shops usually make the half-yard the unit: price it as 0.5 yd and count stock in half-yards, so 15 yd = 30 units. Other approaches are separate length variants, a 0.5 step through theme code, or pricing-calculator apps — [Shopify Community 0.5 increment](https://community.shopify.com/t/0-5-increment-in-product-quantity/251006/2); [Shopify Community fractional](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-can-i-sell-fabric-in-fractional-quantities-like-1-2-or-1-4-yard/280316); [Custom Price Calculator](https://custompricecalculator.com/blog/shopify-fabric-pricing-calculator/). Real listings say "1/2 yard increments, cut continuously" — [Simply Love Fabrics](https://simplylovefabrics.patternbyetsy.com/listing/939443647/regency-zarafa-12-yard-increments-cut). Shops publish cutting rules, for example quantity 3 = 1.5 yd — [Little Fabric Shop cutting info](https://littlefabricshop.com/pages/cutting-info).
- **WooCommerce:** stock WooCommerce takes one product per `?add-to-cart=ID` link. The free plugin "Add multiple products to cart via url" allows `?add-to-cart=12:2,34:1,56:5`. It handles simple and variable products, not grouped ones, with defaults of 50 products and quantity 999 — [WordPress.org plugin](https://hsb.wordpress.org/plugins/add-multiple-products-to-cart-via-url-for-woocommerce/).
- **VTEX** (common for large Brazilian shops): the format is `/checkout/cart/add?sc=1&sku=22&qty=1&seller=1&sku=73&qty=3&seller=1`, with sku, qty and seller repeated for each item — [VTEX Help (PT)](https://help.vtex.com/pt/docs/tutorials/como-montar-a-url-do-carrinho); [VTEX Help (EN)](https://help.vtex.com/docs/tutorials/how-to-assemble-the-cart-url). Any number of SKUs is allowed, but `sc` must match the shop's sales channel — [VTEX Community](https://community.vtex.com/t/redirect-to-cart-or-checkout/16861). There is also the Checkout API — [VTEX developers](https://developers.vtex.com/docs/guides/creating-a-regular-order-with-the-checkout-api).
- **Nuvemshop / Tiendanube** (common for small Brazilian and Latin American shops): `/cart-link/<variant_id>-<quantity>`, for example `/cart-link/123456-3`. The link adds to the customer's current cart — [Nuvemshop Central de Atendimento](https://atendimento.nuvemshop.com.br/pt_BR/vendas/como-compartilhar-o-link-do-carrinho-direto-de-um-produto-da-minha-loja-nuvemshop); [Tiendanube Ayuda](https://ayuda.tiendanube.com/es_CO/ventas/%C2%BFcomo-puedo-compartir-el-enlace-directo-al-carrinho-de-compra-de-un-producto-de-mi-tienda-tiendanube). There is a REST API with OAuth for apps — [TiendaNube api-docs](https://github.com/TiendaNube/api-docs).
- **Tray:** the theme variable `{{ links.cart_add ~ product.id }}` builds an add-to-cart link, but only for products without variants. Products with variants need the JavaScript cart API — [Tray Partners docs](https://partners.tray.com.br/v/themes/construindo-seu-template/desenvolver/como-adicionar-o-botao-comprar-na-listagem-dos-produtos); [Tray add without leaving page](https://partners.tray.com.br/themes/construindo-seu-template/referencias/componentes/adicionar-ao-carrinho-sem-sair-da-pagina).
- **Magento (Signa extension):** an add-to-cart link with SKU and `qty=` — [Mageshop docs](https://docs.mageshop.com.br/books/nexaas-%7C-signa/page/adicionar-produto-ao-carrinho-via-link---signa-commerce/export/pdf).
- **Amazon Add-to-Cart form:** POST or GET to `/gp/aws/cart/add.html` with `ASIN.1`/`Quantity.1`, `ASIN.2`/`Quantity.2`… and `AssociateTag`, which is required for attribution — [Amazon PA-API 5 docs (JP)](https://webservices.amazon.co.jp/paapi5/documentation/add-to-cart-form.html); [GitHub gist](https://gist.github.com/ericdfields/3d4ed9c7f7b559289a102207facd61a7).
- **Mercado Livre affiliates:** links cannot be made on the home page, daily offers, search results or category pages, only on item pages — [Tactus](https://tactus.com.br/como-vender-como-afiliado-no-mercado-livre/).

### Inferences
- A workable design is a **per-shop adapter**: platform (shopify | vtex | nuvemshop | woo | tray | amazon), base URL, unit (for example 0.5 yd, 0.25 yd, 0.5 m, 1 m, or a fat-quarter variant), minimum cut, and variant IDs per fabric. The app rounds each fabric's need up to whole units. For example, 1.3 m of fabric at a 0.5 m-unit shop is 3 units, and at a half-yard shop it is 1.3/0.4572 = 2.84, so 3 units. The app shows the waste.
- Shopify and VTEX handle a whole design (many fabrics) in one link. Nuvemshop's format is documented per variant, and whether several `/cart-link/` items chain in one URL is unclear. WooCommerce and Tray shops may need the shop's cooperation (a plugin or JS snippet).
- Keeping variant IDs and stock current needs a sync job, either `/products.json` for Shopify or a partner feed. Scraping without permission risks breaking the shop's terms. Formal partnerships with feed access are safer.
- Fat quarters and precuts are separate products or variants, not lengths. The app could suggest "buy a fat-quarter bundle" when a design uses less than about 50 × 55 cm of each of many fabrics.
- Shopify's `?ref=`/channel attribution and discount parameters could carry an affiliate code or a partner discount without a separate affiliate network.

### Gaps
- Shopify's developer page (shopify.dev cart permalinks) could not be fetched. Current support for `/cart/` permalinks on every theme and checkout is unverified.
- Loja Integrada's cart-link format was not found.
- Whether Mercado Livre or Magalu let an outside site pre-fill a multi-item cart was not found. They appear to allow only item-level affiliate links.
- Amazon's Add-to-Cart form is documented on an older or Japanese page. Whether it still works on amazon.com.br was not confirmed.

## 3. Precedents: apps and sites that link designs to buying fabric, and affiliate programmes with commission rates

### Takeaway
The closest precedent is **Quiltster**: plan a quilt in its planner, then order the finished plan as a kit from partner quilt shops in its marketplace. Partners fulfil the orders, and Quiltster keeps 10% of each kit sale. EQ8 and PreQuilt show real manufacturer fabrics but were not found to fill carts. Quiltler 3 and QuiltFit stop at yardage and shopping lists. Affiliate rates in the quilt niche run from about 6% (Fat Quarter Shop, 7-day cookie, Awin) to 10% (Connecting Threads), up to 30% at a small shop (Fabric Hut). Brazilian marketplaces pay about 2–16% by category. Missouri Star has an affiliate programme by application, but its rate is unpublished.

## 4. Licensing: can an app legally show manufacturers' fabric images, and how did EQ and PreQuilt get the rights?

### Takeaway
Fabric prints are copyrighted artwork, often licensed in from designers. Showing them commercially needs the rights holder's permission, and no public licence for software was found. Electric Quilt sends users to manufacturers for permission and does not say publicly how it got its library. The pattern seen (EQ8's SKU-labelled library, Robert Kaufman's mock-up programme, Moda's JPG zips, PreQuilt's Suzy Quilts deal) is that the industry supplies images under informal or partner permission, because it wants sales. A paid app should get **written agreements** with each manufacturer or distributor, or use retailer-supplied images under the retailer's partnership.

### Cited findings
- Electric Quilt: "Please contact the fabric manufacturer with further questions on permission of use." EQ separates its copyright-free blocks from other material — [EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=1190). EQ's own media need prior permission — [EQ media kits](https://electricquilt.com/media-kits/).
- The EQ8 library carries manufacturer, designer, line and SKU for each of its 6,000+ fabrics. Results did not state the licence terms — [EQ support](https://support.electricquilt.com/?p=2614).
- A quilting blog notes that manufacturer images downloaded into EQ8 are for personal use and should not be posted online or in patterns — [Meadow Mist Designs](https://meadowmistdesigns.blogspot.com/2020/02/eq8-importing-fabric-series-post-2.html).
- "Licensed" fabric (for example Disney) carries an agreement between the manufacturer and the licensor, not the end users, and there may be restrictions on use — [Sewing.org](https://www.sewing.org/scripts/blog/?p=2188).
- Robert Kaufman's partnership document: designers keep rights to their patterns. RK uses them for promotional mock-ups and fabric-requirement planning and may supply JPEGs of collections — [RK doc](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/1g9xtxk4PeL6d7LMW9PMkNf5dioeivzh7iEFpXwjoIXc/mobilebasic).
- Independent designers on Spoonflower set their own terms, for example commercial use with credit and separate licensing rates — [Spoonflower listing](https://www.spoonflower.com/fabric/8859197).
- PreQuilt's visible partnership is with Suzy Quilts: pattern mock-ups with real swatches including AGF Pure Solids — [Suzy Quilts](https://suzyquilts.com/best-way-to-pick-fabric-for-a-quilt/). No public statement on manufacturer licensing was found.

### Inferences
- Solid-colour palettes (Kona 365, Bella 473, AGF Pure Solids 120 plus 15) are low risk. They are colour values and names, not artwork, though brand names and colour names are trademarks to use fairly. They are a good first step for many brands at once.
- Printed fabrics need permission. Practical routes are (a) a manufacturer partnership (EQ8 and RK show it is normal), (b) a retail partner such as Missouri Star whose product feed includes images it may sublicense, and (c) user photos (PreQuilt and EQ8 let users import fabric), which puts the use on the user's side for personal designs.
- Scraping Shopify `/products.json` images without permission would be technically easy but legally weak, especially for a paid app.
- Manufacturers want sell-through. A pitch of "we send buyers to your stockists' carts" fits their wholesale-only policies (Moda, AGF, Riley Blake) better than selling direct does.

### Gaps
- EQ's and PreQuilt's actual contracts with manufacturers (paid or free, exclusive or not, approval of new collections) were not found.
- No legal source (US, Brazilian or EU copyright law) on showing product images to drive retail sales was retrieved. An IP lawyer should confirm, especially under Brazilian Lei 9.610/98 (background knowledge, not verified here).
- Moda's terms for the downloadable "JPG Images" zips are unknown.
