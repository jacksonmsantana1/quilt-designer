# Market context, adjacent and indirect competitors, and unmet needs in quilt design software (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked by the network policy here (first attempt, quiltingcompany.com, failed to resolve), so every finding below comes from web-search result text and the URLs it showed. Numbers have not been checked against the full source pages. 35 searches were used. Where a source sells a competing product, or looks like an SEO/content-farm page, that is flagged.

The owner's app, for comparison: free, runs in the browser, offline-first. 27 traditional blocks with editable parts; borders, sashing, binding and backing; symmetry; fussy cutting; the user's own fabric photos (file or webcam); quilting-stitch preview; cutting plan; A4 PDF with real-size templates; 3D cloth-physics drape on a bed or table; metric (mm); optional sync between devices.

## 1. How big is the market, and who buys design software?

### Takeaway
The US quilting industry is roughly a $4.5–5 billion a year market with a steady 9–11 million active quilters. The core buyer is a retired woman in her 60s (average 67 in the 2026 survey) who buys mostly from local shops and spends freely. Newer and younger quilters are a growing share and find the craft through social media. No reliable public figure exists for the share of quilters who use design software.

### Cited Findings

**US market size (industry surveys, the most credible figures)**
- The 2025 Quilter's Survey (run by Handi Quilter, reported by the Craft Industry Alliance) says the market "appears to be softening somewhat". The previous year's survey had projected $4.958 billion annual revenue for 2025. Data was collected in February 2025, before the US tariff announcements. — [Craft Industry Alliance, 2025 survey](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/)
- Later Craft Industry Alliance material and secondary sites cite about **$4.5 billion** annual revenue (down from the $4.958B estimate), with growth slowing. The $4.5B figure shows up mainly in secondary roundups, so treat it as a reported update. — [CIA reflections on 2025 / predictions 2026](https://craftindustryalliance.org/craft-industry-reflections-on-2025-and-predictions-for-2026/); [craftedblog.com Quilting Statistics 2026](https://craftedblog.com/quilting-statistics/); [quiltgearhq.com](https://quiltgearhq.com/quilting-statistics/)
- A 2026 guide attributes to the survey a projection of **$5 billion by 2027**. I could not confirm this against the primary coverage. — [lindas.com 2026 beginners guide](https://lindas.com/blogs/learn-and-love/quilting-supplies-for-beginners-2026-guide)
- **9–11 million active consumers**, a number that has held steady for about 10 years. — [CIA 2025 survey](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/); [CIA 2026 survey](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)
- Older figures, noted as old: the 2014 *Quilting in America* summary put the spending of "dedicated quilters" at 60% of industry spending (US$2.27 billion), and a 2010 edition estimated 21 million US "quilters" under a broader definition. — [QIA summary 2014 (fabshopnet PDF)](https://fabshopnet.com/wp-content/uploads/downloads/QIA_summary_2014.pdf); [Craft Industry Alliance, how quilting came to dominate the US sewing market](https://craftindustryalliance.org/how-quilting-came-to-dominate-the-u-s-sewing-market/)
- *Quilting in America* (the series that began in 1994) seems to have been replaced in practice by the Handi Quilter "Quilter's Survey". In 2024 that survey had more than 37,000 respondents (average completion time 66 minutes). In 2024 it was sent by email to more than 1.8 million quilters through several brands. — [CIA 2024 survey](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2024/); [Handi Quilter Quilters' Survey page](https://handiquilter.com/quilters-survey/); [quilterssurvey.com](https://quilterssurvey.com/)
- Sampling caveats: a commenter noted that younger quilters are not represented. A 2025 respondent called the survey "less and less relevant to most of the quilters I am around". Past surveys "may skew older due to how they've been distributed". — [CIA 2024/2025 survey pages and comments](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/); [tcrcrafts.com quilting statistics](https://tcrcrafts.com/quilting-statistics)

**Global "quilt market" reports (low reliability; they define the market differently and mostly measure finished bedding quilts, not the craft)**
- TechSci: global quilt market **US$695.84M in 2024**, reaching $1,156.12M by 2030 (8.8% CAGR). — [TechSci Research](https://www.techsciresearch.com/report/quilt-market/30675.html)
- Expert Market Research: about **US$5.32B in 2024**, 5.70% CAGR from 2025 to 2034, $9.26B by 2034. — [Expert Market Research](https://expertmarketresearch.com/reports/quilt-market)
- Reanin: US$5,289.93M in 2024, reaching $6,961.19M by 2031 (4.0% CAGR). — [Reanin](https://www.reanin.com/reports/global-quilt-market)
- Lucintel: 5.7% CAGR from 2025 to 2031. — [Lucintel via MarketResearch.com](https://www.marketresearch.com/Lucintel-v2747/Quilt-Trends-Forecast-Competitive-43464067)
- These estimates disagree by about 7x (TechSci versus EMR). They are paid or teaser reports about bedding quilts, so they are not useful for sizing the hobby or software market.

**Demographics and spending**
- 2024 survey: the average quilter is female (98%), retired (70%) and "in her 60s". Respondents started their first quilt at about age 39 on average, after sewing for an average of 22 years. — [CIA 2024 survey](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2024/); [tcrcrafts.com](https://tcrcrafts.com/quilting-statistics)
- 2024: about 80% said they spend the same or more time and money than in earlier years. 61% prefer a local quilt shop and 16% prefer buying online. — [CIA 2024 survey](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2024/)
- 2025: **18% of respondents were new to quilting**, up from 11% the year before. — [CIA 2025 survey](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/)
- 2026 survey (presented 8 May 2026 at h+h americas): the typical quilter is over 60 (**average age 67**) and retired, and comes to quilting seriously in her mid-to-late 40s. A significant group under 60 still works full or part time, and many people start in their 30s. **Social media is now the main way all generations discover quilting.** The survey suggests shops offer smaller, cheaper items such as beginner kits. It also describes quilters as comfortable with technology. — [CIA 2026 consumer survey](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)
- Modern Quilt Guild (a proxy for "modern", usually younger quilters): over 18,000 members worldwide in 2023, over 16,000 in 2024 (a fall). — [MQG 2022-23 annual report](https://www.themodernquiltguild.com/about-us/annual-report); [MQG 2023-24 annual report](https://www.themodernquiltguild.com/annual-report)

**Younger and modern quilters**
- TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Pinterest are bringing younger people into quilting and sewing. Most of the evidence is anecdotal or comes from local TV. — [KTVB](https://ktvb.com/article/news/local/idaho-today/quilting-is-making-a-comeback-to-a-younger-generation-thanks-to-tiktok/277-9e939513-d55a-46df-860f-1786b2cd26a5); [Axios Seattle, Dec 2024](https://www.axios.com/local/seattle/2024/12/11/knitting-needlepoint-crafts-grandma-hobbies-tiktok); [Global News "Grandmacore"](https://globalnews.ca/video/11090698/grandmacore-why-hobbies-like-knitting-quilting-are-making-a-comeback-with-gen-z/)
- A WGSN study found that pandemic "digital fatigue" pushed Gen Z towards hands-on crafts. John Lewis reported sewing machine sales up 127% in April 2020 (general sewing, not quilting). — [i-D](https://i-d.co/?p=40095)
- What appeals to younger quilters: sustainability (reusing scraps and old clothes) and moving away from traditional florals (a shop blog, low authority). — [Soul Stitchery blog](https://www.mysoulstitchery.com/blogs/soul-stitchery-blog/the-next-generation-of-quilters)
- Instagram and TikTok have replaced local quilting bees as the community space. — [Jackson Hole News&Guide](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/jackson_hole_daily/our_world_people/brighter_side/quilt-wyoming-fills-northwest-college-halls/article_5467c172-764b-483d-b51a-bff2a77d2a04.html)

**Share using design software**
- No credible figure was found. Worldmetrics claims "40% of quilters use cloud-based quilting software" and "15% of professional quilters use 3D quilting design tools", but the page gives no traceable source and looks unreliable. — [worldmetrics.org](https://worldmetrics.org/quilting-industry-statistics/)
- Handmadelytics claims EQ8 is "used by 64% of published designers", credited vaguely to an AQS survey and not verifiable. — [handmadelytics.com](https://handmadelytics.com/sewing/current-trends-in-quilting)
- An older, small Quiltblox shop survey: almost 60% of respondents were "interested or might be interested" in EQ products. This measures interest, not use. — [Quiltblox "And the Survey Says"](https://quiltblox.com/and-the-survey-says-quilt-design-software/)

**Brazil (the owner's home market)**
- No numbers on Brazil's patchwork/quilting market were found. Academic sources say "clubinhos de patchwork" (middle-class women's groups) began in the early 1990s, and the Brazil Patchwork Show reached its 10th edition in 2016. — [Dialnet article](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/8653867.pdf); [Anais do Museu Paulista (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/c62b7da78e5c4710b921e681ad01c15f)

### Inferences
- The buyers who spend the most are older and retired, and they buy through local shops. This suits a free, simple tool that shops or teachers can recommend. Self-service paid software is harder to sell to them, because they lean on hand-holding, classes and printed books (see the EQ starter box below).
- The 18% new-quilter share and the 2026 "social media is the main discovery channel" finding point to a younger group who use phones and browsers, and who are poorly served by Windows/Mac desktop software. That is the natural audience for a free browser app.
- Industry figures are US-centric and measured in inches. Metric markets (Europe, Brazil, Australia and others) are not measured at all, which makes them less contested.

### Gaps
- No credible share or count of quilters who use design software. The full Handi Quilter survey reports may contain it but are not public.
- No global hobby-quilting market figure (as opposed to bedding quilts). No Brazil, EU or UK market sizes.
- The 2026 survey's revenue figure was not confirmed from the primary source.
- No good age breakdown of younger quilters (for example the share under 40).

## 2. Adjacent and indirect competitors, and is anyone doing realistic 3D draping on a bed?

### Takeaway
No quilt-design product found offers physics-based 3D draping of the user's own quilt design on a bed or table. The closest are Swatchit's AI-generated "lifestyle scene" previews (a photorealistic image, not 3D physics), professional home-textile 3D tools (Style3D, aimed at brands), and static Photoshop bedding mockups sold to pattern sellers. The adjacent market splits into $5k–$50k longarm automation systems, $900–$1,600 embroidery software, an $80–$290 desktop quilt-design tier, $5–$15 iPad apps, and $50–$100/year browser subscriptions.

### Inferences
- Real-time cloth-physics draping of a design the user made, on a bed or table, appears to be **unique among consumer quilt tools** (as far as search can show). The nearest rival idea is AI "lifestyle scene" images (Swatchit), which look photorealistic but are not physically faithful to size or drape. Pattern sellers who buy Photoshop bed mockups to market their patterns are a possible niche for a "make a listing image" export.
- Longarm software (Pro-Stitcher, CreativeStudio, Quilt Path, IntelliQuilter, Art & Stitch) is tied to $5k–$50k machines and handles quilting stitches, not piecing design or cutting. It is complementary rather than competing. Exporting the quilt's outline and stitch plan in a format these systems read could be a future bridge (not researched here).
- The browser and phone tier (PreQuilt, Quiltler, Swatchit) is the real competition. All three are subscription or freemium, and all are inch-first US products.

### Gaps
- No confirmation of whether EQ8 has any "quilt on a bed" or room view (none appeared in searches).
- Swatchit's paid price and how its scenes work (generative AI versus 3D) are unknown.
- No standalone prices for Gammill CreativeStudio, Art & Stitch or Pro-Stitcher Designer, and no confirmation of any subscriptions in longarm software.
- No specific evidence that quilters use Canva or Excel.
- No pattern-writing tool built for quilt designers (for example a tech-editing tool) came up. Designers appear to use EQ8 plus Illustrator or Inkscape.

## 3. What do quilters most complain about or wish for in design tools?

### Takeaway
The recurring complaints are price, a steep and "frustrating" learning curve (EQ needs paid lessons and books), desktop-only software with no iPad, phone or Chromebook version, and awkward design tools. Many quilters still design on graph paper. Specific gaps include poor metric support (EQ8's metric mode does not convert inch-based blocks), the wish for real piecing and cutting charts with seam allowances, and the effort of scanning one's own fabric correctly. Cloud sync and collaboration complaints were not directly found, but every major desktop tool lacks them.

### Inferences
- The owner's app speaks directly to several documented pain points: free (price), runs in a browser and works offline (no desktop-only lock-in; tablets can use it), metric-native in mm (a real gap in EQ8), fabric photos from a file *or webcam* (simpler than scan, straighten and crop), cutting plan plus real-size A4 templates (seam-allowance and cutting-chart wishes), a guided tour (learning curve), and 3D drape (the "visualizer" wish).
- Since the owner's "Add fabric photos" buttons are currently hidden, closing that gap matters: own-fabric photos are a commonly requested and commonly fiddly feature.
- Cutting optimisation is served by separate single-purpose apps. Building low-waste cutting layouts and yardage into the design tool (as QuiltYield does alone) would cover a need that people currently pay for separately.
- Learning curve is the biggest complaint after price. Guided onboarding and a few starter quilts may matter more than adding more blocks.

### Gaps
- Few first-hand Reddit r/quilting threads came up directly. Most forum evidence is from Quiltingboard and is several years old.
- No direct quotes were found asking for cloud sync or collaboration (sharing with a quilt group, teacher or longarmer). That this is wanted is an inference only.
- No data on how many quilters use metric. No Brazilian or Portuguese-language feedback on quilt software.

## 4. What monetisation models and price points do quilters accept?

### Takeaway
Quilters have long paid one-off desktop licences ($80–$290 for design software, $900–$1,600 for embroidery software) and will pay $5–$15 once for iPad apps. The newer browser and mobile tools are subscriptions at about $50–$100 a year ($7.50–$10/month), with free trials or limited free tiers. Other proven money-makers are bundles with hardware (Brother, BERNINA), books, lessons and starter boxes (EQ), and pattern sales ($6–$25 per PDF). No confirmed fabric-shop affiliate scheme inside a design app was found.

### Inferences
- Price anchors a newcomer will be compared with: free (Quilt Assistant), $5–15 once (iPad), about $50/year (PreQuilt, Quiltler), and about $240 once (EQ8). A free core with an optional paid tier at about $30–60/year, or a one-time "supporter" licence of about $20–50, fits these anchors. The natural paid features are things that cost money to run, such as cloud sync and photo storage, plus perhaps high-resolution "listing image" renders from the 3D view.
- Since 61% prefer their local quilt shop, a shop or teacher channel (shop-branded fabric libraries, class handouts made with the app, or a link to buy the fabric shown) may matter more than app-store discovery. That is an inference; no app-to-shop affiliate scheme was confirmed.
- A tier for pattern designers (as PreQuilt Market does) fits the owner's real-size PDF templates and 3D previews. It would let designers produce pattern pages and marketing images, replacing Photoshop bedding mockups.
- Paid lessons and books show that quilters pay for learning. Free video tutorials and a guided tour are a competitive advantage over EQ's paid add-ons.

### Gaps
- No vendor-confirmed PreQuilt, Quiltler or Swatchit pricing (all from third parties or store listings).
- No data on conversion rates or revenue for quilt software companies. Electric Quilt is private.
- No confirmed fabric-manufacturer or fabric-shop affiliate or partner program inside a quilt design app. EQ's licensed fabric libraries were not researched. A search on PreQuilt and fabric makers returned only Moda / Ruby Star Society retail pages.
- No quilting-specific survey on willingness to pay or attitudes to subscriptions.
- No pricing evidence for Brazil or other non-US markets (purchasing power, local-currency pricing).
