# US online quilt fabric buyers: who they are and how they decide

Method note (2026-11-07): research used web search only. Direct page fetches of craftindustryalliance.org and
fabshopnet.com failed (those hosts are not allowed in this session's network settings), so figures from those sites
come from search-result excerpts of the pages, not from reading the full pages. The full Handi Quilter survey tables
are not public anywhere (Handi Quilter only shares a summary). Treat every number as "as reported in the source's
summary" and check it against the original before publishing.

## Quilter demographics: age, gender, income, experience; younger quilters; time and money spent

### Takeaway
The core US quilter who buys fabric is a woman in her 60s, often retired, in a comfortable household ($75K+ income), who
started quilting seriously in her mid-to-late 40s. A small group of "dedicated" quilters makes most of the spending (about
60–72% of it, in the Quilting in America surveys). Younger quilters are arriving through social media, but no reliable
quilting-specific number measures how many.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 (Handi Quilter consumer survey, presented by Sarah Watts at h+h americas on 8 May 2026):** the typical quilter is
  over 60 (average age 67), retired, and took up the craft seriously in her mid-to-late 40s. The write-up says past surveys
  may skew older because of how they are distributed, and that "the core consumer hasn't dramatically changed, the context
  around them is shifting". It suggests shops offer smaller, affordable items such as beginner-friendly kits for newcomers —
  [Craft Industry Alliance, Quilting Industry Consumer Survey Results 2026](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)
- **2025 (Handi Quilter "Quilter's Survey", collected February 2025, before the tariff announcements):** more than 1.8 million
  quilters were invited by email through five leading brands, and more than 30,000 answered. The average quilter is a woman
  in her 60s, retired, with household income above $75K. 77% spend the same or more time and money than in previous years —
  [FabShop News, Today's Quilting Trends, June 2025 (PDF)](https://fabshopnet.com/downloads/Todays-Quilting-Trends-June2025.pdf);
  [Craft Industry Alliance, Quilting Trends Survey Results 2025](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/)
- **2024 (Handi Quilter survey, as summarised):** the average quilter began quilting regularly at age 48. A reader comment
  notes the full survey results are not available; Handi Quilter shares only a summary —
  [Craft Industry Alliance, Quilting Trends Survey Results 2025](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/)
- **2021 (same survey series):** sent to more than 1 million quilters in February 2021, with about 30,000 replies —
  [Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/?p=42839)
- **2014 (Quilting in America, F+W / Quilts Inc.):** "dedicated quilters" were 12.2% of quilting households but made 60.4% of
  industry spending. Profile: female, 64, household income $101,080, spends $3,296 a year on quilting, and 88% have a room
  set aside for sewing or quilting —
  [QIA summary 2014 (PDF, FabShop)](https://fabshopnet.com/wp-content/uploads/downloads/QIA_summary_2014.pdf)
- **2017 (Quilting in America, last edition found):** market $3.7 billion. "Dedicated quilters" (spending more than $500 a
  year) made 72.2% of spending. Their average age was 63 (down from 64), and they had quilted for 19 years. The under-45 group
  had an average household income of $98,000. The original report has since been taken down, so these come from secondary
  write-ups — [While She Naps](https://whileshenaps.com/how-quilt-market-could-invest-in-its-own-revival-by-catering-to-online-retailers);
  [QuiltingHub, Dedicated Quilters](https://www.quiltinghub.com/Articles/ArticleID/474)
- **Earlier comparisons:** in 2010, 6.2% of quilters were "dedicated" and made 69% of spending. In 2006 the dedicated
  quilter was 59 with an $87,026 household income, spending $2,304 a year —
  [QuiltingInAmerica 2010 (PDF)](https://www.longarmuniversity.com/PDFs/WA-Legis/QuiltingInAmerica-2010.pdf);
  [Black Threads blog, 2006](https://blackthreads.blogspot.com/2006/11/quilting-in-america-2006.html)
- **Market size, 2026:** about $4.5 billion a year with growth visibly slowing. Consumers have become selective and are
  sewing from their own stash — [Craft Industry Alliance, Market Research](https://craftindustryalliance.org/category/marketresearch/)
- **Younger crafters:** Axios (December 2024), citing TikTok, reported that "grandma hobbies" such as needlepoint and crochet
  are taking off with younger people; #needlepoint posts rose 400% in the first 10 months of 2024 over 2023. This is not a
  quilting figure — [Axios Seattle, 2024-12-11](https://www.axios.com/local/seattle/2024/12/11/knitting-needlepoint-crafts-grandma-hobbies-tiktok)
- **Younger crafters:** the US Chamber cites Mintel (2025): 71% of consumers call themselves crafters, and Gen Z and
  millennials are driving renewed interest in crafts such as quilting —
  [US Chamber of Commerce, "Stress fuels crafts market"](https://www.uschamber.com/co/good-company/launch-pad/stress-fuels-crafts-market)
- **Younger crafters:** YPulse (2025-03-10) reports that hands-on "granny hobbies" are among Gen Z's favourite activities —
  [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2025/03/10/hands-on-granny-hobbies-are-gen-zs-new-favorite-activities/)
- **Younger crafters:** the industry's own trade fair ran a 2025 session titled "Engaging young millennial quilters: a vital
  and expanding demographic" (Pam Selkirk, Andover Fabrics) —
  [h+h americas 2025 session](https://koelnmesse.swoogo.com/hha25/session/2807379/engaging-young-millennial-quilters-a-vital-and-expanding-demographic.-pam-selkirk-of-pam-selkirk-designs-with-andover-fabrics)
- **Younger crafters:** a local TV report says quilting is coming back with a younger generation thanks to TikTok —
  [KTVB](https://ktvb.com/article/news/local/idaho-today/quilting-is-making-a-comeback-to-a-younger-generation-thanks-to-tiktok/277-9e939513-d55a-46df-860f-1786b2cd26a5)
- **Unreliable figure:** Gitnux claims "Gen Z quilters grew by 35% from 2020–2023" and "11 million Americans identify as
  quilters in 2023", but gives no primary source and its numbers don't agree with each other. Don't use them —
  [Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/quilting-industry-statistics/)

### Inferences
- The person who decides on, and pays for, fabric online is mostly an experienced, affluent woman of 60 or more with a
  dedicated sewing room and a stash. A tool has to suit her: readable text, no jargon, and real fabric names.
- The 2024–2026 surveys are emailed through brands' customer lists, so they over-represent older, already-engaged
  quilters. Younger, social-media-first quilters are probably under-counted by the very data the industry uses.
- Spending is concentrated in the "dedicated" group, so planning tools that help with big purchases (whole quilts,
  backing, bundles) touch most of the money.

### Gaps
- No Modern Quilt Guild member survey with published age or income results turned up. MQG ran a SurveyMonkey survey in
  2012 ([form](https://www.surveymonkey.com/r/MQG2012Survey)), but no results were found.
- No Quilting in America edition after 2017 was found; the series appears to have stopped.
- No reliable 2024–2026 figure for the number of US quilters, the share under 45, or average annual spend. The last hard
  spend figure is $3,296 a year per dedicated quilter (2014).
- Time spent quilting per week or year: no current figure found.

## Digital habits: Instagram, YouTube, Pinterest, TikTok, Facebook groups; how quilters discover fabric and patterns

### Takeaway
YouTube tutorials (above all Missouri Star's), Facebook groups and Instagram are the main places quilters learn and get
ideas; Pinterest is used to look for patterns, and TikTok is how younger people are coming to the craft. Missouri Star shows
that video tutorials can turn directly into fabric sales. Hard, representative numbers per platform are missing.

### Cited Findings
- **Missouri Star Quilt Company:** YouTube's own blog calls it the largest seller of pre-cut quilting fabric in the world.
  By 2012 the channel had more than 25,000 subscribers —
  [YouTube Official Blog, "Missouri Star Quilt Company: Our YouTube Story" (2017)](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/missouri-star-quilt-company-our-youtube_7/)
- **Missouri Star (2019 press release):** 550,000 YouTube subscribers worldwide, and about 90% of revenue earned through its
  website — [PR Newswire, 2019](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/some-companies-grow-jobs-in-america-brick-by-brick-missouri-star-quilt-co-is-doing-it-quilt-by-quilt-300843329.html)
- **Missouri Star revenue:** estimates conflict, from "upwards of $40 million" to "nine figures", and none is audited —
  [American Lifestyle Magazine](https://americanlifestylemag.com/?p=42076); [About Missouri Star](https://www.missouriquiltco.com:443/pages/about-us)
- **Small survey of a shop's own audience:** Facebook was the top source of quilting inspiration, with Instagram and YouTube
  close behind; Pinterest ranked lower than expected. 84 respondents learn new techniques online (YouTube, blogs,
  newsletters). The sample is self-selected —
  [Rope and Anchor Trading Co., Quilters' Community Survey](https://www.ropeandanchortradingco.com/blog/quilters-community-survey)
- **Creator survey of her own followers (2019):** people preferred whichever platform they already followed her on —
  [Christa Quilts, Social Media Survey results](https://christaquilts.com/2019/11/14/social-media-survey-the-results/)
- **Market data, June 2026:** younger sewers are introduced to sewing and textile crafts mostly through social media
  (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Pinterest) —
  [FabShop, Fabric and Quilting Market Data and Stats, June 2026 (PDF)](https://fabshopnet.com/downloads/Fabric-and-Quliting-Market-Data-and-Stats-June2026.pdf)
- **Marketing guide for quilting businesses (February 2026):** quilters search Pinterest for patterns and class ideas, and
  TikTok works as a search tool ("best ruler for quilting", "binding tips") —
  [Hey Tori, social media for quilting businesses, Feb 2026](https://www.heytori.tech/post/what-s-new-in-social-media-this-month-february-2026-quilting-business-edition)
- **Pattern discovery:** quilters find patterns through Pinterest, fabric makers' websites (free and paid patterns),
  r/quilting and Facebook groups — [Hailey Stitches, How to find quilt patterns online](https://haileystitches.com/how-to-find-quilt-patterns-online/)
- **Platform directories:** Feedspot lists quilting YouTube channels and TikTok creators active in 2026 —
  [Feedspot, quilting YouTube channels](https://videos.feedspot.com/quilting_youtube_channels/);
  [Feedspot, quilting TikTok influencers](https://creators.feedspot.com/quilting_tiktok_influencers/)

### Inferences
- Discovery runs from content to shopping cart: a tutorial or a feed post shows a finished quilt in a named fabric line, and
  the viewer buys that line, often as a precut. Being able to "try it in my own quilt" fits right after that moment of
  inspiration.
- Facebook groups matter more for the 60+ core; Instagram and TikTok matter more for younger quilters.

### Gaps
- No representative US survey (2024–2026) giving the share of quilters using each platform.
- No current Missouri Star subscriber count or revenue from a reliable source.

## Purchase journey online: precuts, kits, whole collection vs single prints, minimum cuts, cut-to-order, shipping thresholds, order value

### Takeaway
Most quilters still say they prefer local quilt shops, but about a fifth to a quarter prefer to buy fabric online, and the
JOANN closures of 2025 pushed more buying online. Precuts and curated bundles from one collection are the main online
products, because they take the coordination guesswork away. Cut fabric is usually final sale. Free shipping starts around
$59–$100.

### Cited Findings
- **2025 survey:** 64% prefer to buy quilting supplies at a local shop (up 3 points), and 13% prefer online. For fabric and
  thread, 51% prefer local and 22% prefer online. Inflation is pushing quilters to use their stash —
  [Craft Industry Alliance, Quilting Trends Survey Results 2025](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/)
- **Another CIA breakdown (year not clear in the excerpt):** 48% buy fabric most often at a local quilt shop and 25% online.
  For notions, online leads 36% to 30% — [Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/?p=60826)
- **2025 trend:** online purchases are falling after the pandemic as quilters return to tactile experiences —
  [FabShop News, June 2025 (PDF)](https://fabshopnet.com/downloads/Todays-Quilting-Trends-June2025.pdf)
- **2026 trend:** the closure of all JOANN stores in 2025 raised online fabric buying and helped independent retailers —
  [FabShop, Market Data and Stats, June 2026 (PDF)](https://fabshopnet.com/downloads/Fabric-and-Quliting-Market-Data-and-Stats-June2026.pdf)
- **Conflict in the data:** the June 2025 summary says online buying is falling, while the June 2026 one says it rose after
  JOANN closed. These may describe different years (the survey before the closures, then after).
- **2020 survey:** fabric was the most sought-after product. 66% preferred to buy fabric and thread from an independent
  quilt shop and 57% preferred online; respondents could choose more than one —
  [Craft Industry Alliance, Quilting Trends Survey Results 2020](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-trends-survey-results-2020/)
- **2017 (secondary):** 68% of dedicated quilters bought fabric, batting or thread online, and 97% bought in person. A
  commentator says the data disproves the idea that online buyers are mainly millennials —
  [While She Naps](https://whileshenaps.com/how-quilt-market-could-invest-in-its-own-revival-by-catering-to-online-retailers)
- **Precuts:** an academic paper citing Quilting in America names precut fabrics as a key, growing innovation —
  [Iowa State Digital Press (ITAA proceedings)](https://iastatedigitalpress.com/itaa/article/13496/galley/14228/view)
- **Precuts, Missouri Star:** says it helped standardise precut fabric, which "revolutionized the quilting industry" —
  [Missouri Star, Precut Fabrics](https://missouriquiltco.com/pages/precut-fabrics)
- **Precut sizes:** fat quarter 18" × 22", jelly roll strips 2.5" wide (usually 40 strips), charm squares 5" × 5", layer cake
  10" × 10" — [Shabby Fabrics, Precuts](https://www.shabbyfabrics.com/c85/);
  [Just Sew Happy, 5-inch squares](https://justsewhappy.com/collections/precuts/5inchsquares)
- **Precuts from one line:** sellers say mixing precuts from the same line makes the fabrics work together —
  [My Soul Stitchery, Pre-cut fabric](https://www.mysoulstitchery.com/collections/precut-fabric)
- **Curated bundles (2025, one online shop's survey):** 81% want curated bundles of prints with coordinating solids —
  [Suzy Quilts, What Quilters Want](https://suzyquilts.com/what-quilters-want-in-2023/) (note: this URL says 2023 while the
  search excerpt said 2025; check the year before citing)
- **Shipping, Missouri Star:** $5.99 flat on orders up to $58.99; free standard shipping from $59 —
  [Missouri Star, Shipping & Returns](https://missouriquiltco.com/pages/shipping-returns)
- **Shipping, other shops:** free over $99 or $100 —
  [Gather Here, Shipping + Returns](https://gatherhereonline.com/pages/shipping-and-returns);
  [My Favorite Quilt Store](https://myfavoritequiltstore.com/llms.txt)
- **Minimum cuts vary:** one US shop's minimum is 1/2 yard, with no swatches, quarter-yard cuts or fat quarters cut from the
  bolt; another cuts a half yard if a fat quarter is out of stock —
  [Fabricworm FAQ](https://fabricworm.com/pages/faqs); [Cottoneer Fabrics FAQ](https://www.cottoneerfabrics.com/faqs/)

### Inferences
- Precuts and bundles from one collection are, among other things, the industry's way of handling "will these look
  together?": the designer has already chosen the fabrics. A tool that previews a chosen precut or bundle in a real quilt
  layout would match how people already buy.
- Free shipping around $59–$100 pushes people to add more to one order, which favours buying a whole quilt's worth at once,
  so working out the yardage helps the shop's order value too.

### Gaps
- No published average order value for online quilt fabric shops was found.
- Fat Quarter Shop's and Connecting Threads' own shipping, minimum-cut and returns terms did not appear in search results.
- No figure on how many buyers choose a whole collection or bundle versus single prints, or on how many buy kits.

## Pain points: colour on screen, no touch, print scale, how fabrics look together, how much to buy, returns on cut fabric, sold-out collections

### Takeaway
The recurring pains are: colour looks different on screen; you can't feel the fabric or judge the size of a print; it's
hard to know whether fabrics go together; cut fabric can't be returned, so a mistake is permanent; and a line can sell out
before you go back for more. Buyers cope with swatches, buying extra, and sticking to precuts from one line.

### Cited Findings
- **Colour on screen (forum):** "hard to tell the exact color of fabric you buy online"; zooming in on a swatch can make it
  look like a different colour; what you see depends on your monitor —
  [Quiltingboard, "Buying fabric online"](https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/buying-fabric-online-t218902-4.html);
  [Quiltingboard, colour misrepresentation in online fabric](https://quiltingboard.com/main-f1/color-misrepresentation-online-fabric-t312959.html);
  [Quiltingboard, "How true are fabric colors online"](https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/printfriendly-how-true-fabric-colors-line-t52790.html?pp=40)
- **Colour on screen (shops):** fabrics are usually lighter and brighter in person than on screen —
  [Fabric Company, True-to-colour online fabric shop guide](https://fabriccompany.com/blogs/updates/true-to-color-online-fabric-shop);
  [Linda's Electric Quilters, Online vs in-store fabric buying](https://lindas.com/pages/online-vs-in-store-fabric-buying-quilters)
- **Coping with colour:** order swatches for colour-critical projects; buy only where returns are lenient; check reviews for
  colour accuracy — [Linda's Electric Quilters](https://lindas.com/blogs/tips-and-tricks/quilting-fabric-for-sale-online);
  [Little Fabric Shop, fabric sample product](https://littlefabricshop.com/products/fabric-sample)
- **Judging whether fabrics go together:** an experienced forum member argues that if fabrics go together on screen they
  generally go together in person, even if the shade is slightly off —
  [Quiltingboard](https://www.quiltingboard.com/showthread.php?p=5048796)
- **Not buying enough:** the bigger risk is buying too little of a fabric, because the line may be gone when you go back —
  [Linda's Electric Quilters, Online vs in-store](https://lindas.com/pages/online-vs-in-store-fabric-buying-quilters)
- **Sold out:** retailer listings show many layer cakes, charm packs and jelly rolls sold out or with "only 2 units left" —
  [Patched Works, Precut fabric](https://patchedworks.com/collections/precut-fabric);
  [Just Sew Happy, 5-inch squares](https://justsewhappy.com/collections/precuts/5inchsquares)
- **Returns on cut fabric:** cut yardage can't be returned because it is cut for the order and can't be restocked; precut
  bundles are often returnable within 30 days if unused —
  [Gather Here, Refund policy](https://gatherhereonline.com/policies/refund-policy);
  [Shannon Fabrics, Shipping and Returns](https://shannonfabrics.com/shipping-and-returns) (no returns on cut fabric, custom
  orders or sample yardage); [Little Fabric Shop](https://littlefabricshop.com/pages/shipping-order-details) (all
  by-the-yard sales final); [Revolution Fabrics, Return policy](https://revolutionfabrics.com/pages/return-policy)
- **Stash and caution (2025–2026):** inflation and tariffs make quilters more selective and push them to sew from their stash —
  [Craft Industry Alliance, 2025](https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2025/);
  [Craft Industry Alliance, Market Research](https://craftindustryalliance.org/category/marketresearch/)

### Inferences
- When the fabric can't be returned, the person carries the risk of every wrong guess about colour, the size of a print, how
  fabrics go together, or yardage. A preview that shows the actual fabric in the actual quilt, with yardage worked out,
  removes most of that risk before anything is cut.
- No surveyed figures put a size on print scale or "touch" as pains; they appear only in shop guides and forum posts.

### Gaps
- No r/quilting threads came up in search (Reddit seems not to be indexed by this search tool); the quotes are from
  Quiltingboard forums instead.
- No survey measures the share of online buyers unhappy with colour, or the share of returns or complaints by cause.
- Not found: a quantified figure on how often quilters buy the wrong yardage.

## Quilters wanting to "see the fabric in the quilt before buying"; design tools and mockups before purchase

### Takeaway
There is a clear and growing set of tools built for exactly this: PreQuilt (web and app), Electric Quilt 8 (desktop), and
design boards on shop sites (eQuilter). Their own marketing and reviews describe them as "see the quilt before you cut" and
"buy with more confidence". No survey measures how many quilters want this or use it.

### Cited Findings
- **PreQuilt:** an online tool from Canada to design and preview quilts, with solid-colour swatches matched to real
  manufacturer lines (so you buy those exact colours), a printed-fabric importer (photograph a print in a shop and try it in
  the design), more than 100 blocks, and a fabric calculator —
  [Canadian Quilter, "Visualize your next quilt with PreQuilt"](https://canadianquilter.com/members-only-studio/visualize-your-next-quilt-with-prequilt);
  [While She Naps, "PreQuilt is a sleek new design tool for quilters"](https://whileshenaps.com/prequilt-is-a-sleek-new-design-tool-for-quilters/);
  [Stitched in Color, "Welcome PreQuilt" (July 2024)](https://www.stitchedincolor.com/blog/2024/7/3/welcome-prequilt);
  [The Workroom, "The person behind PreQuilt"](https://theworkroom.ca/blogs/learn/the-person-behind-prequilt)
- **Electric Quilt 8:** lets you "see your quilt on the screen before you cut a piece of fabric", imports fabric photos or
  makers' downloads, and updates yardage as blocks, colours, borders or layout change —
  [Sewing Parts Online, EQ8](https://www.sewingpartsonline.com/products/electric-quilt-8-quilt-design-software);
  [Pieced Just Sew, "EQ8: not just for designers" (2020)](https://piecedjustsew.blogspot.com/2020/07/eq8-not-just-for-designers.html)
- **Comparison (review):** four years of PreQuilt's Basic annual plan cost about $200, still less than EQ8; EQ8 suits
  experienced designers, PreQuilt is easier to justify "when the goal is visualizing fabrics and buying with more confidence" —
  [omidsaffari.com, PreQuilt review (pt-BR)](https://omidsaffari.com/pt-br/blog/programa-para-patchwork-prequilt)
- **Forum demand:** a quilter asks for a "quilt visualizer"; answers point to EQ and to eQuilter's design board, noting it is
  limited to that shop's stock — [Quiltingboard, "Quilt visualizer?"](https://www.quiltingboard.com/showthread.php?p=8386834)
- **Mobile:** an App Store quilt-design app has a free version (four fabric swatches, twelve classic patterns) with the full
  version unlocked in the app — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6755566364)

### Inferences
- The idea is proven: makers of tools market them by the same promise (see it before you buy or cut). What is missing is
  the link from preview to purchase across shops, since eQuilter's board shows only its own stock and PreQuilt prints come
  from the quilter's own photos.
- No found tool shows the quilt draped on a bed in 3D; that seems to be a point of difference, but it was not checked
  systematically.

### Gaps
- No survey or poll with a percentage of quilters who want to "see the fabric in the quilt before buying" or who use design
  software before buying. The evidence is the existence of the tools, their marketing and forum questions.
- No user numbers for PreQuilt or EQ8 found.
