# Design-to-buy: tools that link "try this fabric in a quilt" with buying it (US, 2026)

**Method note for the report writer.** As in the earlier competitor report, the page-fetch tool could not reach any site from this session (quiltler.com, prequilt.com and wyldwoodcreative.com all failed to resolve). Every finding below comes from web-search result text and the URL it came from, not from opened pages. Many sources are the companies' own marketing or help pages. Prices and features should be checked on the live pages before anyone repeats them publicly. This note does not repeat the general competitor matrix in `research/reports/Quilt design software competitors.md`. It covers only the link between designing or previewing and buying.

## 1. Do shops offer "visualize this fabric" or quilt-mockup tools on product pages?

### Takeaway
Very few US fabric shops do. The two clear cases are **Quiltster**, a planner plus marketplace where shops turn a planned quilt into a kit you can buy, and **eQuilter's Free Pattern Designer**, which lets you drag shop fabrics into a pattern and add it to the cart. Missouri Star, Fat Quarter Shop, Shabby Fabrics and Spoonflower showed no quilt visualizer in the results. Spoonflower's previews only show one print at scale on a product.

### Cited Findings
- **eQuilter (large US online shop):** its pattern pages let a shopper "customize your pattern by dragging thumbnail images to the pattern". You can "order the pattern as configured, or customize it to your liking with any of our thousands of fabrics", then click Add to Cart. Fabrics saved to the shopper's Wish List appear in a "Fabric Selector" below the pattern, and this needs a login. Pattern pages show "Fabric Requirements" for the top and optional backing. — [eQuilter help: free pattern designer](https://www.equilter.com/help/free_pattern_designer); [eQuilter pattern page example](https://www.equilter.com/pattern/1743/elegant-floral-kitty); [eQuilter pattern page example](https://www.equilter.com/pattern/1020/dragon-wings)
- The search results did not say whether the patterns are free or how many shoppers use the designer. — same sources (gap)
- **Quiltster (planner plus marketplace):** anyone can buy kits and supplies from partner quilt shops "across the U.S." without a subscription. A subscription is needed for the Quilt Planner (design, try fabrics, yardage charts). — [Quiltster: How it Works](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=13004); [What is Quiltster?](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=11071)
- In the planner you can filter to "Products I Can Buy". On adding the project to the cart, you get a warning if an item is out of stock or "there is not enough fabric in stock to make your quilt", and you're asked to choose replacements. — [Quiltster: Add Fabric](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=11631); [Buying Your Quilt Planner Project](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=4699)
- Shops can make "Quiltster Kits" by creating a project in the planner and converting it to a listing. Customers then "see exactly how the fabrics look in the finished quilt before purchasing". "Classic Kits" are listed without the planner. — [How to use Quiltster as a Tool for your Shop](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=2406); [How it Works](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=10143)
- Quiltster's subscription is quoted at US$9.99/month or US$99/year on a seller post (possibly dated). — [Quiltster](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=9717)
- Quiltster is tied to Quiltworx (Judy Niemeyer paper-pieced patterns). Quiltworx Box Club subscribers get a free planner trial that rolls into a paid plan, and a 72×72 Quiltworx kit was listed at US$225 for fabric only or US$330 with the pattern (an old listing). — [Quiltworx Box Club + Quiltster](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=8595); [Quiltster pattern listing](https://www.quiltster.com/pattern/240451)
- **Wyldwood Creative (small shop):** its "Design Tools" page links to a separate developer's "Quilt Mockup Tool" (Instagram @quilt.mockup.tool) and asks visitors to donate to the developer's ko-fi. The shop also sells custom kits by request, priced at the retail price of the fabric plus a US$15–20 labor fee, with no discounts. — [Wyldwood Design Tools](https://wyldwoodcreative.com/pages/design-tools); [Story of the Quilt Mockup Tool](https://wyldwoodcreative.com/blogs/inspiration-and-tutorials/the-story-of-the-quilt-mockup-tool); [Wyldwood Custom Quilt Kit](https://wyldwoodcreative.com/products/custom-quilt-kit)
- **Floyd the Fox moodboard:** a blog post mentions a shop's moodboard tool for rearranging fabrics. A commenter wrote that mood boards "would be a great add for all online businesses. Much easier than flipping through the cart". This is anecdotal demand from a shopper. — [Stitched in Color: Once upon a Fox](https://stitchedincolor.substack.com/p/once-upon-a-fox)
- **Spoonflower:** product images are "renderings to show how a design will look on a product" at the correct scale. Preview windows have rulers to judge the pattern at the chosen size, and an "Also available in…" link shows the same design on other products. Help pages warn that the only way to know how colours will look is a physical sample, so they sell test swatches. — [Spoonflower help](https://support.spoonflower.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030324532); [Spoonflower help](https://support.spoonflower.com/hc/en-us/articles/36131346905357); [Spoonflower help](https://support.spoonflower.com/hc/en-us/articles/204474630)
- Spoonflower's quilt-related "design" tool is the Fill-A-Yard template, including a "2 Yard Cheater Quilt Template", which prints a pieced-looking top. It is not a visualizer for piecing with bought fabric. No 2026 Spoonflower AI quilt preview was found. — [Spoonflower blog](https://blog.spoonflower.com/?p=21723); [Spoonflower cheater quilt shop](https://spoonflower.com/en/shop/cheater-quilt)
- A Spoonflower blog post tells designers to make their own mock-ups, for example from stock photos, if Spoonflower's renders "aren't enough". — [Spoonflower blog](https://blog.spoonflower.com/?p=38796)
- Searches for Missouri Star Quilt Co. and Fat Quarter Shop design tools returned only physical rulers and templates, with no online quilt designer or fabric visualizer. — [Missouri Star tools](https://www.missouriquiltco.com/pages/tools-and-templates); [Fat Quarter Shop](https://www.fatquartershop.com/rapid-fire-lemoyne-star-tool)

### Inferences
- Shops clearly see value in "see it in the quilt, then buy the kit". The working examples, though, sit inside a closed catalogue: Quiltster's partner shops and Quiltworx patterns, and eQuilter's own stock and patterns. No shop-neutral tool was found that lets a quilter design freely and then buy the exact fabrics from a shop of their choice.
- Quiltster's out-of-stock warning shows a practical problem any design-to-buy link must solve: fabric runs out, and collections are short-lived.
- The biggest US online shops (Missouri Star, Fat Quarter Shop) seem to sell through video tutorials and kits rather than visualizers. That fits the earlier finding that Missouri Star's app is tutorials plus shop.

### Gaps
- None of the shop tools' usage or sales figures were found.
- It is not confirmed whether Shabby Fabrics, Hancock's of Paducah, Fat Quarter Shop or Connecting Threads have any "audition fabric" feature on product pages, because the pages couldn't be opened.

## 2. Design software with fabric libraries and shop links: do they earn commission or have "buy these fabrics" buttons?

### Takeaway
Only Quiltler (an embeddable designer with "where to buy" links, sold to fabric companies) and Quiltster (a marketplace commission of 10–15%) were found to make money from the buying step. EQ8, PreQuilt and Swatchit use real manufacturer fabric names so you can buy them, but no buy buttons or affiliate links turned up. One September 2026 review says plainly that PreQuilt is "not a fabric marketplace".

### Cited Findings
- **Quiltler for fabric companies:** an embeddable version of its designer lets shoppers lay a company's prints into a quilt to decide how many yards to buy. The fabric company gets a shopping list of its fabrics with where-to-buy links, and the embed carries only that company's fabrics. Analytics count where-to-buy clicks. — [Quiltler: Fabric Companies](https://quiltler.com/fabric-companies)
- Quiltler's live demo dashboard shows 324 where-to-buy clicks against 4,634 designer views, about 7% click-through. This is a sample dashboard on a vendor page, not verified data. — [Quiltler partner demo](https://quiltler.com/partner-demo)
- Quiltler pricing conflicts across its own pages: paid plans "start at $29/month for 15 collections" with own branding (demo page), and a "Brand" tier is listed at US$79/month for unlimited collections and a quiltler.com subdomain page (fabric-companies page). The free plan shows a "Designed with Quiltler" credit. — [Quiltler partner demo](https://quiltler.com/partner-demo); [Quiltler: Fabric Companies](https://quiltler.com/fabric-companies)
- Quiltler also publishes a "Fabric Companies That Don't Use AI (2026)" list, a search-ranking page aimed at the same buyers. — [Quiltler](https://quiltler.com/fabric-companies-that-dont-use-ai)
- **Quiltster fees:** one terms page says Quiltster keeps 10% of each quilt kit sale. Two pages give 15% on fabric sales. Sellers also pay a subscription (price not found). For now sellers "can list and sell Quilt Kits" only. — [Quiltster Seller Terms](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=5155); [Quiltster Seller Agreement](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=10470); [Who Fulfills Marketplace Orders?](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=9996)
- Quiltster has a "Pattern Designer Partnership" and fabric-company partners who supply digital versions of their collections. Its pitch to fabric companies is that sales teams can show collections in kit form before they reach shelves, and make kit variations for marketing without sewing a sample. — [Quiltster: Our Partners](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=11388); [Pattern Designer Partnership](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=12277)
- **PreQuilt:** swatches "come from Kona, Art Gallery, Moda, Tilda and lots more". Its colours represent actual solid fabric lines, so you can plan and then buy those exact colours. Buying happens elsewhere. — [Stitched in Color: Welcome PreQuilt](https://www.stitchedincolor.com/blog/2024/7/3/welcome-prequilt); [While She Naps](https://whileshenaps.com/prequilt-is-a-sleek-new-design-tool-for-quilters/)
- A September 2026 review calls PreQuilt "planning software rather than a fabric marketplace, pattern-instruction library, or calibrated proofing system". It says the tool "narrows the candidates; it does not certify color", and quotes Basic at US$50/year. — [Omid Saffari review](https://omidsaffari.com/blog/prequilt-review-for-fabric-color-planning)
- PreQuilt is a Canadian web app by Laura Henneberry and Gar Liu. — [The Workroom](https://theworkroom.ca/blogs/learn/the-person-behind-prequilt)
- **Electric Quilt (EQ8):** it ships with about 6,200 manufacturer fabrics and sells "Stash" fabric downloads. Stash 2026-02 adds 1,000+ new fabrics from FreeSpirit, Northcott, Tilda and Hoffman. A 2021 article quotes US$7.95 each, three times a year, and there is a free monthly collection. The shop says to "ask for EQ8 at your local quilt shop". No buy-this-fabric links or affiliate scheme were found. — [EQ shop](https://electricquilt.com/online-shop/); [Electric Quilt](https://electricquilt.com/); [On Point Quilter: Fabric Sources for EQ8](https://www.onpointquilter.com/blog/fabric-sources-for-eq8)
- **Swatchit:** calculates per-colour yardage "with Kona Cotton fabric name matching" and makes share links. Its App Store listing doesn't mention a shopping list or buy links. — [App Store: Swatchit](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6760553382)
- **QuiltFit:** its shopping list turns estimates into a checklist "with fabric names, colors, quantities and optional prices". It has no shop links. **Sew Help Me** makes shopping lists and can store links to favourite shops. — [App Store: QuiltFit](https://apps.apple.com/pl/app/id6776541705); [Sew Help Me](https://mwm.ai/apps/sew-help-me/1515971634)
- **AI tools:** a low-quality article describes "Quiltink" as generating a pattern with layouts, colour guides and yardage from an image or text. No buying link was mentioned, and the claim is unverified. — [okboomer-ai](https://okboomer-ai.pages.dev/article/ai-quilt-design)

### Inferences
- The pattern is that **consumer tools charge the quilter, while B2B tools charge the fabric company or take a cut from shops.** Quiltler sells the designer to manufacturers as marketing, at about US$29–79/month. Quiltster takes 10–15% of kit and fabric sales. No consumer design app was found taking affiliate commission on fabric.
- A "shopping list with fabric names" (QuiltFit, Swatchit's Kona names, PreQuilt's real swatches) is the common substitute for a buy button. Quilters seem to carry the list to a shop or a search box themselves.
- For Block & Bind, a low-risk first step could be a shopping list with manufacturer names and SKUs, plus optional "find it" search links. A shop or manufacturer embed (Quiltler's model) is the proven way to charge businesses.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found for affiliate programmes (Missouri Star, Fat Quarter Shop or Amazon affiliate links) being used inside a design app. Shop affiliate rates for fabric were not researched.
- Quiltler's real customer list (which fabric companies embed it) was not found.
- Quilt Wizard, Quiltography and Patchwork Pal had no buying link in any result. They seem to have none, but this is unconfirmed.

## 3. Fabric manufacturers' digital tools and "virtual quilt" features

### Takeaway
None of the manufacturers searched (Robert Kaufman, Moda, Riley Blake, Art Gallery Fabrics) were found offering an interactive quilt designer of their own. They use lookbooks, shop locators and calculators, and they lend their swatches to third parties (EQ Stash, PreQuilt, Quiltster, Quiltler's embed).

### Cited Findings
- Art Gallery Fabrics publishes digital lookbooks: "curated image composites and a direct link to the patterns", described as "the best, most in-depth look at our fabric collections". These are static, not interactive. — [AGF Fabric Lookbooks](https://liveartgalleryfabrics.com/fabric-lookbooks/); [AGF on Issuu](https://issuu.com/artgalleryfab/stacks)
- Robert Kaufman has a "where to buy" shop locator. Its known app is QuiltingCalc, a calculator (covered in the earlier report). No Kona colour or palette app was found. — [Robert Kaufman: Where to Buy](https://www.robertkaufman.com/where_to_buy/)
- Searches for Riley Blake and Moda design tools returned only shop listings and preview videos of new collections. — [Generations Quilt Patterns](https://www.generations-quilt-patterns.com/riley-blake-fabric.html); [YouTube: new Moda & Riley Blake releases](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XND-3NPJOAA)
- Manufacturer swatches reach quilters through EQ's Stash packs (FreeSpirit, Northcott, Tilda, Hoffman) and through Quiltster's fabric-company partners. — [EQ shop](https://electricquilt.com/online-shop/); [Quiltster: Our Partners](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=11388)

### Inferences
- Manufacturers seem happy to license or supply swatch images rather than build tools. That makes "manufacturer fabrics in Block & Bind" a partnership or data question more than an engineering one. The CLAUDE.md note says the app's 1,500 fabrics are drawn by code and the makers' logos have no permission yet. Real swatches would need the same permission talks EQ and Quiltster must have had.

### Gaps
- Moda's B2B digital catalogue (for shops) was not found in the results. Whether manufacturers charge or pay for inclusion in EQ, PreQuilt or Quiltster is unknown.

## 4. Evidence of results: conversion lift from visualizers

### Takeaway
No quilt- or quilting-fabric-specific conversion data was found. The only number near quilting is Quiltler's demo figure of about 7% click-through to where-to-buy, which is vendor sample data. Home furnishing and furniture visualizer studies claim large lifts (CB2 +7% up to "9x"), but almost all come from vendors and compare self-selected users with non-users.

### Cited Findings
- Quiltler demo: 324 where-to-buy clicks from 4,634 designer views. This is a sample dashboard. — [Quiltler partner demo](https://quiltler.com/partner-demo)
- **DecoratorsBest** (online seller of fabric, wallpaper and furniture) via Roomvo, the vendor: "9x higher purchase rate" for shoppers who used the visualizer, plus a "+67%" figure with an unclear label. This is the closest case to fabric retail found. — [Roomvo case study: DecoratorsBest](https://hello.roomvo.com/gated-case-study-decorators-best); [Roomvo outbound version](https://hello.roomvo.com/outbound-case-study-decorators-best)
- **Heal's** (UK furniture, upholstery options) via Cylindo, the vendor: higher conversion and fewer returns. A summary says AR users were "8x more likely to convert", with unverified method. — [Cylindo: Heal's](https://www.cylindo.com/customer-case-studies/heals/)
- **Wayfair** "View in Room 3D" figures conflict across secondary blogs: a 92% conversion lift (one adds 43% fewer returns), or "3–4x". **IKEA** figures also conflict: a 189% conversion increase with 40% fewer returns, or an "11x purchase likelihood". No primary Wayfair or IKEA source was found. — [Single Grain](https://www.singlegrain.com/digital-marketing/ar-experiences-that-boosted-conversion-rates-by-189/); [7CGI](https://7cgi.com/blog/3d-product-rendering-for-ecommerce/); [The Planner Studio](https://theplanner.studio/blog/augmented-reality-furniture-retail-reduce-returns-increase-confidence/) (which figure comes from which page could not be checked, because the pages couldn't be opened)
- **CB2**, from a 2019 Digital Commerce 360 analysis as reported secondhand: +7% conversion, +21% revenue per visit and +13% average order value against non-AR pages. This is the most modest figure and the likeliest to come from a controlled comparison. — reported in the same search results ([Single Grain](https://www.singlegrain.com/digital-marketing/ar-experiences-that-boosted-conversion-rates-by-189/) / [7CGI](https://7cgi.com/blog/3d-product-rendering-for-ecommerce/)); primary source not opened
- **Shopify** (May 2022): merchants adding 3D content saw "a 94% conversion lift" on average. This is widely quoted and is a platform's own claim. — via [7CGI](https://7cgi.com/blog/3d-product-rendering-for-ecommerce/)
- Other vendor cases: EQ3 (Cylindo) +112% for AR users and twice the spend; Gat Creek (Threekit) +35% conversion and +134% average order value in seven weeks; DFS +112% for 3D products. Vendor-reported return reductions run from about 25% to 40%. — via [Cylindo blog](https://blog.cylindo.com/augmented-reality-furniture-retail-advantages); [iEnhance](https://www.ienhance.co/insights/how-to-increase-conversion-rates-in-ecommerce-with-3d-and-augmented-reality)
- Textile-specific vendor content (Imagine.io, Twinbru, Zolak curtain visualizers) argues that 3D fabric visuals lift online sales. The search results showed no independent numbers. — [Imagine.io](https://resources.imagine.io/industry-news/ar-experiences-improve-conversion-rates-for-textile-brands); [Twinbru](https://blog.twinbru.com/why-3d-fabric-textures-are-the-future-of-e-commerce); [Zolak](https://zolak.tech/blog/what-is-3d-curtain-visualization)
- Spoonflower itself tells customers that screen previews can mislead on colour and sells physical test swatches. That is an admission that visualizers don't settle colour. — [Spoonflower help](https://support.spoonflower.com/hc/en-us/articles/204474630)

### Inferences
- The "users vs non-users" figures (8x, 9x, 11x) mostly measure that engaged shoppers buy more, not that the tool causes it. The CB2 +7% number is the most believable scale for a real lift. For a pitch to shops, "we raise confidence and cut returns" is safer than any multiplier.
- In quilting the main risks are colour and scale, plus the quilter's fear of buying too little or too much. A visualizer tied to true scale and yardage (which Block & Bind's 3D drape and cutting plan already cover) addresses the same doubts the furniture studies claim to reduce. This is reasoning, not evidence.

### Gaps
- No Spoonflower, eQuilter, Quiltster or Missouri Star data on whether previews or planners raise sales.
- No primary Wayfair, IKEA or Shopify document was opened, so the exact wording and method are unconfirmed.

## 5. Failed attempts and lessons

### Takeaway
The clearest failure found is Threadbias (2015). The cost of running a community sank the site, and its design tool was split off as Quilt Canvas. The other lessons are practical ones seen in working tools: stock runs out, colours on screen mislead, and closed catalogues limit reach.

### Cited Findings
- Threadbias closed its community because "the cost to keep the Community running has taken quite a toll financially". The online quilt design software stayed as a separate site, rebuilt as "Quilt Canvas", and Threadbias logins still worked. — [While She Naps news](https://whileshenaps.com/sewing-and-creative-business-news-from-around-the-web-this-week/); [Road Home Quilting](https://roadhomequilting.blogspot.com/2015/08/threadbias-switching-to-quilt-canvas.html)
- Amazon quietly shut down Fabric.com, a large online fabric seller. This is shown in the results as a forum thread only, with no date or detail in the snippet, and needs checking. — [NamePros thread](https://thenamepros.com/threads/amazon-is-quietly-shutting-down-fabric-com.1286678/post-8740524)
- Quiltster builds stock warnings and forced replacement into checkout because a planned fabric may not be in stock in the needed amount. — [Buying Your Quilt Planner Project](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=4699)
- PreQuilt "does not certify color", and Spoonflower recommends physical swatches, so even good visualizers hand the final colour decision to a sample. — [Omid Saffari](https://omidsaffari.com/blog/prequilt-review-for-fabric-color-planning); [Spoonflower help](https://support.spoonflower.com/hc/en-us/articles/204474630)
- Wyldwood's shop mockup tool depends on a volunteer developer funded by ko-fi donations, which is fragile. — [Wyldwood Design Tools](https://wyldwoodcreative.com/pages/design-tools)
- Quiltster is locked largely to Quiltworx patterns and partner shops (see the earlier report), and its seller side currently sells kits only. — [Quiltster Seller Agreement](https://blog.quiltster.com/?p=10470)

### Inferences
- The design-to-buy link seems to work best as a **kit** (one shop, fixed fabrics, a known pattern) rather than as an open "buy anything you designed" cart, because of stock and the variety of shops.
- Small quilt sites die from running costs (Threadbias, and others in the earlier report). A design-to-buy feature that leans on a partner's catalogue and checkout, rather than on Block & Bind holding stock data, keeps costs low.

### Gaps
- No post-mortems were found for Play Crafts, PatternJam or MyWebQuilter, or for any shop that tried and removed a visualizer.
- No data was found on why quilters do or don't buy from a planner (for example, Quiltster's planner-to-kit conversion).
