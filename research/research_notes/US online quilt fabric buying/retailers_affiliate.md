# US online quilting-fabric retailers and affiliate norms (researched 2026-11-07)

Method note: this session could not fetch pages (ui.awin.com, gripsintelligence.com, fabshopnet.com were all blocked by the network allowlist), so every finding below comes from web-search result summaries, not from reading the page itself. Treat rates as "reported by the listing", and confirm them on the network dashboards before using them in a pitch.

## 1. Main online sellers, positioning, revenue and traffic

### Takeaway
A handful of large online-first quilt shops (Fat Quarter Shop, Missouri Star, Shabby Fabrics, Connecting Threads, eQuilter) lead US online quilting-cotton sales, next to general marketplaces (Amazon, Etsy) and print-on-demand (Spoonflower). Joann closed all its stores and its website in 2025, which pushed more fabric buying online and into independent shops. No company publishes audited revenue, and third-party estimates disagree by up to 10x.

### Cited Findings
- Fat Quarter Shop: Grips Intelligence puts its November 2025 e-commerce revenue at about $10.42M, against $3.48M for missouriquiltco.com and $2.11M for shabbyfabrics.com in the same comparison. The search summary did not say whether these are monthly figures, but the November date suggests they are. — [Grips Intelligence, fatquartershop.com](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/retailers/fatquartershop.com); [Grips, shabbyfabrics.com](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/retailers/shabbyfabrics.com)
- Fat Quarter Shop: Similarweb's company profile gives annual revenue of $25M–$50M and about 1.1M visits over 3 months (2026 profile). — [Similarweb company profile](https://www.similarweb.com/es/company/fatquartershop.com); [Similarweb competitors, March 2026](https://www.similarweb.com/website/fatquartershop.com/competitors)
- Fat Quarter Shop: Semrush reports organic search traffic down 4.19% month on month (page updated 2025-11-12). — [Semrush](https://cw.semrush.com/website/fatquartershop.com/overview)
- Shabby Fabrics: Similarweb shows a global rank of about #91,312, a 52.27% bounce rate and 3.63 pages per visit (2026; period not stated). — [Similarweb competitors](https://www.similarweb.com/website/fatquartershop.com/competitors)
- Missouri Star Quilt Co.: revenue estimates conflict. LeadIQ says $100M–$250M with about 231 employees (March 2026); Zippia says $7.9M with 196 employees; Clodura says $5.5M. — [LeadIQ](https://www.leadiq.com/c/missouri-star-quilt-company/5a1d98b82300005c00875b61); [Zippia](https://www.zippia.com/missouri-star-quilt-careers-1555444); [Clodura](https://www.clodura.ai/directory/company/missouri-star-quilt-company)
- Missouri Star had 400+ employees during the pandemic (moved into the warehouse to fill online orders), and Hamilton, MO, drew about 32,000 tourists a month before COVID (March 2021). — [KCUR 2021-03-06](https://www.kcur.org/arts-life/2021-03-06/how-northwest-missouris-quilt-town-has-survived-since-the-pandemic-kept-its-32-000-monthly-tourists-away)
- Missouri Star's growth came from YouTube tutorials (YouTube's own case study, April 2017). — [YouTube blog](https://youtube.googleblog.com/2017/04/missouri-star-quilt-company-our-youtube_7.html)
- Missouri Star runs "Quilter's Cash", a customer rewards program (2 points per $1 spent), which is separate from any affiliate program. — [Quilter's Cash T&C](https://missouriquiltco.com/pages/quilters-cash-terms-conditions)
- Hawthorne Supply Co (formerly Hawthorne Threads): run by a husband and wife in Upstate New York, selling other manufacturers' collections since 2008. — [Hawthorne Our Story](https://hawthornesupplyco.com/our-story)
- Stash Fabrics: an online shop carrying Moda, FIGO and Fableism Supply Co collections (store news feed). — [stashfabrics.com feed](https://www.stashfabrics.com/feed?Page=1)
- eQuilter: offers free shipping on orders over $100 (site banner, 2026). — [equilter.com/about](https://equilter.com/about)
- Joann announced in February 2025 that it would close all ~800 stores, and Joann.com stopped taking orders. Press coverage named Missouri Star, Fabric Mart and Spoonflower as places to buy instead. — [NBC Montana 2025-02-25](https://nbcmontana.com/news/nation-world/fabric-and-craft-retailer-joann-to-go-out-of-business-and-close-all-of-its-stores-02-25-2025); [The Federalist 2025-03-03](https://thefederalist.com/2025/03/03/joann-fabric-is-almost-dead-but-sewing-is-forever/); [KPBS 2025-02-28](https://www.kpbs.org/news/economy/2025/02/28/fabric-giant-joann-is-closing-down-what-happens-to-those-who-relied-on-the-retailer)
- Where quilters buy: in a 2025 survey (Craft Industry Alliance / Quilting Trends, as reported by a secondary site), 64% preferred local shops for supplies and 13% online. For fabric and thread it was 51% local and 22% online. — [tcrcrafts.com (citing CIA, 2025-05-13)](https://tcrcrafts.com/quilting-statistics)
- In the 2020 survey, 66% preferred independent quilt shops for fabric and thread, and 57% said they liked shopping online (up from 51% in 2017). — [Craft Industry Alliance, Quilting Trends Survey 2020](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-trends-survey-results-2020/)
- CIA's 2026 consumer survey (Handi Quilter data) says Gen X quilters buy both online and in stores, and buy online when shop hours don't suit them. It calls younger quilters the biggest growth opportunity. — [CIA consumer survey 2026](https://craftindustryalliance.org/quilting-industry-consumer-survey-results-2026/)

### Inferences
- By the Grips figures, Fat Quarter Shop is probably the largest pure-online quilt-cotton seller by revenue in late 2025, ahead of Missouri Star, even though Missouri Star is better known. Grips only measures one domain, and Missouri Star also has stores and a town of shops, so the gap may be overstated.
- Most quilters still prefer local shops for fabric (about 51% local against 22% online in 2025). For a design app, that makes "send this quilt's fabric list to your own local shop" as important as linking to the big online stores.

### Gaps
- No revenue or traffic figures found for Connecting Threads, eQuilter, Pink Castle, Lark Cottons, Hawthorne or Stash Fabrics.
- No figures found for Amazon, Etsy, Walmart or Hobby Lobby quilting-cotton sales specifically.
- The Grips pages couldn't be opened to check whether the November 2025 numbers are monthly or annual.
- Nothing found on Pink Castle Fabrics or Lark Cottons in this round of searches.

## 2. E-commerce platforms used by independent quilt shops

### Takeaway
Shopify is the default for small fabric shops (with fabric add-ons for selling by fractional yards), and Like Sew is the main niche system built for quilt shops (POS plus online store). The others (Big Cartel, GoDaddy, Wix, Square) turn up only in passing. Shopify's cart permalinks are documented and support a prefilled cart, which is what a "buy this quilt's fabric" return link needs.

### Cited Findings
- Like Sew is built for quilt and fabric shops and handles sales, inventory, classes and marketing, online and in store, as one system. Capterra rates it 4.4 from 28 reviews (2026). — [Capterra Shopify vs Like Sew 2026](https://www.capterra.com/compare/83891-10021014/Shopify-vs-Like-Sew)
- Like Sew's price isn't published ("No pricing info" on GetApp). — [GetApp Like Sew](https://www.getapp.com/sales-software/a/like-sew/)
- Shopify plans start at US$29 a month (Capterra, 2026). — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/compare/83891-10021014/Shopify-vs-Like-Sew)
- "Sew Much Commerce Fabric Stash" is a Shopify app that lets fabric shops sell fabric by fractional yards. — [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/fabric-shop)
- FabShopNet (the industry network) published a guide to Shopify for fabric and quilt shops in 2026, which shows Shopify is common enough in the niche to need one. — [FabShopNet](https://fabshopnet.com/shopify-for-fabric-and-quilt-shops/)
- A quilt-business blog also names Big Cartel (cheap, shop-only site) and GoDaddy (easy, but limited shipping options). — [rachelrossi.design](https://rachelrossi.design/?p=22573)
- Shopify cart permalinks. The legacy format is `/cart/{variant_id}:{quantity},{variant_id}:{quantity}`. Optional parameters can prefill email and address and apply `discount=` codes, and `continue_to` can send the buyer to the cart instead of checkout. Each link replaces the buyer's current cart, links longer than 2,048 bytes go to the home page, and selling plans aren't supported. A newer "Cart and Checkout Permalinks" format (Universal Commerce Protocol, `dev.ucp.shopping.permalink`) replaces the legacy one, but line-item properties and notes aren't supported in it yet. — [shopify.dev cart permalinks](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/create-cart-permalinks.md); [shopify.dev agents permalinks](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout/permalinks)
- An example of a quilt shop on a non-Shopify platform: Modern Domestic PDX's online shop uses a Lightspeed/ecwid-style URL scheme (`/collection/page25.html`). This is an inference from the URL, not confirmed. — [shop.moderndomesticpdx.com](https://shop.moderndomesticpdx.com/fabric/page21.html)
- Stash Fabrics' `feed?Page=` URLs and eQuilter's `/category/<id>/` URLs don't look like Shopify (inference from URL patterns only). — [stashfabrics.com](https://www.stashfabrics.com/feed?Page=1); [equilter.com](https://www.equilter.com/category/241/solid-cottons-muslin-+-pfd)

### Inferences
- Fabric sold by the yard in fractions is what makes prefilling a cart hard. On Shopify with a fractional-yard app, the variant is often a "1/4 yard" unit and the quantity is a count of quarter yards, so a link would be `/cart/<quarter-yard variant>:<number of quarters>`. How each app models this would need checking app by app.
- For Like Sew and other non-Shopify shops, no public prefilled-cart link format was found. Sending a printable or emailed fabric list may be the only option there.

### Gaps
- No BuiltWith-style count of how many US quilt shops use Shopify, Like Sew, BigCommerce, Wix or Square. BuiltWith/Grips pages weren't reachable.
- The platforms of the big named retailers (Missouri Star, Fat Quarter Shop, Shabby Fabrics, Connecting Threads) couldn't be confirmed.
- Whether Like Sew has a cart-link or API for outside apps: not found.

## 3. Affiliate programs: rates, cookies, networks

### Takeaway
Quilting retailers' affiliate rates cluster at 4–10%, with short to medium cookies (7–45 days). Awin is the main network in this niche (Fat Quarter Shop, Connecting Threads, Etsy), then CJ (Spoonflower), FlexOffers (Keepsake Quilting) and in-house programs (Linda's). Missouri Star has an invitation-style affiliate program with no published terms.

### Cited Findings
- **Fat Quarter Shop** (Awin, merchant 89535): 6% on qualifying purchases, 7-day cookie. No commission on returned, cancelled, fraudulent or self-referral orders, and no bidding on its brand name in search ads. A directory lists the program as added in March 2024. No official FQS page states the rate. — [Awin merchant profile 89535](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/89535); [Affilitizer](https://www.affilitizer.com/programs/www.fatquartershop.com)
- **Connecting Threads** (Awin): 15-day attribution period per the Awin profile. A search summary also said it "pays 10% commission on every sale you drive", but that came from an aggregated result and isn't confirmed. — [Awin merchant profile 94593](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/94593); [Affilitizer](https://www.affilitizer.com/programs/www.connectingthreads.com)
- **Missouri Star Quilt Co.**: a December 2022 blog post invites quilters who share their projects on social media to apply to be an "official Missouri Star affiliate". No rate, cookie or network found. — [Missouri Star blog 2022-12-08](https://missouriquiltco.com/blogs/missouri-star-blog/share-your-love-for-missouri-star)
- **Keepsake Quilting** (FlexOffers): 45-day cookie. — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/keepsake-quilting-affiliate-program/)
- **Etsy** (Awin for North America; also on FlexOffers): usually reported as 4% for US publishers and 2% for social/UGC creators, with a 30-day cookie on the website and 7 days in the app. Etsy's own help page says rates vary by category and show in the dashboard. — [Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360000335987-The-Affiliate-Programme); [Lasso](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/etsy/); [Geniuslink](https://geniuslink.com/blog/etsy-affiliate-program/)
- **Spoonflower** (CJ Affiliate): most directories say 5%, Shopper.com says 5–10%, LinkClicky says 1%. Cookie 30 days (FlexOffers) or 60 days (Post Affiliate Pro). The sources conflict. — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/spoonflower-affiliate-program/); [Post Affiliate Pro](https://www.postaffiliatepro.com/affiliate-program-directory/spoonflower-affiliate-program/); [LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/spoonflower/)
- **Amazon Associates**: the US Arts, Crafts & Sewing rate couldn't be confirmed. Many physical-product categories were 1–4% in early 2025, and Amazon cut some US rates by up to 50% without announcement from about 2026-03-09 (Adweek). The 15% often seen for this category is the *seller* referral fee, not the affiliate rate. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/amazon-associates-affiliate-rate-cuts-publishers/); [Novadata, May 2026](https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-associates-commission-cut-may-2026); [runfutureproof (seller fee)](https://www.runfutureproof.com/amazon-fees/arts-crafts)
- Other niche comparisons: Stampington (craft publisher, AvantLink) 6% base and 45-day cookie; Linda's (quilting brand, in-house) up to 10%; American Heritage Textiles (LinkConnector) up to 8%; Superior Threads 10% (search summary); Mood Fabrics 5% with a 30-day cookie (search summary); The Fabric Hut "up to 30%" (search summary, an outlier). — [Stampington](https://stampington.com/Become-a-Stampington-Affiliate); [Linda's](https://lindas.com/pages/affiliate-program-overview); [LinkConnector](https://www.linkconnector.com/affiliate-campaign-description/?cid=7512)
- **Shabby Fabrics, eQuilter, Hawthorne Supply Co, Stash Fabrics, Pink Castle, Lark Cottons, Walmart, Hobby Lobby**: no affiliate program found in search. Walmart and Hobby Lobby weren't specifically searched.

### Inferences
- The going rate a fabric shop pays an affiliate in this niche is about 5–10% of the sale. 6% with a 7-day cookie (Fat Quarter Shop) is a realistic benchmark for a big shop. A design app asking more than about 10% would be above the norm.
- Short cookies (7 days at FQS) don't suit a quilt designer, where people often plan for weeks before buying. A prefilled cart link (bought now) helps here.

### Gaps
- Connecting Threads' rate (10%?) couldn't be confirmed, because the Awin page wasn't reachable.
- Missouri Star's terms aren't public.
- Amazon's current US Arts, Crafts & Sewing rate is unconfirmed after the 2026 cuts.
- Walmart (Impact) and Hobby Lobby weren't researched. Hobby Lobby isn't known to have an affiliate program (unverified).

## 4. Quilt shop gross margin on fabric, and shops paying apps a share

### Takeaway
Quilt shops usually price fabric at "keystone" (twice wholesale), which gives about a 50% gross margin before discounts, sales and shipping. Store-level gross margin is reported at around 40–50%. A 5–10% referral share is therefore 10–20% of the shop's gross profit on the fabric. No examples were found of shops paying a quilt-design app a share of sales.

### Cited Findings
- Keystone (doubling the wholesale cost) is the standard retail markup, and many quilt shops keystone fabric and add a little for shipping (forum anecdote about Dallas-area shops). — [QuiltingBoard forum](https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/fabric-prices-t98852.html); [Chron](https://smallbusiness.chron.com/determine-retail-markup-yarn-store-78867.html)
- A small-business guide puts craft and fabric store gross margin at about 40–50%, with annual revenue of $200K–$800K. No data is cited. — [ZenBusiness](https://www.zenbusiness.com/start-a-business/retail/craft-and-fabric-supply/)
- A financial-model vendor assumes 35% margin on fabric against 75% on workshops. This is unverified. — [financialmodelslab](https://financialmodelslab.com/products/quilt-shop-kpi-metrics)
- FabShopNet publishes "Benchmarks for Quilt Shops", but it couldn't be opened. — [FabShopNet](https://fabshopnet.com/?p=1310)
- Retailer pages show some shops pay a share to professional quilters (My Favorite Quilt Store's "Pro Quilters" program), but its terms weren't seen. — [myfavoritequiltstore.com](https://myfavoritequiltstore.com/pages/pro-quilters)
- No PreQuilt (the closest competing quilt-design app) affiliate or shop-partner program was found in search. — (no source; searched 2026-11-07)

### Inferences
- With roughly 45–50% gross margin before costs, shops can usually afford 5–10%. That is what the large online shops already pay through Awin. A small independent shop with lower volume may push back on more than about 5%.

### Gaps
- No authoritative (CIA or FabShopNet) margin figure was read directly.
- No documented case of a quilt shop paying a design app or software platform a share of sales.

## 5. Shop-side selling tools that already exist

### Takeaway
Shops buy wholesale from distributors and fabric companies' reps, and increasingly on Faire. Online they sell through their own stores plus marketplaces (Etsy, eBay). No niche-wide product feed was found that an app could plug into across many shops.

### Cited Findings
- Faire carries quilting brands selling wholesale to shops, for example P&B Textiles (fabric) and Traditional Primitives (patterns), with 60-day payment terms and free returns on opening orders. Wholesale prices are shown only to signed-up retailers. — [Faire brand pages](https://www.faire.com/brand/b_bf85usf7k4)
- Fractional-yard selling on Shopify is handled by apps such as Sew Much Commerce, whose vendor also runs SEO and Google/Facebook ads for shops. — [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/fabric-shop)
- Like Sew bundles POS, inventory, online store and marketing for quilt shops. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/compare/83891-10021014/Shopify-vs-Like-Sew)

### Inferences
- Shopify stores expose public `/products.json` feeds and can push to the Google Merchant Center. That could be the practical "product feed" for an app, but it wasn't researched here.

### Gaps
- Distributor tools (e.g. Brewer Quilting & Sewing Supplies' retailer ordering) and fabric-company product data feeds (Moda, FreeSpirit, Riley Blake) weren't found.
- Google Shopping feeds from quilt shops weren't researched.
