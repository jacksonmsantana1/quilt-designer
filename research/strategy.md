# Strategy: how to win when software costs almost nothing

Built on the research in `research/reports/` (competitors, fabrics and carts, teachers, creators) and the
111-place list in `research/ateliers/`. Numbers marked *estimate* are my own working, not sourced.

## 1. The problem, stated plainly

If anyone can build a quilt designer in weeks, features stop being an advantage. The 3D drape, Portuguese
screens, block drawing and 1,000 blocks can all be copied: NiftyFifty already gives 1,200–1,400 blocks away,
Quiltler ships fast, and AI makes copying cheaper every month. So the question is not "what can the app do?"
but **"what does the app have that a copy wouldn't?"**

Four things can't be copied with code, because they take time, trust and contracts:

1. **Who uses it together**: teachers, their students, creators and shops working in the same place.
2. **Data nobody else has**: Brazilian fabrics with real photos, live prices and stock, linked to shops.
3. **Physical commerce**: the step from "design" to "fabric delivered", run with partner shops.
4. **Brand and community**: being *the* patchwork app of Brazil, at every festival and in every class.

The plan is to spend the cheap resource (software) to build the expensive one (relationships, data, commerce).
A rule of thumb for your own time: **about 70% on people and partners, 30% on code.** The code part is now
the easy part.

## 2. Change what the app *is*

Today it is described as "a quilt design program". Programs are commodities. Position it instead as
**the path from idea to finished quilt for Brazilian patchwork**:

> Learn in class → design (or open your teacher's pattern) → see it on your bed → buy exactly the fabric
> needed at a nearby shop → cut with the plan → show the finished quilt.

Every step a competitor doesn't cover is a step where the person stays with you. Design alone is one
step; the whole path is six.

## 3. The four moats, and how to build each one

### Moat 1 — The teacher network (distribution + people don't leave)

- Teachers use it free, for good, and get a **class mode**: send a block or pattern to all students, see
  their versions, print handouts with the class logo.
- A **QR code on every printed pattern and handout** opens the design in the app. Paper patterns are how
  patchwork is taught in Brazil; this makes every handout an invitation.
- **"Professora parceira" title**, listed in the app's teacher map (students find a nearby class). Teachers
  value status and students more than a few reais.
- The first 50 teachers get **"fundadora" terms** (a higher share for life). Early partners who are treated
  better are the hardest for a rival to take away.

### Moat 2 — The Brazilian fabric catalogue (data nobody else has)

The research found that big brands sell only through shops, and no competitor shows a single Brazilian
brand. The practical way to get "all brands" is **through partner shops, not through mills**:

- Each partner shop on Shopify, VTEX or Nuvemshop shares its **product feed**: photo, brand, price, stock.
  The catalogue becomes the union of the partner shops, automatically up to date, and the cart works by
  construction because every fabric *is* a product in a real shop.
- Shops already photograph each fabric for their own site, so they can give permission. Have a lawyer
  confirm the use of printed designs (Lei 9.610/98) before launch.
- Add mills directly where they agree (Fernando Maluhy, Karsten Ateliê, Peripan, Fabricart). What you can
  offer them that no one else can: **demand data** (which of their fabrics are being designed with, by
  region, before anyone buys). That is the currency for image rights.
- Shops **without** an online store (most ateliers on the list) get a **WhatsApp order**: the app writes
  the shopping list with quantities and sends it to the shop's WhatsApp. This covers the long tail of
  Brazilian patchwork commerce, which runs on WhatsApp, and no foreign competitor will think of it.
- The person's own fabrics (photo or webcam) form a **stash**. Their stash lives in your app: another
  reason not to leave.

### Moat 3 — Commerce: design to cart (and later to kit)

This is the moat with the most money in it, and it changes the teacher deal.

- A double-bed quilt needs roughly 10 m of fabric including backing and binding (*estimate*). At the
  prices found (about R$24–78 a metre depending on brand), that is **about R$250–780 per quilt**
  (*estimate*).
- A referral fee of 8% from partner shops would be about **R$20–60 per quilt** (*estimate*). A student
  who makes two quilts and a few small pieces a year is worth more in fabric than in subscription.
- **Many teachers own shops** (about half of the 111 places on the list sell fabric). For them the best
  offer is not "R$6 a month per student", it is **"your students' fabric orders go to your shop"**. That is
  an offer no software company abroad can match.
- Later: **pre-cut kits**. The app already produces the cutting plan; partner shops cut and ship a kit.
  Running that with real shops is operations, not code, and it is very hard to copy.

### Moat 4 — Community and content (network effects)

- **"Fazer este quilt"**: every finished design in the gallery has a button that loads it, fits it to your
  bed, and fills the cart. More users → more designs → more reasons to join.
- A **pattern shop** inside the app for Brazilian designers and teacher-creators (Ana Cosentino, Natália
  Engel, Tia Lili): they sell patterns and course packs, the app takes a share. Sellers bring buyers, and
  buyers attract more sellers.
- **Official app of the festivals**: Floripa Quilt, Brazil Patchwork Show, Limeira. Contest entries and
  public voting through the app, a "plan your visit" map of exhibitors. Festivals are where the community
  meets; be part of how they run.
- **Senac and schools**: get the app into the 160-hour patchwork course curriculum. A curriculum lasts years.

## 4. Pricing: you can't be "the most used" behind a paywall

The research's top threat to a paid app is free tools and graph paper. NiftyFifty is free. If every student
must pay before designing, the app will never be the most used. Recommendation:

| Who | What they pay | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone | Free | Design, the block library, cutting plan, shopping list, a few saved projects |
| Students / quilters | Plus, about R$19.90/month or R$149/year | Unlimited projects, 3D on your own bed size, stash, sync, HD images, all patterns from their teacher |
| Teachers | Free for good | Class mode, handouts, map listing, share of Plus and of fabric orders |
| Shops | Free to join; referral fee on orders | Their catalogue in the app, orders by cart link or WhatsApp |
| Mills | Later: paid launches of new collections | Demand data, featured collections |

The app stays "paid" for people who want more, the teacher still earns, and the free tier is what makes it
the most used. Money comes from three sides (Plus, fabric orders, patterns) instead of one.

## 5. The path, phase by phase

### Phase 0 — Prove it in São Paulo (now → month 3)

- Finish the basics: real login, saving, the **"+ Add fabric"** button, Pix payments (Asaas).
- **5 ateliers that also sell fabric** from the list (São Paulo has 41 places). One shop's catalogue fully
  connected (cart link), the others by WhatsApp order.
- Sit in on classes. Watch students over 60 use it. Fix whatever confuses them the same week.
- **Pass/fail test**: do students open it *between* classes, and does at least one order reach a shop
  each week? If not, change the product before growing.

### Phase 1 — Become the classroom standard in Brazil (months 3–12)

- 100 ateliers, region by region (São Paulo, then Paraná and Santa Catarina, where the list is strongest).
- 3–5 teacher-creators with course packs inside the app (start with those who own shops).
- 10+ shops with live catalogues; WhatsApp orders for the rest. First mill agreement.
- Stand at Floripa Quilt and Brazil Patchwork Show (April). Run the beginners' contest through the app.
- **Target**: the app is used in 1 of every 3 patchwork classes you can reach.

### Phase 2 — Turn on the flywheel (months 12–24)

- Gallery with "Fazer este quilt", pattern shop, teacher map.
- Pre-cut kits with 2–3 partner shops.
- Festival partnerships, Senac curriculum.
- Creators on the 20%/12-month terms for reach beyond the ateliers.
- **Target**: most new users arrive through other users (shared designs, classes, gallery), not through
  you.

### Phase 3 — Repeat the model abroad (from month 18)

- The model travels: in each country, local teachers plus local shops plus local creators.
- Spanish-speaking Latin America first, through creators already found (Mininos de Sánchez, Retales de Mil
  Colores, Momovipro) and Mexico's shops and Expo Quilt México.
- Then metric countries (Portugal, Spain, Europe, Australia) with "see it on your bed, cut in cm".
- Being the most used **worldwide** is a long game against EQ8, Missouri Star and others. Being the most
  used **in Portuguese and Spanish** is reachable in 2–3 years, because nobody is there yet.

## 6. What to measure

One main number: **quilts that went from design to a fabric order this month.** It only grows if people
use the app, teachers bring them, the catalogue is good and shops deliver. All four moats feed it.

Supporting numbers: active ateliers; weekly active students; share of students still active 3 months
after their course; orders per partner shop; designs opened from someone else's share or QR code.

## 7. What not to do

- Don't compete on block count or features. Reach 1,000 blocks because it's cheap, then stop counting.
- Don't go wide too early. 100 ateliers that love it beat 1,000 sign-ups that leave.
- Don't build cart integrations before a shop has agreed to take the orders.
- Don't sign exclusive deals you can't honour. "Fundadora" terms win loyalty without exclusivity clauses.
- Don't depend on one creator, one shop platform or one mill.

## 8. Shops that only sell fabric

They don't teach, so the teacher deal doesn't fit them. But they are the other half of the model: teachers
bring people, shops supply the fabric. Of the 111 places on the list, 20 sell fabric without teaching (13 of
them with a website), and the list left out general fabric shops on purpose, so the real number is much
larger.

### What a fabric shop wants, and what the app gives it

| The shop's problem | What the app gives it |
|---|---|
| Customers ask "how much do I buy?" and staff work it out by hand | **Counter mode**: a tablet or phone at the counter; the customer picks a design, the app gives exact metres per fabric, plus batting, thread and binding. Fewer mistakes, bigger and more complete sales |
| Customers can't picture the fabrics together | **QR code on each bolt**: scan it and the fabric goes straight into your quilt, shown in 3D on a bed, before anything is cut |
| Slow stock sits on the shelf | **Shop kits**: the shop designs a quilt with fabrics it has in stock and publishes it; nearby users see "Kit da semana" and buy it in one tap |
| Competing with Mercado Livre, Magalu and big online shops | Orders arrive from quilters who already know exactly what they need, close by, ready to pay |
| Not knowing what to stock | Anonymous data on what local quilters design with: colours, brands, styles |
| Teachers send students elsewhere | **"Loja parceira da turma"**: the app matches the shop with teachers who don't own a shop; the shop offers a discount to that class |

### Joining in ten minutes, whatever the shop's technology

Most of these shops are small, so the app has to meet each one where it is:

1. **WhatsApp only**: the shop gives its WhatsApp number and photographs its fabrics with the phone (the
   same photo feature as "+ Add fabric", with a printed card in the picture for true size and colour).
   Price and stock can be "consultar". Orders arrive as a WhatsApp message with the list.
2. **A spreadsheet**: one file with name, brand, price per metre, unit (50 cm, metre, fat quarter), photo.
3. **An online store**: connect Shopify, VTEX, Nuvemshop, Tray or Loja Integrada; catalogue, price and stock
   update themselves and the app fills the cart.
4. **Later**: stock kept in sync, kits, and a cutting service (pre-cut kits).

A shop can start at step 1 and move up when it wants. No shop should need a developer.

### Who gets the order

This is the question shops will ask first. Write the rule down and show it in the app:

- **The quilter always chooses the shop**; the app never hides other shops.
- Default, in order: the shop that brought her to the app, then her teacher's shop or her class's partner
  shop, then the nearest shop with the fabric in stock.
- A shop that brings a customer to the app (QR at the counter, on the bag, on the receipt) becomes her
  **home shop**: her orders go there by default for as long as she keeps that shop.
- Show prices, but **don't build a "cheapest first" ranking**. Sort by: your shop, nearby, in stock. Shops
  won't join an app that turns them into a price war.
- When no partner shop has a fabric, fall back to an affiliate link (Mercado Livre, Magalu, Amazon).

### How shops pay

You can't see whether a WhatsApp conversation became a sale, so a commission only works for online carts:

| Shop type | How it pays (*estimates, test in the pilot*) |
|---|---|
| Online store (cart links) | A fee on orders that come from the app, tracked by link or code: about 5–8% |
| WhatsApp only | A small monthly plan (about R$49–99) for the listing, kits and orders, or a fee per order the shop confirms in the app |
| Everyone, at first | **Free during the pilot.** A city needs enough shops that every design finds its fabric before anyone is charged |

When the teacher recommended the shop, pass part of the shop's fee to the teacher. That way teachers without
a shop also earn from fabric, not only from Plus.

### Where it fits in the plan

- **Phase 0 (São Paulo)**: next to the 5 ateliers, add 2–3 fabric-only shops near them, so students of
  teachers without a shop have somewhere to buy. Candidates from the list: Ponto X Tecidos (São Paulo),
  Casa Criativa and Cris Mazzer (Campinas), Patchwork Sorocaba, Costura Tudo (Ribeirão Preto).
- **Phase 1**: in each new region, sign up shops and teachers together, never one without the other.
- **Phase 2**: shop kits, QR codes on bolts and counter mode become the reasons shops ask to join.

## 9. If a big rival copies you

Assume someone copies the app within a year. They get the software. They don't get: the teachers who
already teach with it, the shops already connected, the stash and projects people already keep there, the
festival seat, or the Brazilian fabric data. That is the point of building those first, while the app is
still small and nobody is watching.
